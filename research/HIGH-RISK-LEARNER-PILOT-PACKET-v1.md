# Orange Plan Academy — high-risk learner pilot packet v1

**Status:** ready to run; no learner results collected yet  
**Purpose:** find misunderstandings that would make Austin re-record after filming  
**Participants:** 3–5 people who resemble the intended learner; at least one early retiree, one Bitcoin-heavy household, and one person who is not a financial professional

## Rules for the facilitator

- Use the current concept script or rough read, not the final polished video.
- Do not explain an answer until the learner has committed to one.
- Ask the learner to point to the number or sentence supporting the answer.
- Record the learner's exact wording when confused.
- A professional disclaimer does not count as comprehension.
- Stop immediately if a custody test would expose a real secret or move meaningful Bitcoin.

## Pass standard

A lesson passes when at least 80% of participants can:

1. state why they entered the lesson,
2. explain the worked example,
3. identify the decision,
4. trace the important number or status to its source,
5. distinguish app output from external proof,
6. and name where the decision returns to the Plan.

Any misunderstanding that could cause a wrong withdrawal, tax action, loan decision, custody move, or estate instruction is blocking even when only one learner makes it.

# Test 1 — confidence and earliest retirement date

**Lessons:** Core 1.3

Ask:

1. What does 94.6% mean in this plan?
2. Why is age 51 shown when the household plans to retire at 55?
3. What changes when the confidence target moves from 80%?
4. Does a higher confidence number automatically mean the plan is better?
5. Which number is the learner choosing and which number is the app calculating?

Blocking misunderstandings:

- believing deterministic and Monte Carlo dates still exist separately,
- believing age 51 is a recommendation to retire,
- treating 80% as a universal safety law,
- or believing confidence predicts one future.

# Test 2 — where the Cash Flow number came from

**Lessons:** Core 2.1 and 4.3

Show:

- $4,261 capacity before extra debt,
- $500 extra auto principal,
- $3,761 displayed after planned Debt,
- $3,500 contribution route,
- $261 operating cushion.

Ask:

1. Why does Cash Flow show $3,761 when the household puts $4,000 to work?
2. Where is the $500?
3. What amount is actually available for account contributions?
4. What would be double-counted if the learner routed another full $4,000 from the screen?

Blocking misunderstanding:

- treating $3,761 and $4,000 as conflicting calculations,
- or routing the $500 debt decision twice.

# Test 3 — sell versus borrow

**Lessons:** A2.1–A2.3

Show the fictional $30,000 need:

- sale: 0.30 BTC, $10,000 basis, $20,000 gain before current tax conclusion,
- borrow: $30,000 against 1.50 BTC at 20% starting LTV,
- illustrative 65% action and 80% liquidation lines.

Ask:

1. What changes on day one under each option?
2. Does the loan increase net worth?
3. What is provisional about the tax comparison?
4. Why is retained Bitcoin not the same as unencumbered self-custody?
5. Which future assumption is carrying the apparent advantage?
6. What would make the household reject borrowing even when ending net worth is higher in one modeled path?

Blocking misunderstanding:

- “loan proceeds are tax-free” stated without agreement review,
- calling an illustrative LTV safe,
- counting pledged Bitcoin as ordinary available custody,
- or assuming refinance or price appreciation is the repayment plan.

# Test 4 — Roth conversion range

**Lessons:** Core 5.2 and A3.2

Show:

- $40,000 expected ordinary income,
- $80,000 planning ceiling,
- $40,000 first-pass room,
- no/smaller/ceiling comparison.

Ask:

1. Why is $40,000 not automatically the recommended conversion?
2. What exact facts must be known first?
3. Where does the conversion tax come from?
4. What is the difference between Preview, saved strategy, and executed conversion?
5. What result belongs to the CPA?

Blocking misunderstanding:

- automatically filling the bracket,
- treating the full Roth balance as contribution basis,
- treating a saved strategy as completed,
- or ignoring healthcare and Bridge effects.

# Test 5 — early account access

**Lessons:** A4.1

Show the $101,948 first-year draw and a source map using taxable Bridge, possible employer-plan access, supported Roth contribution basis, and HSA healthcare reimbursement.

Ask:

1. Which source is available without special proof?
2. Which source requires the employer plan document?
3. Why is penalty-free not the same as tax-free?
4. Why is the full Roth balance not the early Bridge?
5. What does the saved voluntary blend prove—and what does it not prove?
6. How are required RMDs treated?

Blocking misunderstanding:

- applying Alex's age-55 facts to another account or person,
- rolling the plan before checking access,
- using unsupported Roth/HSA amounts,
- or assuming the blend creates legal access.

# Test 6 — healthcare and income

**Lessons:** A4.2–A4.3

Show the illustrative quote:

- $12,000 net premium,
- $6,000 expected out-of-pocket,
- $18,000 all-in first-year cost,
- conversion raises quote by $5,000.

Ask:

1. Why is $12,000 not the Plan input?
2. Which figures must come from a current quote?
3. How can a conversion change healthcare cost?
4. What can the HSA fund?
5. Why is CrowdHealth experience not proof that health sharing equals insurance?
6. Why do three strategies providing the same $100,000 cash produce different outcomes?

Blocking misunderstanding:

- using a national average as the quote,
- treating premium as full cost,
- treating all HSA spending as qualified,
- or treating illustrative Marketplace amounts as current promises.

# Test 7 — passphrase and multisig

**Lessons:** A5.1–A5.3

Ask:

1. What specific problem would a passphrase solve?
2. What new permanent-loss path does it create?
3. Why does Alex's ability to build DIY multisig not prove the family should use it?
4. What must collaborative custody prove if the provider disappears?
5. What is the safe migration sequence for 1.50 BTC?
6. What information must never enter Orange Plan or the pilot record?

Blocking misunderstanding:

- passphrase as a default “higher level,”
- memory as the only passphrase backup,
- 2-of-3 described with four keys,
- one coordinator balance treated as recovery proof,
- or moving the full balance in the first test.

# Test 8 — trust ownership and Bitcoin access

**Lessons:** A6.1–A6.2

Show the mismatch:

- trust says it owns 1.50 BTC,
- Jordan is successor trustee,
- Bitcoin remains in Alex's individual wallet,
- only Alex can recover it.

Ask:

1. Does owning Bitcoin automatically mean the family needs a trust?
2. What problem must the trust solve?
3. Why does the signed document not finish the transfer?
4. What is the difference between key possession and legal authority?
5. Which four records must agree before the asset is complete?
6. Should the seed phrase be placed in the trust document?

Blocking misunderstanding:

- “buy a trust” as the default result,
- assuming a signed trust moved the asset,
- confusing trustee authority with a signing path,
- or putting Bitcoin secrets into the legal or app record.

# Final capstone prompt

Ask the learner to explain the plan in six sentences:

1. When can the household retire and what confidence target are they using?
2. What is the current monthly route?
3. What is the Bitcoin allocation and drawdown exposure?
4. How is the first retirement year funded?
5. Which Advanced gate is actually active?
6. What is the next action and who owns the external proof?

The capstone fails when the learner can repeat definitions but cannot explain the household decision.
