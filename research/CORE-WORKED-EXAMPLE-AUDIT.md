# Orange Plan Academy — Core worked-example audit

**Status:** complete for the pre-dictation Core  
**Scope:** 28 spoken Core lessons; versioned walkthroughs excluded  
**Standard:** the concept lesson must teach the example before the screen-share implements it

## The standard

Mentioning Alex and Jordan is not enough.

A concept example passes when it shows enough of this chain for the learner to understand the decision:

1. **Situation** — what is true for the household?
2. **Concrete input** — what numbers, facts, or process state matter?
3. **Decision** — what choice is being made?
4. **Reasoning** — why does that choice fit?
5. **Trade-off** — what improves, worsens, or becomes more fragile?
6. **Outcome** — what changes in the plan or real-world process?
7. **Transfer** — what analogous question should the learner answer?

The example can be numeric, comparative, or procedural. It does not have to use arithmetic when the concept is custody, legal authority, or maintenance.

The walkthrough is not the first example. It is the current app implementation of a concept the learner has already seen worked.

## What was preserved from the live decks

The repaired scripts retain the strongest teaching devices from the live material:

- reserve formula and stress decision,
- drawdown × allocation,
- tax-lot math,
- tax timeline,
- year-by-year retirement cash flow,
- debt-ratio comparison,
- custody failure and recovery,
- and estate access/communication scenarios.

Incorrect or retired examples were not restored merely because they were concrete. They were replaced when they taught the old two-date retirement model, universal RMD age, prescriptive debt ladder, “normal” 50% Bitcoin-loan LTV, unsafe device-wipe instruction, or an incorrect four-key diagram labelled 2-of-3.

## Review rule for Austin

Austin is reviewing the **example and the judgment**, not only the prose.

For each lesson, ask:

- Is this the example I would actually use with a client?
- Does the example make the concept simpler rather than adding numbers for their own sake?
- Is the recommendation mine?
- Is the trade-off honest?
- Can the learner use the same reasoning in their own plan?

When the answer is no, replace the example before marking the lesson approved. Do not defer the first real explanation to the walkthrough.
