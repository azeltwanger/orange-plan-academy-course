## Ownership rules

1. One difficult concept has one full home.
2. A later lesson may recall it in one or two sentences but does not reteach it.
3. Every lesson ends in one main decision.
4. Spoken video owns reasoning and judgment.
5. Lesson text owns changing facts, detailed checklists, and professional qualifications.
6. Visuals own comparisons that do not need every row read aloud.
7. Walkthroughs own temporary app controls and click paths.
8. Advanced owns triggered edge cases.

## Core-to-Advanced boundary

The core names the gate and the risk but does not require:

- Monte Carlo sampler mechanics,
- detailed custom return periods,
- Bitcoin-backed loan capacity, lender terms, and liquidation modeling,
- conversion sizing, harvesting, and relocation execution,
- current-year contribution-limit tables,
- self-directed retirement-account Bitcoin implementation,
- Rule of 55, 72(t), Roth-basis, and other access execution,
- passphrase design,
- collaborative or DIY multisig setup,
- UTXO management,
- trust selection, estate tax, special-needs, or business succession,
- or long-term-care product comparison.

Those topics remain discoverable through a condition visible in the learner's plan.

## Remaining runtime changes

Only three kinds of edits should change runtime from here:

1. Austin replaces wording with a more natural explanation.
2. A professional review adds or removes a material qualification.
3. A demo checkpoint shows that an example needs a different explanation.

Do not add another global completeness pass. New detail must either replace less useful text, live in lesson text, or move to Advanced.
