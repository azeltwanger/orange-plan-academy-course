## Ownership rules

1. One difficult concept has one full home.
2. A later lesson may recall it briefly but does not reteach it.
3. Every lesson ends in one main decision.
4. Spoken video owns reasoning, trade-offs, examples, and Austin's judgment.
5. Lesson text owns changing facts, detailed checklists, and professional qualifications.
6. Visuals own comparisons that should be understood without every row being read aloud.
7. Walkthroughs own temporary routes, controls, save behavior, and clicks.
8. Advanced owns triggered implementation complexity and edge cases.
9. App-owned outputs are explained in the lesson only when they change the decision or prevent a material misunderstanding.

## Core-to-Advanced boundary

Core names the gate and risk but does not require:

- Monte Carlo sampler mechanics,
- detailed custom return periods,
- Bitcoin-backed loan capacity, lender terms, and liquidation modeling,
- conversion sizing, harvesting, and relocation execution,
- current-year contribution-limit tables,
- self-directed retirement-account Bitcoin implementation,
- Rule of 55, 72(t), Roth-basis, and other account-access execution,
- passphrase design,
- collaborative or DIY multisig implementation,
- UTXO management,
- trust selection, estate tax, special-needs, or business succession,
- or long-term-care product comparison.

Those topics remain discoverable through a condition visible in the learner's plan.

## Runtime acceptance test

A section stays spoken when it does at least one of the following:

- creates the mental model,
- changes the decision,
- explains an important app number,
- prevents a consequential mistake,
- or gives Austin's planning judgment in a way the learner needs to hear.

A section moves to lesson text, a visual, walkthrough, or Advanced when it is primarily:

- a changing table,
- a provider-specific procedure,
- a professional implementation rule,
- a click path,
- or repeated context from an earlier lesson.

## Remaining runtime changes

Only four kinds of edits should materially change runtime now:

1. Austin replaces wording with a more natural explanation.
2. A professional review adds, removes, or relocates a material qualification.
3. The deployed UI shows that a candidate result needs a different explanation.
4. A learner pilot identifies a repeated misunderstanding that the current layer does not solve.

Do not add another global completeness pass. New detail must replace less useful text, live in the appropriate reference layer, or move to Advanced.
