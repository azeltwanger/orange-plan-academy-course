## Per-lesson score

Score each category 0–2.

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate / question | Cannot state why the lesson applies | Roughly identifies the topic | States the exact trigger and learner question |
| Worked example | Cannot reproduce the example | Repeats some numbers without the reasoning | Explains inputs, decision, trade-off, and result |
| Number source | Treats output as unexplained or magic | Finds the page but not the source | Traces the number/status to the input or record |
| Judgment | Misses or reverses the recommendation | Understands the recommendation but not why | Explains the conditional judgment and stop rule |
| App versus proof | Treats app output as proof | Knows outside proof exists but cannot name it | Names the exact provider/professional/evidence owner |
| Decision / finish line | Cannot say what to do | Names a vague next step | States the decision, completion rule, and return to Core |

**Maximum per lesson: 12**

- 10–12: pass
- 8–9: revise for clarity, retest affected question
- 0–7: blocking rewrite before filming

Any safety-critical misunderstanding is blocking regardless of total score.

## Safety-critical flags

Mark immediately:

- [ ] Loan called safe or tax-free without agreement review
- [ ] Loan repayment depends only on higher Bitcoin price
- [ ] Full Roth balance treated as accessible basis
- [ ] Planned conversion treated as executed
- [ ] Marketplace illustration treated as a current quote
- [ ] Unsupported HSA amount treated as qualified
- [ ] Passphrase stored only in memory
- [ ] Full meaningful Bitcoin balance used for first custody test
- [ ] 2-of-3 described with more or fewer than three keys
- [ ] Seed/private key/passphrase placed in app, legal document, or pilot record
- [ ] Signed trust assumed to transfer direct Bitcoin automatically
- [ ] Key possession treated as legal authority

## Correction rule

Use the smallest fix that solves the repeated misunderstanding:

1. Replace an abstract sentence with the worked example.
2. Move the important source explanation earlier.
3. Remove a competing recommendation.
4. Add one contrast: label versus fact, Preview versus saved, planned versus executed, app versus proof.
5. Tighten or split the visual.
6. Change the walkthrough only when the concept was understood but the app route failed.
7. Send a professional question only when the confusion comes from an unresolved high-stakes claim.

Do not add paragraphs merely because one participant wanted more information.

## Course-level pass

The pilot passes when:

- every tested lesson averages at least 10/12,
- no participant has an unresolved safety-critical flag,
- at least 80% can complete the final six-sentence capstone,
- at least 80% can trace the four highest-risk numbers to their source,
- and no repeated misunderstanding remains about app output versus external proof.
