# Orange Plan Academy — guided plan-building architecture

**Status:** working architecture for the source-rich course recut. Canonical lesson scripts remain unchanged until the course map and owner decisions below are accepted.

## The governing promise

Orange Plan calculates the plan. Academy tells the member:

1. what to build next;
2. what decision belongs in that step;
3. how to make the decision;
4. where it is entered or saved;
5. what changed after the step;
6. when that part of the plan is finished.

The course should feel like the human teaching layer for **Plan → Build & improve**: one next task, one primary decision, enough depth to make it well, and a clear finished checkpoint.

## Source and authority order

1. Austin's newest explicit decisions and dictation.
2. Accepted Orange Plan redesign contracts and final approved on-screen behavior.
3. The Academy landing-page promises.
4. Austin's slide decks for decision flow, diagrams, examples, and teaching order.
5. Austin's coaching calls for real questions, natural explanations, and useful examples.
6. Current professional or primary-source verification for tax, legal, insurance, healthcare, custody, and provider claims.
7. The older course master as supplemental detail only.
8. Generated prose only where the stronger sources leave a real teaching gap.

Old navigation, old labels, old confidence presentation, and old product behavior never survive merely because they appear in a deck or master file.

## Course shape

The public experience is **Start Here plus ten guided working sessions**, not a library of disconnected financial-planning lectures. Each working session may contain several short concept videos, one worked example, and one implementation session.

### Phase 1 — Get the numbers true

#### 0. Start Here

**Member leaves with:**

- the course sequence;
- the role of Ask;
- the distinction between Orange Plan and Academy;
- the no-secrets rule;
- the material needed for the first plan.

#### 1. Build the first working plan

**Decision flow:**

1. Understand current facts, expected future changes, assumptions, and scenarios.
2. Add household and retirement basics.
3. Add every account, custody location, and current holding.
4. Add current income, living spending, taxes, required debt payments, and retirement spending.
5. Review the saved assumptions.
6. Run the first Plan result.
7. Read planned timing, earliest modeled timing, chance of success, result freshness, and what remains preliminary.

**Finished when:** the first honest plan exists and the member can explain which missing details may still change it.

### Phase 2 — Decide what every dollar does

#### 2. Build cash flow and the Reserve

**Decision flow:**

1. Establish normal living spending.
2. Establish bare-bones spending.
3. Calculate the monthly surplus.
4. Find useful recurring savings without turning the process into austerity.
5. Choose the Reserve job, target months, and monthly funding pace.
6. Add expected income and spending changes as life events.
7. Separate expected events from hypothetical scenarios.

**Finished when:** the monthly surplus is believable, the Reserve has a target and pace, and expected changes are reflected in the baseline.

#### 3. Give every debt a job

**Decision flow:**

1. Verify balances, rates, required payments, terms, and collateral.
2. Separate debt tolerance from debt fragility.
3. Read debt-to-income and debt-to-assets.
4. Stress the balance sheet after a Bitcoin drawdown.
5. Assign each debt: minimum, extra, refinance, consolidate, pay off, or monitor.
6. Set the extra-debt claim against the monthly surplus.
7. Move detailed Bitcoin-backed borrowing mechanics to Advanced while preserving the core risk comparison.

**Finished when:** every debt has one job, one reason, and an amount reflected in the cash-flow plan.

#### 4. Build the allocation and next-dollar plan

The slide-deck flow controls this working session.

**Decision flow:**

1. Review the current setup: accounts, holdings, contribution destinations, tax treatment, and timeframe jobs.
2. Set the whole-plan target allocation, including the Bitcoin drawdown test.
3. Assign money by timeframe: Reserve / available now, before 59½, and after 59½.
4. Build the contribution waterfall:
   - Reserve at the chosen pace;
   - employer match when available;
   - extra debt from Session 3;
   - remaining dollars by timeframe;
   - account choice;
   - holdings inside the account.
5. Choose the account wrapper: taxable, Traditional, Roth, HSA, education, direct Bitcoin, or another available account.
6. Choose Traditional, Roth, or a mix.
7. Choose the holdings inside each account.
8. Confirm four separate layers:
   - whole-plan asset allocation;
   - timeframe allocation;
   - account allocation and tax location;
   - specific holdings inside each account.
9. Preview the target and create the outside execution list: payroll elections, transfers, account openings, and recurring purchases.

**Finished when:** every new dollar has a route, every receiving account has a job and tax treatment, and every contribution has an intended holding.

#### 5. Build the tax strategy

**Decision flow:**

1. Understand lots and basis with one worked Bitcoin example.
2. Reconcile transfers, duplicates, and missing history.
3. Read taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth buckets.
4. Find the low-income planning window.
5. Compare Roth conversions and intentional Traditional withdrawals before RMDs.
6. Identify gain- and loss-harvesting opportunities.
7. Consider state tax only as part of a real life decision.
8. Build the CPA handoff rather than pretending the app files or executes the strategy.

**Finished when:** basis gaps are visible, the tax window is understood, and the member has one or two modeled questions to take to a CPA.

### Phase 3 — Make retirement executable

#### 6. Build the retirement paycheck

This is the deepest core working session.

**Decision flow:**

1. Set realistic early-retirement spending, including flexible and irregular costs.
2. Build the durable income floor.
3. Calculate the portfolio-funded gap by year.
4. Count and fund the years before 59½.
5. Count and fund the years before Social Security, pensions, and other durable income.
6. Price healthcare before Medicare when applicable.
7. Understand sequence-of-returns risk.
8. Choose account withdrawal order and asset sale order separately.
9. Compare a strict order with a tax-aware annual blend.
10. Establish the Reserve draw-and-refill rule.
11. Compare selling, borrowing, and holding without treating any one as morally superior.
12. Read chance of success as the headline percentage, with the exact simulation count beneath it.
13. Read Current versus Preview before saving a strategy.
14. Understand the annual portfolio-value guardrail review.

**Finished when:** the member can explain where the retirement paycheck comes from in early years, later years, and a bad market.

### Phase 4 — Keep the plan alive

#### 7. Choose and prove the custody plan

**Decision flow:**

1. Name the job of each meaningful Bitcoin pool.
2. Compare direct self-custody, collaborative custody, institutional custody, and an intentional split.
3. State how much direct control matters.
4. Run the One-Failure Test.
5. Harden the accounts and email.
6. Prove any direct-custody recovery before meaningful funds depend on it.
7. Record only the no-secrets process in Protect.

**Finished when:** the custody direction is intentional, the largest current failure is named, and the next protection action has an owner.

#### 8. Build the family handoff

**Decision flow:**

1. Choose and ask the executor and backup.
2. Review will, power of attorney, healthcare directive, and beneficiary forms.
3. Align legal authority with the actual custody method.
4. Write the no-secrets heir letter.
5. Assemble the no-secrets executor packet.
6. Establish and test the communication backstop.
7. Identify insurance gaps the household cannot carry itself.
8. Route trust decisions to Advanced and attorney review unless a clear trust job exists.

**Finished when:** the family knows the first call, the first documents, and the first mistakes to avoid without any secret being stored in the plan.

#### 9. Maintain the plan

**Decision flow:**

1. Run the five-minute monthly pass.
2. Update facts that changed and leave settled strategy alone.
3. Resolve Needs Attention.
4. Choose one to three actions.
5. Run the annual review across Home, Cash Flow, Plan, and Protect.
6. Complete the year-end tax review before the calendar closes.
7. Refresh the recovery proof, family handoff, Your Plan, and encrypted app backup.

**Finished when:** monthly and annual dates are scheduled and the member can complete a quiet month without rebuilding the plan.

#### 10. Read and share Your Plan

**Decision flow:**

1. Position — where the household stands today.
2. Trajectory — retirement timing, chance of success, and year-by-year funding.
3. Risk — assumptions, scenarios, debt, taxes, custody, and family continuity.
4. Actions — the one to three next moves.
5. Explain the plan to a spouse or trusted person.
6. Create the CPA and attorney handoffs.
7. Save the annual PDF and compare it with the prior year.

**Finished when:** the member can explain where the household stands, where it is headed, what could break the plan, and what happens next.

## Standard working-session format

Every session uses the same six-part pattern:

1. **The result:** “By the end, you will have…”
2. **The minimum concept:** only what is needed for the current decision.
3. **One continuing household:** the same fictional plan evolves through the course.
4. **Build it:** enter, review, or preview the decision in Orange Plan.
5. **Read what changed:** explain the result, accuracy change, and Current-versus-Preview status.
6. **Done when:** three to five factual completion checks.

## Production format

- Concept videos are conversational talking-head lessons supported by editor graphics from the decks.
- The course is not slide-led.
- Durable concept videos may name the destination and decision, but exact click paths belong in separate replaceable walkthroughs.
- Walkthroughs are recorded only against the final approved app commit for the owning surface.
- The same fictional household is used through the entire course; short de-identified client examples show how different facts change the answer.
- Core lessons build the plan. Advanced lessons explain specialized methods and implementation risk.

## Proposed core / Advanced boundary

### Core

- first Plan result;
- assumptions and scenarios;
- cash flow and Reserve;
- debt jobs and basic Bitcoin-backed-loan risk;
- whole-plan allocation;
- timeframes and account access;
- contribution waterfall;
- Roth versus Traditional decision;
- holdings inside accounts;
- basis and tax buckets;
- low-income window;
- retirement paycheck;
- healthcare before Medicare for early retirees;
- withdrawal order and annual blend;
- sell-versus-borrow comparison;
- chance of success and guardrails;
- custody choice and recovery proof;
- legal baseline, heir letter, insurance gaps;
- monthly, annual, and Your Plan reviews.

### Advanced

- custom return and volatility modeling;
- detailed Bitcoin-backed-loan thresholds and operating rules;
- 72(t) / SEPP;
- execution-level Roth conversion schedules;
- harvesting execution and current-law mechanics;
- state residency implementation;
- UTXO consolidation and coin-control depth;
- passphrase and multisig implementation;
- trusts and specialized estate structures.

## Owner decisions needed before canonical scripts are recut

Recommended defaults are shown in bold.

1. **Primary member:** **a high-conviction Bitcoin household, roughly 35–55, building toward work-optional life or early retirement.** Other life stages remain supported through branches and examples.
2. **Continuing demo household:** **a fictional married composite with two incomes, two children, a mortgage, employer plan, Roth account, taxable Bitcoin, taxable brokerage, 529, one high-rate debt, and a goal before 59½.** No real client or Austin-family numbers.
3. **Visible structure:** **keep Start Here plus Modules 1–10, but present each module as one guided working session rather than a subject library.** Short lessons remain inside each session for filming and editing.
4. **Sequence:** **Debt strategy is completed before the final contribution waterfall.** Allocation education may begin earlier, but no contribution plan is called finished until the extra-debt claim is known.
5. **Timeframe language:** **teach Reserve, Bridge, and long-term money in plain English; map them once to the app's current labels: available now, before 59½, and after 59½.** Retire “Forever” as an app label unless Austin wants it as a teaching phrase.
6. **Core boundary:** **college funding remains optional; healthcare before Medicare stays core for early retirees; sell-versus-borrow stays core while loan mechanics move to Advanced; trusts remain Advanced.**
7. **Runtime:** **no hard 150-minute cap. Aim for roughly 4–5 hours of core teaching plus separate implementation sessions, with every video earning its place.**
8. **Filming gate:** **Austin may dictate durable concept lessons as they are accepted. Exact app walkthroughs wait for the corresponding redesign surface to be approved and stable.**
