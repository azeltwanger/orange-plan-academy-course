# Apply the teaching in your own Orange Plan

These are the actual planning actions, not homework, a quiz, a submission or another practice household. Keeping a current choice is valid when it fits.

## Build your retirement plan

Begin your own Orange Plan with the retirement date and life you want to compare. Continue with your starting numbers.

## Put your starting numbers in one place

Reconcile accounts, holdings, owners, debts and starting dates once. Keep missing source information visible rather than inventing it.

## Choose assumptions you understand

Choose understandable assumptions and a dollar convention; compare a weaker path while keeping household choices unchanged.

## Read the result and find the next useful change

Read the result, identify the main constraint, and compare one useful change. Use Ask for an explanation when available, then verify the underlying record.

## Decide what you can afford to save

Establish sustainable monthly surplus, separate payroll contributions and employer match, and choose a spending change only when it can really be made.

## Size the Reserve for the job it has to do

- Explain the essential spending basis, chosen months, target, eligible existing funds and gap.
- Compare a funding pace with the same cash flow used for debt and other commitments.
- Explain when dependents or reliance on one income would make faster cash funding worth slower extra-debt repayment.
- Keep required payments funded; carry the proposed reserve amount into the Debt session before finalizing contributions and outside transfers.

## Plan for the changes coming in your life

Put expected life changes on the timeline with dates, amounts, dollar conventions and real funding sources; avoid counting them again in regular spending.

## Choose the college support you intend to fund

For your situation: You intend to help pay for a child’s education.

- State what the parent commitment covers and which resources count for each child.
- Explain the gap and the assumptions behind the saving pace, including when saving starts and when bills arrive.
- Fit the proposed contribution beside existing priorities or name the trade-off still unresolved; communicate and review the commitment.

## Choose which debts to pay down

Choose an affordable action for each actual debt, applying the Reserve decision and future payoff conditions once.

## Decide whether financing is worth it

Compare cash purchase, financing and waiting with the same purchase and resources. Choose a funding route that still works under its actual payment and exit terms.

## Compare unusual financing terms

For your situation: You are comparing an unusual financing agreement or a structure with nonstandard terms.

Explain all payment phases and final settlement under a weaker case before choosing the complex financing proposal.

## Set borrowing limits and a repayment plan

Set household borrowing limits and a repayment source, with earlier action points and a usable response before the lender’s boundary.

## Manage a Bitcoin-backed loan, including repeat borrowing

For your situation: You have or are considering a Bitcoin-backed loan, including repeat borrowing.

Record the actual full balance, contract limits, supporting Bitcoin and funded top-up/repayment response in your debt plan. No separate assignment or submitted sheet.

## Build the whole portfolio around your life

Choose the whole portfolio by timeframe, account access and each asset’s job. Explain what the non-Bitcoin portion supplies and check whether the mix fits the household.

## Choose accounts and compare Traditional with Roth

Choose eligible receiving accounts and compare Traditional and Roth at equal household cost, while preserving early-access funding.

## Choose where new money goes and what it buys

Assign each available dollar once to a receiving account and investment. Compare a new-contribution route with any tax-aware one-time shift; implement only the chosen real contribution.

## Understand a sale and choose the relevant tax action

Separate proceeds, documented gain or loss, tax and usable cash; decide whether a relevant sale or harvest serves the plan. Use the transaction lesson for actual identification and missing records.

## Prepare the records and instructions for a tax-sensitive sale

For your situation: You intend to make a tax-sensitive sale or need to repair the records it depends on.

Prepare an eligible transaction packet with timely identification, replacement review and post-execution reconciliation—or deliberately pass.

## Use tax timing to compare withdrawals and Roth conversions

Compare withdrawal and conversion timing with current and future tax, access and healthcare effects; identify the separate tax-payment source.

## Compare Roth conversions over several years

For your situation: You are comparing Roth conversions across several years.

Select a bounded conversion schedule with after-tax and liquidity justification and a current-year review rule.

## Build the retirement paycheck across the important years

Build the spending and income timeline for each spouse, then show which accessible resources fund each retirement period and the remaining gap.

## Check access to retirement accounts before 59½

For your situation: Your early retirement depends on access to a retirement account before 59½.

Verify an account- and person-specific access route, dates, evidence and invalidating actions before a dependent rollover or distribution.

## Budget healthcare and its tax effects

Choose a coverage path and coordinated income/cost assumptions in the actual plan, with enrollment and HSA timing confirmed.

## Compare selling, borrowing and other funding

Compare selling, borrowing and other funding for the same spending need, including cash received, taxes, debt, collateral and repayment.

## Manage spending and the Reserve through changing markets

Choose a retirement Reserve and refill approach, spending strategy and review rule. Compare the effects of a difficult market without counting the same money twice.

## Choose a custody arrangement you can maintain

Choose custody by control, responsibilities and named failure; record non-secret current status in Orange Plan and use relevant implementation help.

## Compare passphrase, multisig and professional support

For your situation: You are considering a passphrase, multisig or professional custody support.

Choose a custody architecture for a named failure and verify the complete safe recovery path and retained responsibilities.

## Protect access and verify recovery

Safely verify the applicable recovery scope and secure real access routes; record status, never secrets, in the plan.

## Make it possible for someone else to manage the plan

Connect ownership, legal authority, beneficiaries and non-secret custody instructions. Make the starting process findable and identify who completes unfinished legal or provider actions.

## Decide whether a trust has a job in the plan

For your situation: You need to decide whether a trust adds a specific legal or family benefit.

Resolve the trust purpose with qualified review and identify actual funding, beneficiary, custody and successor actions or a simpler baseline.

## Cover the risks your household cannot afford to carry

Identify the household losses it cannot afford to carry; compare existing protection, coverage gaps, cost and the real implementation step.

## Keep the plan current without starting over

Set the review date, update actual records, and apply relevant changes without restarting the plan or reacting to every price move.

## Read your completed plan and finish the next actions

Read the plan’s key choices and limits, resolve the highest-priority unfinished action, and keep the current non-secret household summary available.
