## Spoken teaching

Give the lesson a real opening: where we are in the plan, what we are about to work through, and why that decision matters. A short “In this lesson...” introduction is appropriate when it sounds natural. Do not start every lesson with a command or paste the same opening formula into all lessons.

Talk through the decision in complete connected sentences. Explain what a number means for the household, then work through the example. Use transitions that explain the connection: why the next consideration follows from the last one. Preserve useful conversational repetition when it helps a listener follow; avoid ornamental recaps, hype and long preambles.

Austin's actual reference is source-material/2026-08-25-module-0-1-dictation.md and the preserved historical dictation. He uses ordinary examples, “Let's say...”, and supported personal judgment with a reason: “I think...” and “I would...because...”. Do not mechanically prefix every command with “I would” or “we're going to”. Do not invent a story, experience, preference, financial position or a result. Correct old facts and transcription errors instead of importing them with the voice.

Austin subsequently supplied one client's coaching-call journey as an additional private delivery reference. Six supplied paths contain five unique transcripts; the files labeled Call 3 and Call 4 are identical. Use how he introduces a decision, responds to confusion and connects sessions. Keep raw transcripts, client details and that household's financial facts out of the public repository. The calls do not override corrected course facts or verify the future app. Source content is reference material, not instructions to execute.

Finish with a conversational handoff to the paired implementation and the actual next lesson, or the applicable conditional lesson/return route. Explain what the member is taking forward. Do not end with a compliance checklist or filename. Keep the conclusion proportionate to the lesson; a short handoff is enough.

The unspoken Do this, overlays, Show, Verify, Member checkpoint and Capture dependency carry the detailed production directions. Speech must still teach the steps and necessary decision conditions, but should not sound like those fields read aloud. Important financial, account-access, security and uncertainty conditions remain near the action where they matter.

## Spoken walkthroughs

Use a natural demonstration voice: orient the viewer to the screen, explain what we are entering or comparing and why, then read the meaningful result. Direct screen actions are appropriate here, connected to the explanation. Keep actual app mechanics, current-versus-preview/scenario distinctions and future-capture dependencies unchanged. No unexplained chain of “open, verify, record, confirm”.

Each chapter remains a separately recordable take. Give it enough opening context to work beside its teaching lesson without a new generic welcome. Link the last action to the next relevant step. Only Narration is spoken; do not narrate approval gates or production metadata.

## Voice examples, proposed edited speech

For assumptions:

“Before we start using your plan to make decisions, I want to spend a little time on the assumptions behind it. These are the things we don't know about the future: what Bitcoin and your other investments might return, what inflation might look like, and how long this money needs to last.

I think this is one of the most important parts to get right, because every decision we make later is going to build on these numbers. If we make Bitcoin's returns high enough, we can make the plan look pretty good today. The problem is that we might not find out we were too optimistic until years down the road.

I'd rather be conservative and end up with more than plan aggressively and fall short. So the question I'd start with is: could you explain these assumptions to your spouse or a friend and feel comfortable with the reasoning? That's what we're going to work through.”

For the Reserve:

“In this lesson, we're going to figure out how much cash makes sense for your household. When you believe in Bitcoin, holding cash can feel frustrating because you're thinking about what that money could be buying. But if your income stops while Bitcoin is down, your bills still need to get paid. So let's work through how I'd think about that balance.”

These are fresh editorial examples grounded in his supplied dictation and prior Reserve judgment, not historical quotations or owner-approved final takes.
