## 6.3 Read the simulation result and use spending guardrails

*`TEACH` · ~6 min · PRE-DICTATION FILMING DRAFT*

This lesson turns the completed retirement strategy into an operating rule.

Orange Plan runs the plan through 1,000 market paths. Every path still uses the income, taxes, debts, life events, contributions, retirement spending, withdrawal order, and other decisions in the saved plan. The sequence of market returns changes from one path to the next.

A result of 820 of 1,000 means the exact plan funded the household through the planning age in 820 simulated paths. The other 180 paths needed an adjustment before the end of the plan.

Use that result as a stress test of the current facts, assumptions, and strategy.

🎬 VISUAL — 1,000 simulated paths resolve into “820 of 1,000 worked” and “Money lasted through age 95.”

The normal Orange Plan standard is 800 successful simulations out of 1,000. The app uses that standard to find the earliest modeled retirement date. A custom standard lives in Advanced model settings for a power user with a specific reason to change it.

The goal is a plan you actually want that reaches the Orange Plan standard. Chasing 1,000 of 1,000 by working much longer or spending far less can produce a safer-looking plan that no longer matches the life you want.

The result also needs a truthful freshness state.

- **Preliminary** means important facts or accuracy details remain.
- **Current** means the relevant facts and calculation receipt are current.
- **Stale** means a modeled fact changed after the last run.
- **Unavailable** means the calculation or required source data failed.

Read the count together with the planning age, the planned retirement date, the earliest modeled date, and the freshness state.

The simulation result helps set the plan. Spending guardrails help operate it after retirement.

Orange Plan translates the spending policy into three portfolio values for the current year:

- the lower guardrail;
- the current retirement-portfolio value;
- the upper guardrail.

🎬 VISUAL — Lower guardrail · Current portfolio · Upper guardrail on one labeled line, followed by “Within your guardrails.”

When the current portfolio stays between the two guardrails, the spending plan remains inside the policy and no spending review is required.

Crossing the lower guardrail opens a review of whether spending should move down.

Crossing the upper guardrail opens a review of whether the household can spend more.

The guardrail summary communicates the portfolio levels and whether a review is needed. The separate annual review calculates the proposed spending adjustment, shows it in Preview, and lets the household decide whether to save it.

The underlying policy still uses the full remaining plan: income, taxes, debt, life events, assumptions, withdrawal strategy, and planning age. The guardrail levels must be calculated for the current year and the same retirement-portfolio definition each time.

Annual changes remain capped so the retirement paycheck does not swing sharply from one review to the next. A large move may take more than one annual review to work through.

The upper guardrail matters because a retirement plan should give permission as well as protection. A household with more margin than it needs may be able to spend more, give more, or move another goal forward.

The annual review also connects the result to the Reserve.

When the plan is under pressure, the household may lower spending and pause a discretionary Reserve refill instead of selling assets into weakness. When the plan is healthy, the Reserve can refill toward its target. A Reserve near its hard floor still needs attention because the cash is there to prevent a forced sale.

The Retirement Income walkthrough shows the income floor, gap, Bridge, withdrawal order, and saved spending policy. Module 9 recalculates the completed plan, reads the final current simulation result, and saves the yearly baseline.
