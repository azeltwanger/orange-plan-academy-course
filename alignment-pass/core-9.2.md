## 9.2 Compare decisions and read Your Plan

*`TEACH` · ~5 min · PRE-DICTATION FILMING DRAFT*

Current is the saved plan. Preview is the proposed change. Scenarios are where you test one focused question without changing Current.

That separation keeps the baseline trustworthy.

A useful scenario asks one clear question:

- What happens if I retire three years earlier?
- What happens if Bitcoin returns are lower?
- What happens if we move to another state?
- What happens if we pay this debt faster?
- What happens if Social Security starts at a different age?

Change only the inputs required to answer the question.

When the scenario points to a real planning change, move that decision into Preview and compare it beside Current.

🎬 VISUAL — Current and Preview side by side: 790 of 1,000 → 824 of 1,000, planned date, earliest modeled date, spending, and taxes.

Start with the simulation result. Read the exact count, the through-age, and the freshness state.

Then compare the outcomes that materially changed:

- planned retirement date;
- earliest modeled retirement date;
- retirement spending;
- taxes;
- accessible Bridge money;
- debt and LTV risk;
- Reserve pressure;
- estate value;
- and any new failure point.

A decision can improve one number and weaken another. The side-by-side comparison helps you see the whole trade-off.

Save the change when the Preview reflects the plan you intend to use. Current then becomes the new saved baseline. Keep or delete the scenario as evidence, but give every saved version a clear name and purpose.

The final completed-plan review happens after every Build & improve area is intentionally complete.

Recalculate the plan and read:

1. how many of the 1,000 simulations worked;
2. the age the money was tested through;
3. the planned retirement date;
4. the earliest modeled retirement date at the Orange Plan standard;
5. whether the result is Current, Preliminary, Stale, or Unavailable;
6. the one next action, when one is required.

The Orange Plan standard is 800 successful simulations out of 1,000 for a normal user. The finished plan should reach that standard through a strategy the household actually wants. The goal is a usable plan, not the highest possible score.

Your Plan turns the saved baseline into a read-only document for the household and the professionals who help carry it out.

I would read it in four passes: position, trajectory, risk, and actions.

🎬 VISUAL — Your Plan reading order: Position → Trajectory → Risk → Actions.

**Position** asks whether the current facts are true. Check net worth, Bitcoin, account ownership, holdings, and debts. Fix an incorrect fact at its source, then regenerate Your Plan.

**Trajectory** asks where the plan is headed. Read the simulation count, planned retirement date, earliest modeled date, spending plan, and retirement funding together.

**Risk** asks what could break the plan. Review alternate Bitcoin paths, sequence risk, the Reserve and Bridge, debt and LTV, taxes, custody, and family continuity.

**Actions** are the one to three next steps that can be finished. A plan review earns its value when the actions happen.

The assumptions and methodology explain what every result rests on. You should be able to defend the major return, inflation, spending, longevity, tax, and withdrawal assumptions in plain language.

Save one Your Plan PDF after each annual review, with the year in the filename. Compare the new document with the prior year:

- net worth and Bitcoin's share;
- simulation count and result state;
- planned and earliest modeled retirement dates;
- spending and guardrail status;
- and whether last year's actions were completed.

Use Your Plan as the agenda for the family conversation. Share the relevant tax pages and lot records with the CPA. Share the protection summary with the estate attorney without including secrets.

The final walkthrough recalculates the completed plan, tests one decision, compares Current with Preview, saves the intended change, reads Your Plan in this order, and saves the first yearly PDF.
