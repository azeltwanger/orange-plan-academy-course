# Use Orange Plan AI to understand the numbers and make better decisions

Orange Plan AI is most useful as a second set of eyes on the current financial plan.

The app calculates the plan. The AI helps the learner:

- understand an output,
- trace it to the source,
- identify the main bottleneck,
- compare two real decisions,
- challenge assumptions and risks,
- interpret a Scenario,
- and turn the result into a practical next step.

The goal is useful judgment from the household's own plan—not a generic summary of the page.

## Five useful jobs

### 1. Explain a number

Ask the AI to reconcile the output and identify the source.

**Example**

> Why does Cash Flow show $3,761 when the household is putting $4,000 to work every month? Reconcile the numbers and tell me which amount is already included in Debt.

A useful answer should explain that the $500 extra auto principal is already inside planned debt payments and the remaining $3,500 is routed to accounts.

### 2. Review and prioritize the full plan

Use a broad review when it is not clear where to begin.

**Example**

> Review my full plan. Rank the top three things that deserve attention by impact. Tell me what looks strong, what is holding the plan back, and what can wait.

The result should prioritize the few issues that materially change the plan rather than produce a long equal-weight checklist.

### 3. Compare a real decision

Name the two options and ask for the benefit, cost, risk, and plan effect of each.

**Example**

> Compare keeping retirement spending at $100,000 with using the Balanced spending amount. Show the monthly difference, what happens to confidence and the retirement date, and what flexibility we give up.

Other useful comparisons include:

- Extra debt versus Bridge funding
- Traditional versus Roth contributions
- Rebalancing through new contributions versus a taxable sale
- Earlier retirement versus higher spending
- Selling Bitcoin versus borrowing against it

### 4. Challenge the plan

Use the AI to look for fragility rather than only confirming a preferred answer.

Ask:

- Which assumption is doing the most work?
- What would force us to sell Bitcoin during a major drawdown?
- What is the strongest argument against this strategy?
- Which missing input could materially change the decision?
- What important risk are we underweighting?

### 5. Turn the result into action

Ask the AI to separate the response into:

- **Action now** — a real-life step
- **Model next** — a specific comparison or Scenario
- **Cleanup** — data that should be corrected or completed
- **Can wait** — lower-priority work

It can also summarize the facts and questions to bring to a CPA, attorney, custody professional, insurance professional, or lender.

## Start broad or narrow

### Start broad

Use **Review my full plan** when the learner does not know what deserves attention first.

### Start narrow

Use the focused review from the planning area when the decision is already known:

- Cash Flow — spending, reserve, and routing
- Debt — payoff order, payment burden, and leverage
- Allocation — target mix, funding timeframes, and drift
- Tax — basis, conversions, and tax windows
- Income — retirement spending, funding, withdrawals, and borrowing comparisons
- Scenarios — explain what changed and why
- Protect — family access, documents, and the next missing step

The focused review can use the relevant page context, so the learner does not need to restate the entire plan.

## Ask a better question

A useful question has three parts:

1. **Decision** — what are you trying to decide?
2. **Constraint** — what matters or cannot be compromised?
3. **Requested output** — what should the answer show?

**Example**

> I want to retire at 55 without making a Bitcoin-backed loan necessary. Use the current plan numbers. What is the biggest constraint, which input is driving it, and what is the next move?

**Example**

> We are above the Bitcoin allocation band, but we do not want to create a large taxable sale. Compare correcting the drift through new contributions with selling today.

When an answer sounds generic, follow up with:

> Use my plan numbers. Show the evidence, the trade-off, and the specific next action.

## What a strong answer contains

A useful AI answer should include:

1. A direct verdict
2. The plan number or source supporting it
3. The trade-off
4. The next action

Useful follow-ups include:

- Where did that number come from?
- Which input would I change?
- What else should move if I change it?
- Does the current plan already answer this, or is a Scenario needed?
- What would make the opposite choice reasonable?

## Use Scenarios for decisions

Understand the current plan first. Use a Scenario when a real decision or uncertain assumption needs comparison.

For the demo household:

- 3% Baseline inflation: 94.6% confidence
- 4% inflation Scenario: 91.6% confidence

A useful question is:

> Why did confidence fall by 3 points, which future costs changed most, and does this difference change the retirement decision?

The point is not only to read the new number. It is to understand whether the change affects the decision.

## Prepare a professional question

The AI can organize the relevant facts without pretending to provide the final professional answer.

**Example**

> We own 1.75 Bitcoin. Basis is complete for 1.25, reconstruction is pending for 0.40, and 0.10 is unknown. What records should we bring to the CPA, and which planning decisions should wait until basis is resolved?

## Three rules

1. **App math wins.** The AI explains, compares, and prioritizes from calculated plan results.
2. **No secrets.** Never enter wallet backups, seed phrases, private keys, passphrases, PINs, passwords, full account numbers, Social Security numbers, or authentication codes.
3. **Verify execution details.** Current tax, legal, insurance, lending, and custody actions still require the appropriate professional or provider.

Optional memory may hold planning preferences such as preserving Bitcoin, retiring sooner, or reducing stress. It should not hold balances, credentials, account numbers, or recovery information.
