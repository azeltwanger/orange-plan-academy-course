# Fund a known future cost without raiding the reserve

## Six questions

1. What amount is the household committing to provide?
2. When is it likely to be needed?
3. What is already saved?
4. What can be paid from future cash flow?
5. What can come from proceeds, assets, or deliberate financing?
6. When must the amount stop depending on Bitcoin's price?

## Time-horizon lanes

| Time until needed | Planning approach |
|---|---|
| 0–12 months | Stable and liquid |
| 1–3 years | Protect the amount that cannot be replaced from cash flow |
| 3–7 years | Some investment risk with a written de-risking schedule |
| 7–10 years | Bridge funding with year-by-year transition toward stability |
| 10+ years | Long-term assets may carry more of the goal; define the future exit rule |

## Demo vehicle goal

- Purchase ceiling in 5 years: $35,000
- Expected vehicle proceeds: $10,000
- Expected cash flow in purchase year: $5,000
- Amount to accumulate: $20,000
- Simple monthly amount before growth: about $333

## Bill versus source

The life event records when the bill occurs. The funding account or route records where the money comes from. Do not count the same dollars twice.

## Where the gap comes from

- **What it means:** part of the commitment still needing a source
- **Calculated from:** commitment minus current savings, cash flow, proceeds, and deliberate financing
- **Edit source:** life event and funding plan
- **This affects:** routing, Bridge, withdrawals, confidence, and earliest date

## Done when

Every known cost has one commitment, date, funding source, and de-risking rule.
