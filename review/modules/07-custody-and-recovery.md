7.1 Choose the custody setup that matches the Bitcoin and the family
~8 min at 155 wpm · VOICE-MATCHED DRAFT — Austin review + custody review pending
============================================================

The custody decision is not “which setup is the most advanced?”

It is which setup protects the amount, fits the job of the Bitcoin, and can still be recovered by the people who depend on it.

Too little protection for a life-changing balance is a risk. So is more complexity than the household can operate.

== CUSTODY AND ESTATE ARE DIFFERENT JOBS ==

Custody is operational access:

- where the Bitcoin is held,
- what controls it,
- and whether recovery works when a device, person, or provider is unavailable.

Estate planning is legal authority and transfer:

- who can act,
- who receives the property,
- and how the family begins.

A key can move Bitcoin without proving legal authority. A will can name an heir without teaching the heir how the wallet works. Modules 7 and 8 have to connect, but they should not be collapsed into one decision.

== ASK FIVE QUESTIONS FOR EACH BALANCE ==

1. Where is the Bitcoin held?
2. What type of custody controls it?
3. Who understands the process?
4. What happens if the owner is unavailable?
5. What is the largest single point of failure?

The demo household has 1.50 BTC on one hardware wallet and 0.25 BTC at one exchange.

It can answer the first two questions. It cannot yet answer the last three well:

- only one spouse has operated the wallet,
- recovery has not been tested,
- the device and backup share one physical failure domain,
- and the family has no complete process map.

That is not a vague custody problem anymore. It is a short list of work.

== GIVE EACH BALANCE A JOB ==

The 0.25 BTC exchange balance is an operating balance for purchases and transfers. The household sets a limit on how much remains there.

The 1.50 BTC hardware-wallet balance is long-term Legacy capital. It should not depend on one person's memory or an untested backup.

A small practice wallet can have a third job: teaching and testing without exposing the main balance.

Name the job before choosing the custody design. A near-term operating balance and long-term family wealth do not have to use the same setup.

== DOCUMENT THE PROCESS, NEVER THE SECRETS ==

Orange Plan and the Family Custody Map can record:

- custody type,
- provider or general location,
- people involved,
- where the process begins,
- and the date recovery was tested.

They never receive a seed phrase, private key, passphrase, PIN, password, wallet backup, safe combination, or complete spending path.

The document should help the right person find the process without giving a thief enough information to move the Bitcoin.

== FOUR PRACTICAL LEVELS ==

The levels are a teaching tool, not universal dollar thresholds.

**Level 1 — Hardened third-party account**

Reasonable for a limited operating or learning balance when email, password, authentication, recovery, and withdrawal protections are strong. The household accepts provider and access risk.

**Level 2 — Tested single-signature hardware wallet**

The household controls the key. The device, offline backup, receiving and sending process, and recovery test all work. One complete backup can still control the funds.

**Level 3 — Family-ready self-custody**

The setup no longer depends on one person's memory or one physical location. Another trusted person understands the process and can begin a tested recovery without a secret inside the instructions.

This can be a carefully designed single-signature setup or another supported arrangement. A passphrase is not required merely to earn a higher level.

**Level 4 — Multi-key or professionally supported custody**

Used when one-key risk is unacceptable and the household can maintain the added key, provider, configuration, and inheritance complexity.

More sovereignty creates more operational responsibility. More convenience creates more third-party dependence. The goal is the risk the household can actually manage.

== APPLY THE LEVELS TO THE DEMO ==

The exchange balance remains Level 1 operating money after the account is hardened and the maximum balance is set.

The hardware-wallet balance has not completed Level 2 because recovery is untested.

First, the household verifies the device, receive and send process, offline backup, and recovery.

Then it becomes family-ready: Jordan practices on a small wallet, the components no longer share one failure domain, and the custody map gives the family a safe starting point.

Only after the simpler process works does the household compare whether a multi-key or collaborative design removes more risk than it adds.

== REASSESS AT TODAY'S STAKES ==

A setup can become stale without the household touching it.

The Bitcoin value rises, a child is born, a spouse becomes more dependent on the plan, or a provider changes its recovery process.

Review the five questions annually and after a material balance, provider, family, or custody change.

The app can track readiness. It cannot prove the backup works or that another person can recover. The real test supplies the proof.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose the job and custody level for each Bitcoin balance, the maximum operating amount left with a third party, and the largest mismatch to fix first.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Record custody type, people, process status, and the next readiness action in Protect. Keep every Bitcoin secret outside the app and outside the process map.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

Every meaningful balance has a job, custody level, recovery process, and next action—and no secret exists in Orange Plan or the family instructions.

7.2 Set up a hardware wallet and prove the backup works
~8 min at 155 wpm · VOICE-MATCHED DRAFT — Austin review + custody review pending
============================================================

Owning a hardware wallet is not the same as having a recovery process.

The setup is complete when the device is verified, receive and send details are confirmed on the trusted display, the backup remains offline, and recovery works under calm conditions.

Follow the current instructions for the exact device and backup format. The concept is evergreen; the button-by-button procedure is not.

== DEVICE, KEY, AND BACKUP ==

The hardware wallet signs with the private keys under its supported design without exposing them to the connected computer or phone.

The device is replaceable. The wallet backup—and any passphrase required with it—is what recreates access when the device is lost, damaged, or reset.

Different wallets can use different standards and recovery formats. Do not assume every backup works in every device or app. Confirm compatibility before a real recovery or migration.

== BUY AND VERIFY THE DEVICE ==

Buy from the manufacturer or a verified authorized seller when the manufacturer supports one.

Follow the official authenticity and firmware-verification process.

A new device should not arrive with a prewritten backup or an already-created wallet. If the package, firmware, or setup does not match official instructions, stop and contact the manufacturer through a channel found independently.

== GENERATE THE WALLET AND KEEP THE BACKUP OFFLINE ==

Use the official supported application and generate a new wallet on the device.

Record the backup exactly as instructed. Do not photograph it or type it into a computer, phone, password manager, cloud note, website, support chat, or AI tool.

Set the device PIN or local protection. The PIN protects that device; it does not replace the backup.

If a passphrase is used, the exact passphrase may open a separate wallet and may be required with the backup. A wrong passphrase can open a different empty wallet rather than produce an obvious error. Passphrase design is Advanced unless the household already understands and has tested it.

== VERIFY RECEIVE AND SEND ON THE DEVICE ==

Before receiving a meaningful amount, generate an address and verify it on the hardware-wallet display.

Compare the address on the device with the address in the sending application. Do not rely only on copied first and last characters from transaction history.

Send a small amount that makes the process real without making a setup mistake catastrophic.

Then send a small amount back out. Verify destination, amount, and fee on the device display before signing.

If the trusted display does not match what you intended, reject the transaction.

No legitimate support person needs you to approve a transaction to “secure” the wallet.

== TEST RECOVERY WITHOUT CREATING A NEW RISK ==

Use the safest supported method for the device:

- manufacturer backup-check feature,
- restore to a compatible spare device,
- or another documented recovery drill.

A full wipe-and-restore can be a strong test, but do not wipe the only working device holding meaningful funds unless the backup has already been checked, the exact process is understood, and a safe fallback exists.

I would rather use a small practice wallet or spare device for the first test.

Recovery is proven when the expected wallet, accounts, and addresses are reproduced—not simply when an app opens.

If a passphrase is part of the setup, test the exact backup-plus-passphrase combination.

== SEPARATE THE FAILURE DOMAINS ==

A device and backup in the same desk can disappear in the same fire, flood, theft, or move.

Separate the events that can remove them. A durable metal backup can reduce physical damage risk, but it does not solve theft, discovery, or family access on its own.

The right locations depend on the household, legal access, privacy, and whether another component is required to spend.

== SOFTWARE AND SUPPORT ==

Use official software and independently verify the source of updates.

Before an update, confirm the backup and recovery process are current. Do not type the wallet backup into a computer because an update or support message asks for it.

Urgent unsolicited support is a reason to stop, not a reason to act faster.

== LET THE FAMILY PRACTICE SAFELY ==

The spouse or executor's first recovery should not be the main balance after the owner dies.

Use a small practice wallet. Let the person follow the process-level instructions and explain the sequence back without exposing the main backup.

This teaches what the device, backup, passphrase, provider, and legal process each do.

== WHERE READINESS COMES FROM ==

A recovery-ready status is based on:

- verified device and software,
- offline backup,
- confirmed receive and send,
- successful supported recovery test,
- separated failure domains,
- and another person understanding the process when the household requires it.

In Orange Plan, the app records status and date. The real custody process supplies the proof.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose the recovery test, backup locations, and the other person who needs to understand the process.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Record device type and completion status in Protect. Mark recovery complete only after the supported test succeeds. Never enter the backup, passphrase, PIN, or private key.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

A small receive and send work, details were verified on the device, recovery succeeded safely, and the required family participant knows where the process begins.

7.3 Remove single points of failure and harden the accounts
~8 min at 155 wpm · VOICE-MATCHED DRAFT — Austin review + custody review pending
============================================================

Most custody failures do not begin with someone breaking Bitcoin.

They begin with one device, one backup, one location, one person, one email account, or one provider that has no working alternative.

== THREE FAILURE DOMAINS ==

**Physical:** a device, backup, security key, or document is lost in one fire, flood, theft, or move.

**Human:** only one person knows what exists or how the process starts.

**Provider:** an exchange, custodian, email provider, phone carrier, or another service will not allow the household to authenticate or withdraw.

The demo has all three risks: device and backup together, one knowledgeable spouse, and an exchange account whose recovery chain needs hardening.

== RUN THE “ONLY ONE” REVIEW ==

Ask whether recovery depends on only one:

- device,
- backup,
- physical location,
- person,
- authentication method,
- recovery email or phone,
- exchange or custodian,
- required multi-key configuration record,
- or person connecting the legal and technical process.

One item is not automatically a failure when a tested replacement path exists. The question is whether losing or delaying it stops recovery or creates unacceptable risk.

I would rank the top three by cost of loss, then fix the first one. Trying to redesign everything at once is how nothing gets completed.

== FIX THE FAILURE DOMAIN, NOT ONLY THE OBJECT ==

Two backups in one safe may disappear together. Two security keys on one keychain may be lost together. Three multisig keys in one home do not create geographic redundancy.

The fix may be a second copy, location, person, provider, or a threshold design.

Do not create redundancy by scattering complete spending secrets without a theft and privacy plan.

== TEACH THE SECOND PERSON THE PROCESS ==

Jordan does not need the main wallet backup memorized.

Jordan needs to know:

- that the Bitcoin exists,
- which custody type is used,
- where the process documents are,
- who else is involved,
- and what never to share.

Use the practice wallet from 7.2. Ask the person to explain the first steps back rather than only asking whether the document makes sense.

== HARDEN EMAIL AND ACCOUNT RECOVERY ==

Email is often part of every downstream password-reset process, so secure it first.

Use a unique password and the strongest practical authentication the provider supports.

A FIDO security key or properly implemented passkey can provide phishing-resistant authentication because it is bound to the real service.

Keep a backup authenticator or recovery method in a separate location so one lost key does not create a lockout.

When phishing-resistant options are unavailable, an authenticator app is generally stronger than SMS-only authentication. Manually entered one-time codes can still be phished. Also use the carrier's account PIN, port-out protection, or transfer lock when available.

== HARDEN THE EXCHANGE AND FINANCIAL ACCOUNTS ==

For each provider:

- use a unique password,
- enable the strongest supported authentication,
- preserve backup codes or authenticators under the recovery plan,
- review devices, sessions, API keys, recovery contacts, and linked accounts,
- and enable withdrawal allowlists, delays, address locks, alerts, or freezes when supported and understood.

Keep the third-party Bitcoin balance limited to its operating job.

== USE SIMPLE SCAM RULES ==

No legitimate support person needs a seed phrase, private key, passphrase, PIN, password, or one-time authentication code.

Treat unsolicited contact as hostile until independently verified.

Never install remote-access software, share the screen, or let an unsolicited support person control a computer or phone used for financial accounts or Bitcoin.

Urgency is a reason to stop. Close the conversation and contact the provider through its official app or a channel found independently.

Verify transaction details on the trusted device display and reject anything you did not initiate.

Guaranteed returns and “send Bitcoin to receive more back” are scams.

== BUILD THE NO-SECRETS MAP ==

The Family Custody Map lists the asset category, custody type, provider or general location, person who knows the process, and where the process documents are held.

It never contains the backup words, key, passphrase, PIN, password, safe combination, or complete spending path.

A descriptor or non-secret wallet configuration may still reveal addresses and balances. Keep the protected copies required by the custody design without publishing them broadly.

== WHERE READINESS COMES FROM ==

Protect readiness comes from saved checklist items, people, documents, and review dates.

It can identify a missing recovery test or map. It cannot verify that the backup is accurate or that Jordan can execute the process.

The app owns the status. The real test owns the proof.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose the most expensive single point of failure, the specific fix and date, and the authentication standard the household will maintain.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Track the security checklist, people, Family Custody Map, and next action in Protect. Keep credentials and Bitcoin secrets outside the app.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

The top failure is fixed or scheduled, the primary email and financial accounts have strong authentication with a backup method, and another required person can explain the process without receiving the secret.

## MATCHING LESSON TEXT
