2.1 Find your surplus and your two spending numbers
~8 min at 155 wpm · VOICE-MATCHED DRAFT — Austin review pending
============================================================

Before the next dollar goes anywhere, we need to know what is actually left each month and what the household really spends.

The plan needs two spending numbers:

- normal living spending for the life the plan is supporting,
- and bare-bones spending for a temporary period when the household has to cut back.

== WHERE SURPLUS COMES FROM ==

Surplus is calculated. It is not a field you change until the result feels comfortable.

> Income − estimated taxes − living spending − debt payments = surplus

Our demo household earns $190,000 before tax. The simple teaching estimate uses $40,000 of taxes, $80,000 of normal living spending outside debt, and $22,000 of required mortgage and auto payments.

$190,000 minus $40,000, minus $80,000, minus $22,000 leaves $48,000 a year, or $4,000 a month.

That round number is the household's approved monthly routing plan. It is not a promise that the live Cash Flow headline will display exactly $4,000.

== WHY THE APP SHOWS A DIFFERENT SURPLUS ==

The current engine calculates $36,862 of tax for this synthetic household rather than the round $40,000 teaching estimate.

That creates about $4,261 a month before any extra debt strategy.

The household has already chosen $500 a month of extra principal on the auto loan. Cash Flow includes that planned extra payment in the Debt row, so the page shows about $3,761 left after the full debt plan.

The household then routes $3,500 of that displayed amount to the 401(k), HSA, Roth IRA, and taxable Bridge. About $261 remains as operating cushion.

So there are two useful numbers:

- **Decision capacity before extra debt:** about $4,261 a month
- **Displayed surplus after the planned extra debt:** about $3,761 a month

The $500 difference is not missing. It is the debt decision already being funded.

This is exactly why you trace the number instead of editing the output. If the app and a round example differ, check the current tax calculation and whether planned extra debt is already included.

== WHY DEBT IS SEPARATE FROM LIVING SPENDING ==

This was one of the most common points of confusion in client calls.

Someone looks at living spending and says the number is too low because total monthly outflow is higher. Usually the difference is the mortgage, car payment, or another debt shown separately.

The app separates debt because it has an ending date.

If a mortgage is paid off at 67, that payment needs to disappear at 67. If it is also inside living spending, the plan funds it once as debt and keeps funding it again as living spending after it is gone.

Living spending is what the household spends outside debt payments. The debt rows own the required payments, extra payments, and payoff dates.

== FIND NORMAL SPENDING FROM REAL LIFE ==

Start with at least 3 months of account history. Use a full year when the recent period includes travel, a move, medical costs, family support, or another unusual event.

One client believed normal spending was around $80,000. The year was closer to $110,000, but it included family visiting from overseas, unusual travel, and support sent to a parent.

The answer was not automatically $80,000 or $110,000. We separated:

- what belongs in an ordinary year,
- what was genuinely one-time,
- and what is irregular but likely to happen again.

A one-time trip does not need to become permanent monthly spending. Recurring family support is not really one-time just because it arrives unevenly.

Normal spending should represent the life the plan is expected to keep funding. If travel or gifts are important and likely to continue, include a reasonable amount. Do not remove everything enjoyable simply to improve the confidence result.

== FIND BARE-BONES SPENDING ==

Bare-bones spending answers a different question:

If income stopped tomorrow, what is the temporary minimum the household could live on while still paying the bills that have to be paid?

For the demo household, normal living spending is about $6,667 a month outside debt. Bare-bones spending is $5,000.

The $5,000 includes essential housing costs outside the mortgage payment, groceries, utilities, insurance, transportation, healthcare, and other costs that cannot be paused. Flexible travel, dining, gifts, and projects can come down temporarily.

That lower number is what the household uses for the working reserve in the next lesson.

== LOOK FOR THE BIG LEVERS ==

This is not a course about tracking every coffee forever.

Review the large recurring categories and ask:

- Is it essential or clearly worth the money?
- Can we keep it and pay less?
- Are we still paying for something we no longer value?

Subscriptions and services are easy wins. Housing, vehicles, childcare, healthcare, and location create the largest changes when the plan needs a meaningful adjustment.

Make the spending decision in real life, then update the plan. Do not lower the input only to make the output look better.

== KEEP COMMITTED CASH OUT OF THE SURPLUS ==

Money already reserved for quarterly taxes or a near-term bill is not available surplus and is not part of the emergency reserve.

If $40,000 is in savings but $15,000 belongs to taxes and $5,000 belongs to a bill due soon, only $20,000 is uncommitted before comparing it with the reserve target.

== MAKE THE ROUTING NUMBER REPEATABLE ==

The calculated result is the maximum available under the current inputs and strategy. The amount you automate should be something the cash flow can repeat.

The demo deliberately keeps about $261 a month unassigned instead of routing every calculated dollar. I would rather leave a small operating cushion than reverse transfers every time bill timing moves.

== WHERE THIS NUMBER COMES FROM ==

For displayed surplus:

**What it means:** money left after estimated taxes, living spending, required debt payments, and any planned extra debt already included on the page.

**Calculated from:** income, engine-estimated taxes, living spending, and the full saved debt plan.

**Edit source:** the underlying income, spending, tax assumptions, or debt strategy—not the headline.

**This affects:** reserve funding, contribution routing, future balances, confidence, and the earliest target-qualified date.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose normal monthly living spending, bare-bones monthly spending, the extra-debt amount, and the contribution amount the household can repeat while keeping an operating cushion.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Review the Cash Flow source rows. Keep debt payments out of living spending, set bare-bones essentials in Reserve settings, and confirm whether planned extra debt is already included before routing the remaining surplus.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

You can rebuild the displayed surplus from its sources, explain the difference between capacity before extra debt and surplus after it, and state the monthly amount the household can actually repeat.

2.2 Size your cash reserve in months of spending
~8 min at 155 wpm · AUSTIN DICTATION + VOICE-MATCHED COMPLETION — calibration master; Austin review pending
============================================================

Let me start by showing you why the reserve matters.

Our demo household holds 1.75 Bitcoin. In the 2022 bear market, Bitcoin fell roughly 77% from its peak. If a job loss happens during a drawdown and the household does not have enough cash, the mortgage and other bills still have to be paid. Now they may be forced to sell Bitcoin at the exact time they wanted to avoid selling it.

On the flip side, too much cash has a cost. It sits outside the long-term growth assets and loses purchasing power to inflation.

The decision is the amount in the middle that protects the household without keeping more cash than the job requires.

== THE FORMULA ==

The working-reserve target is:

> Bare-bones monthly spending × target months

Bare-bones spending came from the last lesson. It is the temporary minimum the household can live on, not the normal lifestyle.

Using the lower emergency number means each dollar of cash buys more time. Orange Plan performs the multiplication. The household chooses the spending basis and the number of months.

== CHOOSE THE TARGET MONTHS ==

The number depends on the household.

I think 3 months is the minimum starting point for a household with two stable incomes and few people depending on it. For most households, 6 months is a reasonable baseline. A single-income household, self-employment, variable pay, or a long job-search risk can justify 12 months or more.

The factors come back to two questions:

How reliable is the income?

And how difficult is it to reduce the monthly spending?

Variable pay, one earner, dependents, self-funded healthcare, a large fixed housing cost, and a specialized job market all push the number higher.

This is not a formula where every factor automatically adds one month. The point is to estimate how long the household may need to operate without a normal paycheck and how much stress it can tolerate during that period.

== THE DEMO HOUSEHOLD ==

The demo household starts at the 6-month baseline.

It has two stable W-2 incomes, which supports the lower end of the range. It also has two children and meaningful fixed housing costs, which argue against reducing the target too far.

They choose 6 months.

Bare-bones spending is $5,000 a month.

Six months times $5,000 gives a $30,000 working-reserve target.

They already hold $30,000 in the reserve account, so the working reserve is fully funded. The next decision is to protect that money from being casually used for a vacation or another planned expense.

== THE RESERVE CHANGES JOBS IN RETIREMENT ==

While working, the reserve protects a lost paycheck and unexpected costs.

In retirement, there is no paycheck to replace. The cash buffer protects the part of spending that has to come from the portfolio, especially during the early years when a drawdown can create the most damage.

I use 18 months as a planning default for the initial retirement cash buffer. It is not a prediction of how long the next Bitcoin recovery will take, and it is not a number everybody has to copy.

The actual retirement target depends on the spending basis selected in Cash Flow, the recurring income in each year, and the household's willingness to reduce spending or sell assets during a weak period.

The canonical demo amount will come from the current app checkpoint after the retirement-income settings are complete. We do not need to invent it in this lesson.

Later in the course, we will decide how that cash buffer is spent and refilled. This lesson owns the target months and basis, not the withdrawal strategy.

== THE SCENARIO QUESTION ==

Before choosing the target, picture this:

Bitcoin falls 50%, income stops for 6 months, and the next job takes longer than expected.

Would you rather hold more cash now and spend through that period safely?

Or would you rather hold less cash, cut harder, and accept the chance of selling Bitcoin during the drawdown?

I do not think there is one right answer. The number should match the risk the household is actually willing to carry.

== WHERE THIS NUMBER COMES FROM ==

For the reserve target:

**What it means:** the cash amount the household chose to protect a period without normal income or portfolio sales.

**Calculated from:** selected monthly spending basis multiplied by target months.

**Edit source:** Reserve settings in Cash Flow.

**This affects:** the reserve funding gap, how much surplus remains for debt and contributions, and the retirement cash buffer later.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose the spending basis, target months, and the maximum amount of monthly surplus the reserve can claim while it is underfunded.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Open Cash Flow → Reserve settings. Enter bare-bones essentials, choose the reserve basis, set target months, and set the monthly build cap.

Orange Plan calculates the target amount and compares it with the holdings marked as reserve.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

The app shows the target amount and current funding, and you can explain why the selected number of months fits the household rather than copying a generic emergency-fund rule.

# 02-3_fund-a-known-future-cost-the-six-questio.md

## SPOKEN SCRIPT

TELEPROMPTER SCRIPT — segment 2.3
2.3 Fund a known future cost without raiding the reserve
~7 min at 155 wpm · VOICE-MATCHED DRAFT — Austin review pending
============================================================

A reserve is for what the household did not see coming.

A vehicle replacement, roof, home purchase, college commitment, or another planned bill needs its own funding source. If a known cost uses the emergency reserve, the household becomes unprotected after paying a bill that was already on the calendar.

== START WITH THE COMMITMENT ==

Do not begin with the largest possible price.

Begin with what the household is actually committing to provide.

A renovation may have a $60,000 dream version and a $30,000 plan. A parent may help with college without promising four years at any school. A vehicle may be funded partly from the current car and partly from cash flow.

The baseline should model the decision the household is making, not the highest number found online.

== THE SIX QUESTIONS ==

For each known cost, answer:

1. What amount are we committing to provide?
2. When is it likely to be needed?
3. What is already saved?
4. What can be paid from cash flow when it arrives?
5. What can come from expected proceeds, assets, or deliberate financing?
6. When must the committed amount stop depending on Bitcoin's price?

The answers create the funding gap and the amount of volatility the money can carry.

== MATCH THE FUNDING LANE TO THE DATE ==

The closer the date, the less the committed amount should depend on an asset that can be down 70% when the bill arrives.

- **Inside 12 months:** keep the known amount liquid and stable.
- **1–3 years:** protect the amount the household cannot replace from cash flow.
- **3–7 years:** some investment risk can remain, with a written schedule for moving committed dollars toward stability.
- **7–10 years:** this is the transition lane people skip. The money can remain in the Bridge, but the plan needs year-by-year de-risking rather than waiting until year 9 and hoping Bitcoin is high.
- **10+ years:** long-term assets can carry more of the goal, with an exit rule for when the horizon becomes shorter.

The principle is not that every future bill must be fully funded in cash today. It is that the amount firmly promised becomes more reliable as the due date approaches.

== RUN THE DEMO VEHICLE GOAL ==

The demo household plans to replace a vehicle in 5 years and sets a $35,000 purchase ceiling.

It expects $10,000 from the current vehicle and $5,000 from cash flow in the purchase year.

That leaves $20,000 to accumulate in advance.

Without assuming growth, that is about $333 a month for 5 years. The household can use current assets, future contributions, or a combination.

The funding plan is built around the $20,000 gap, not the $35,000 headline price.

== THE BILL AND THE SOURCE ARE NOT TWO GOALS ==

The life event tells the projection when the expense occurs.

The account, holding, or contribution route tells the household where the money comes from.

Do not increase a Bridge target for the full bill, set aside the same dollars in a separate holding, and then treat both amounts as available elsewhere.

One bill gets one tracked funding source.

When the amount, date, or decision changes, update the life event and funding rule together.

== WHERE THIS NUMBER COMES FROM ==

For the funding gap:

**What it means:** the part of the commitment that still needs a source.

**Calculated from:** commitment minus current savings, future cash flow, expected proceeds, and deliberate financing.

**Edit source:** the life event and its assigned funding account or contribution plan.

**This affects:** current routing, Bridge funding, future withdrawals, confidence, and the earliest target-qualified date.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose which known costs belong in the baseline, the commitment amount, expected date, funding source, and de-risking point for each one.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

In Orange Plan, add the expected bill as a life event and identify the account or contribution route funding it. Record the de-risking rule outside or inside the app where the current build flow supports it.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

Every meaningful known cost has one commitment, one date, one funding source, and one rule for reducing volatility before the money is due.

# 02-4_optional-college-is-a-funding-stack.md

2.4 Optional: build the college funding stack
~5 min at 155 wpm · VOICE-MATCHED DRAFT — Austin review pending
============================================================

Watch this only if the household expects to help fund education. Otherwise Module 2 is complete without it.

Use the same future-cost method from the last lesson, then make one decision first: what is the family actually promising to provide?

== DEFINE THE FAMILY COMMITMENT ==

I would not begin with the published price of four years at a school the child may never attend.

The commitment might be:

- four years of in-state tuition,
- a fixed dollar amount,
- whatever is in the 529 plus an annual amount from cash flow,
- or a percentage of the final net cost.

The student may also contribute through work, scholarships, aid, or a deliberately limited amount of borrowing.

There is no universal family promise. Model the one the household has actually chosen.

== BUILD THE FUNDING STACK ==

College can be funded from several sources:

- current 529 savings,
- new contributions,
- parent cash flow while the student is enrolled,
- scholarships and grants,
- student or family contribution,
- a planned taxable or Bitcoin sale,
- and deliberately accepted borrowing.

The amount that needs to be accumulated in advance can be much lower than the four-year sticker price.

Our demo household commits to $80,000 total.

It already has $25,000 in the 529. The parents expect $20,000 from cash flow during the college years. The student is expected to cover $10,000 through work, aid, or a defined amount of borrowing.

That leaves $25,000 that still needs to be accumulated or funded from assets.

The goal is a credible $80,000 stack, not necessarily an $80,000 529 balance.

== REDUCE VOLATILITY AS COLLEGE GETS CLOSER ==

Use the same time lanes from 2.3.

When college is more than 7 to 10 years away, keep the broader plan moving and revisit the commitment as the student's options become clearer.

Around 3 to 7 years out, update the likely cost, current savings, aid assumptions, and the amount the family still intends to provide.

Inside the final 1 to 3 years, give at least the near-term committed payments a reliable source that does not require Bitcoin to be at a particular price.

Once the student is enrolled, replace old estimates with the real annual cost and aid package.

== BITCOIN AND BORROWING ARE SOURCES, NOT DEFAULTS ==

Bitcoin can be part of the stack when the horizon is long and the family has flexibility. Do not leave a fixed payment due next year fully dependent on a Bitcoin recovery.

Borrowing is a separate debt decision. Compare the payment, likely student income, parent-retirement impact, and alternatives under the current loan rules. Do not hide parent or private debt inside the college goal.

== WHERE THIS NUMBER COMES FROM ==

For the family funding gap:

**What it means:** the part of the family commitment still needing a source.

**Calculated from:** commitment minus current savings, expected cash flow, aid, student contribution, and accepted borrowing.

**Edit source:** the college life event, education holding, and contribution route.

**This affects:** contributions, Bridge funding, future withdrawals, taxes, confidence, and the earliest target-qualified date.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose the family commitment and the sources expected to cover it.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

In Orange Plan, model the household commitment as the life event, track the education savings and contribution route, and update the stack when the date and actual costs become clearer.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

The sources add to the family commitment and the amount due soon is protected from a forced Bitcoin sale.
