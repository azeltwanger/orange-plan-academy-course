The debt decision is not automatically “pay everything off as fast as possible.”

It is deciding what each balance is doing in the plan, what receives extra principal, and how much debt the household is willing to carry through a full Bitcoin cycle.

A low fixed-rate mortgage and a 20% credit card are not the same problem. Neither are a normal auto loan and a Bitcoin-backed loan that can liquidate collateral.

== VERIFY THE REAL TERMS ==

For each debt, confirm:

- current balance,
- rate,
- required payment,
- fixed or variable status,
- and remaining term or payoff date.

The debt row should own the payment so the app can remove it from future cash flow when the balance is paid off.

For a Bitcoin-backed loan, also gather pledged Bitcoin, current LTV, margin-call and liquidation levels, top-up and release rules, interest terms, and the lender's liquidation process. The detailed cushion analysis is Advanced; the baseline still needs the real loan.

== READ THE TWO PRESSURES ==

Orange Plan shows two ratios because debt creates two different kinds of pressure.

**Debt-to-income, or DTI**, is required monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income. It measures payment pressure.

**Debt-to-assets, or DTA**, is total debt divided by total assets. It measures balance-sheet pressure.

The current app uses these planning bands:

- DTI: healthy below 36%, caution from 36% through 43%, risk above 43%
- DTA: healthy below 30%, caution from 30% through 60%, risk above 60%

These are guideposts, not permission to borrow up to the line.

Income stability, reserve funding, time to retirement, and comfort with debt can justify a much lower household ceiling.

== RUN THE DEMO HOUSEHOLD ==

The demo household has a $280,000 mortgage and an $18,000 auto loan, for $298,000 of total debt.

Required payments are about $1,833 a month against gross monthly income of about $15,833. DTI is roughly 11.6%.

At the illustrative $100,000 Bitcoin price used for the starting balance sheet, gross assets are $745,000. DTA is about 40%.

Those results do not contradict each other.

The payments are comfortable relative to income, while debt is still meaningful relative to the assets.

DTA will also move when Bitcoin moves. The debt may barely change while the asset value falls or rises. That is why a lower ratio after a rally should not automatically become a reason to add debt.

== GIVE EACH DEBT A TREATMENT ==

Each balance receives one deliberate treatment:

- required payment only,
- recurring extra principal,
- planned lump-sum paydown or payoff,
- refinance when the real terms improve the plan,
- or monitor when a variable rate, collateral, or lender rule drives the risk.

Rate matters, but it does not decide everything by itself.

Paying down a high rate gives a guaranteed reduction in cost. Keeping a low fixed rate preserves liquidity and leaves money available for other goals. The middle requires a comparison of rate, payment pressure, reserve, alternatives, and how the household feels about the debt.

If the reserve is weak, I would usually protect the household before using all available cash for a payoff. Otherwise the next emergency can recreate the debt.

== APPLY THE TREATMENTS TO THE DEMO ==

The mortgage is fixed at 3.25% and the payment is comfortable.

The demo keeps the required payment. Paying it faster would move a large amount of liquid money into home equity for a guaranteed return equal to the low mortgage rate.

That can still be the right personal decision for someone who strongly values being debt-free. It is not the priority in this demo while other parts of the plan need funding.

The auto loan is 7% with an $18,000 balance.

The household has a fully funded $30,000 reserve and $4,000 of monthly surplus. It chooses $500 a month of recurring extra principal without using the reserve to wipe out the balance.

Module 4 will route that already-decided $500. It does not reopen the debt decision.

== SET THE HOUSEHOLD CEILING ==

The app shows where the current ratios sit. The household still needs its own line for a new borrowing decision.

Ask:

- What payment load remains manageable if income falls or costs rise?
- What debt relative to assets can we watch through a major Bitcoin drawdown without feeling forced to sell or panic-pay the debt?

The approved demo rule is:

- do not intentionally push DTI above 25%,
- and do not add debt when DTA is 40% or higher.

These are household rules for the fictional demo, not universal thresholds. They leave room below the app's outer caution and risk bands.

== BITCOIN-BACKED DEBT IS OPTIONAL ADVANCED WORK ==

If the learner does not have or actively consider a Bitcoin-backed loan, the core debt plan is complete without lender-capacity modeling.

When a loan exists, the treatment must include current LTV, top-up resources, liquidation behavior, lender risk, and the household's action before a margin call. Those details belong in the gated loan lesson.

== WHERE THESE NUMBERS COME FROM ==

### DTI

**What it means:** share of gross income committed to required debt payments.

**Calculated from:** monthly payments divided by gross monthly income.

**Edit source:** debt payment and income rows.

**This affects:** surplus, flexibility, and capacity to hold through a drawdown.

### DTA

**What it means:** total debt compared with current gross assets.

**Calculated from:** debt balances and asset values.

**Edit source:** debt and asset records.

**This affects:** financial-position read and the household's room to carry or add debt.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose the treatment for each debt, the actual extra-principal amount, and the household ceiling for future borrowing.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Verify each debt row and set only the recurring extra payment or lump sum the household has actually chosen. Read DTI and DTA from their source numbers and record the household ceiling with the plan.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

Every debt has a treatment and reason, extra payments do not weaken the reserve, and the household can state its ceiling before another borrowing decision appears.
