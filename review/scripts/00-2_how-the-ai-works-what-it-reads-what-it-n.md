0.2 Use Orange Plan AI to understand the numbers and make better decisions

============================================================

Orange Plan can calculate a lot of numbers.

The harder part for most people is knowing which number matters, why it changed, and what they should do with it.

That is where the AI is useful.

I think of it as a second set of eyes on the financial plan. It can read the current numbers, help you understand what is driving the result, compare two real choices, challenge an assumption, and turn the analysis into a next step.

The goal is not to have a chatbot repeat the page back to you. The goal is to get useful feedback on a decision.

== FIVE USEFUL WAYS TO USE IT ==

The first is to explain a number.

For example, our demo household has a full $4,000 monthly next-dollar decision, but Cash Flow shows $3,761 left after planned debt.

A useful question is:

“Why does Cash Flow show $3,761 when the household is putting $4,000 to work every month? Reconcile the numbers and tell me which amount is already included in Debt.”

A good answer should explain that $500 of extra auto principal is already inside planned debt payments, while $3,500 is routed to accounts. It should point back to the source instead of giving a generic explanation of budgeting.

The second use is to review the whole plan and prioritize what matters.

When you do not know where to begin, ask:

“Review my full plan. Rank the top three things that deserve attention by impact. Tell me what looks strong, what is holding the plan back, and what can wait.”

That is much more useful than asking, “Is my plan good?”

A full review should not give you twenty equal-priority tasks. It should find the one or two things that actually change the answer.

The third use is to compare a real decision.

You might compare paying extra on debt with funding the Bridge, keeping retirement spending at $100,000 with choosing a higher spending amount, or selling Bitcoin with borrowing against it.

Ask it to show what each choice buys, what it costs, which risks change, and which numbers in the plan move.

For example:

“Compare keeping our retirement paycheck at $100,000 with using the Balanced spending amount. Show the monthly difference, what happens to confidence and the retirement date, and what flexibility we would be giving up.”

The AI should not decide that more spending is automatically better because the app says the plan can support it. It should help you understand the trade-off so you can decide what the extra capacity is for.

The fourth use is to challenge the plan.

Do not use the AI only to confirm what you already want to do.

Ask:

“Which assumption is doing the most work in this result?”

“What would force us to sell Bitcoin during a major drawdown?”

“What is the strongest argument against this strategy?”

“What important risk am I not paying enough attention to?”

This is especially useful with a Bitcoin-heavy plan because a result can look strong while depending on one aggressive assumption, weak liquidity, a fragile debt structure, or a custody process the family cannot operate.

The fifth use is to turn the result into action.

After the explanation or comparison, ask:

“What is the one action to take now, what should I model next, and what can wait?”

That forces the answer to separate three different things:

- a real-life action,
- a question that needs another comparison,
- and cleanup that is not the main decision yet.

It can also help you prepare for a professional.

Instead of showing up to a CPA and saying, “My Bitcoin taxes are confusing,” ask the AI to summarize the known facts, the missing records, and the exact question the CPA needs to answer.

For the demo household, that could be:

“We own 1.75 Bitcoin. Basis is complete for 1.25, reconstruction is pending for 0.40, and 0.10 is still unknown. What records should we bring to the CPA, and which planning decisions should wait until the basis is resolved?”

== START BROAD OR START NARROW ==

There are two good ways to begin.

If you do not know what matters, start with **Review my full plan**. Let it rank the top priorities, then choose one area to work through.

If you already have a decision, start from the page that owns it.

Use Cash Flow for spending, reserve, and routing questions.

Use Debt for payoff order and leverage.

Use Allocation for the target, time horizons, and rebalancing.

Use Tax for basis, conversions, and tax windows.

Use Income for retirement spending, withdrawals, and sell-versus-borrow comparisons.

Use Scenarios when you already know the specific what-if you want to compare.

The focused reviews have the relevant page context. That means you do not need to explain the entire plan again every time.

== ASK FOR JUDGMENT, NOT A SUMMARY ==

A useful planning question has three parts:

1. The decision you are trying to make
2. The constraint that matters to you
3. What you want the answer to show

For example:

“I want to retire at 55 without making a Bitcoin-backed loan necessary. Use the current plan numbers. What is the biggest constraint, which input is driving it, and what is the next move?”

Or:

“We are above the Bitcoin allocation band, but we do not want to create a large taxable sale. Compare fixing the drift with new contributions versus selling today.”

When the answer could apply to anybody, ask again:

“Use my plan numbers. Show me the evidence, the trade-off, and the specific next action.”

== MAKE IT SHOW ITS WORK ==

A strong answer should give you four things:

- a direct verdict,
- the plan number or source supporting it,
- the trade-off,
- and the next action.

You can keep pressing on any part that is unclear.

“Where did that number come from?”

“Which input would I change?”

“What else moves if I change it?”

“Does this require a Scenario, or does the current plan already answer it?”

The AI is most useful when the conversation gets narrower and more specific, not when it turns into a long general lecture.

== USE SCENARIOS FOR A REAL QUESTION ==

Do not create Scenarios just to create more numbers.

First understand the current plan. Then use a Scenario when a real decision or uncertain assumption needs comparison.

Our demo household uses 3% inflation in the Baseline and tests 4% separately. The higher-inflation Scenario lowers confidence from 94.6% to 91.6%.

The useful AI question is not only, “What is the new percentage?”

It is:

“Why did confidence fall by 3 points, which future costs changed the most, and does this difference change the retirement decision?”

That turns the Scenario into judgment instead of another chart.

== THREE RULES ==

The first rule is that the app math wins. The AI explains, compares, and prioritizes from the plan. It should not replace a calculated app result with a second guessed number.

The second rule is no secrets. Never enter a seed phrase, private key, passphrase, PIN, password, wallet backup, full account number, Social Security number, or authentication code.

The third rule is to verify the current execution details when tax, legal, insurance, lending, or custody rules matter. The AI can help you understand the mechanism and prepare a much better question. The appropriate professional or provider confirms the final action.

Optional memory can be useful for planning preferences such as preserving Bitcoin, retiring sooner, or reducing stress. It is not where balances, credentials, account numbers, or recovery information belong.

Choose the first real planning question you want Orange Plan AI to help answer.

Write it using the decision, the constraint, and what you want the answer to show.

If you already know the decision, open the planning area that owns it and use the focused review.

Then ask one follow-up that challenges the first answer or traces the result back to the source.
