# A1 · Modeling and assumptions

## A1.1 · What changes across the 1,000 test runs?

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Why it earns a lesson:** Learners need to understand why confidence is useful without turning the course into Monte Carlo theory.

**Recommended judgment:**

- The built-in model is the working Baseline unless there is a reason to change it.
- A Scenario should change one meaningful assumption first.
- Comparable paths matter more than trying to predict the correct future.
- Do not let learners tune assumptions until they get the date they want.

**Example test:** The 3% versus 4% inflation comparison should remain the primary example because it isolates one input and produces a visible three-point confidence difference.

**Austin decision:** Does the lesson explain enough of the mechanics to earn trust without becoming technical for its own sake?

## A1.2 · When should a holding use its own assumptions?

**Verdict:** KEEP and keep short.

**Recommended judgment:**

- An override corrects a real modeling mismatch.
- It is not a way to rescue a disappointing result.
- A spot Bitcoin ETF should generally follow Bitcoin assumptions rather than a broad stock assumption.
- Most holdings should remain on the broad Plan defaults.

**Example test:** FBTC is the cleanest example because the security label and economic exposure differ.

# A2 · Bitcoin-backed borrowing and leverage

## A2.1 · What does a Bitcoin-backed loan change?

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Opening a loan adds cash and an equal liability; it does not create wealth on day one.
- There is no universal “normal” or “safe” starting LTV.
- The starting LTV should be judged against the signed action/liquidation terms, finite reserves, repayment plan, and household behavior.
- A large price cushion does not remove lender, custody, insolvency, or operational risk.

**Example test:** The $30,000 loan against 1.50 BTC clearly teaches the formula without recommending a real lender or threshold.

## A2.2 · Build the survival and repayment plan

**Verdict:** KEEP. This is the most important borrowing lesson.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Auto top-up is a tool, not proof the loan is safe.
- Top-up Bitcoin and cash paydown resources are finite.
- Those same assets cannot also be counted as emergency, tax, and spending reserves.
- Every loan needs a primary repayment source, backup source, action ladder, and hard exit.
- A repayment plan that requires Bitcoin to rise is a price bet, not a complete repayment plan.

**Example test:** The 0.45 BTC top-up or $6,923 paydown makes resource depletion visible.

## A2.3 · Compare selling Bitcoin with borrowing

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Compare the same cash need.
- Selling exchanges future Bitcoin exposure for tax and simplicity.
- Borrowing exchanges a current sale for interest, debt, collateral, liquidation, counterparty, repayment, and estate risk.
- Holding is not a funding source unless another real asset or income source provides the cash.
- The saved Core plan should work without borrowing unless the household deliberately accepts the full loan structure.

**Austin decision:** Should the final version use only the fictional $30,000 example, or add one short personal/client story without turning the course into a recommendation to borrow?

## A2.4 · Evaluate the lender and write the exit rules

**Verdict:** KEEP, but aggressively tighten.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Rate is not the first comparison.
- Start with who controls collateral, whether it can be reused, the legal claim on failure, price source, notice/cure, liquidation, release, and provider-independent exit.
- Refinancing is not a reliable emergency exit.
- Incapacity and death must be addressed before the loan is treated as a long-term strategy.

**Production treatment:** The spoken lesson should teach the decision framework. The redacted agreement walkthrough should carry clause-by-clause detail.

# A3 · Tax optimization and implementation

## A3.1 · Finish basis and execute lot identification

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Reconcile quantity before optimizing tax.
- Unknown basis stays visibly unknown.
- Prior filed treatment is not rewritten because another method now looks better.
- The lot modeled in Orange Plan matters only when the real sale is identified and documented under the current provider/wallet rules.

## A3.2 · Size a Roth conversion

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- “Fill the bracket” is a starting calculation, not an automatic recommendation.
- Compare no conversion, a smaller conversion, and the proposed ceiling.
- Include state tax, healthcare, Social Security, Medicare-related costs, estimated payments, five-year rules, and cash used to pay tax.
- The decision is a range for the current year, not a fixed conversion schedule for the next decade.

## A3.3 · Harvest gains or losses

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- A tax loss is useful only when the after-tax and exposure result improves the plan.
- A gain realization needs a reason, not merely an available low rate.
- Always compare no action and a smaller action.
- Repurchase and identification rules must be current and asset-specific.

## A3.4 · Evaluate state relocation

**Verdict:** KEEP, but keep concise and strongly conditional.

**Recommended judgment:**

- A possible move belongs in Scenarios; a committed move becomes a life event and later a new Baseline.
- Compare tax with housing, insurance, healthcare, travel, work, family, community, time, and residency evidence.
- Do not move for a small modeled tax difference that disappears after three years of higher life costs.

**Production treatment:** Detailed residency and sourcing rules belong in maintained reference and with the state professional.

# A4 · Early-retirement access and healthcare

## A4.1 · Map the account-access Bridge

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Access, income tax, and additional early-distribution tax are separate questions.
- Do not reduce every account to “locked until 59½.”
- A current-employer plan, Roth contribution basis, HSA, 72(t), governmental 457(b), or another account can have different rules.
- Do not roll a plan until any access option being preserved is understood.
- Every early year needs a primary and backup source.

## A4.2 · Model pre-Medicare healthcare

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Use current household quotes, not a national average.
- Premium is not the full cost.
- The withdrawal and conversion strategy can change the net healthcare cost.
- Health sharing is not insurance and should never be presented as equivalent because Austin personally uses it.
- The HSA is a qualified-healthcare source, not unrestricted Bridge spending.

**Austin decision:** How much of Austin's personal CrowdHealth experience belongs in the spoken example versus the written reference?

## A4.3 · Coordinate access, tax, and healthcare

**Verdict:** KEEP. It is the synthesis lesson, but tighten repetition.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Compare the same spendable-cash need across every strategy.
- The winning strategy must preserve the Bridge after tax and healthcare, not merely lower one year's tax.
- Use an annual funding range rather than a rigid decade-long order.
- Keep quote figures explicitly illustrative.

**Production treatment:** The table comparing the three $100,000 funding paths should do most of the teaching.

# A5 · Advanced custody

## A5.1 · Decide whether a passphrase solves a real problem

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- A passphrase is not the default next level after ordinary single signature.
- It should solve a named threat.
- It creates a second required component and a new permanent-loss path.
- Memory is never the only backup.
- A practice wallet and clean-device recovery come before changing the main wallet.

## A5.2 · Choose collaborative custody or DIY multisig

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Family recoverability matters more than technical status or ideological purity.
- Alex's ability to build DIY multisig does not prove Jordan can recover it.
- Collaborative custody is worth considering when provider-independent recovery works and guided family support earns the fee.
- Tested family-ready single signature can still be safer than unproven multisig.
- Never prescribe one universal owner/executor/provider key split.

## A5.3 · Migrate, operate, and prove recovery safely

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Never make the main balance the first test.
- Prove the destination with a practice wallet, small live tranche, spend from the destination, alternate recovery combination, staged transfer, and family practice.
- Do not destroy the old backup until all dependent wallets/accounts and change outputs are understood.
- UTXO consolidation is a fee/privacy decision, not automatic housekeeping.

# A6 · Trusts and complex estate planning

## A6.1 · Does a trust solve a real problem?

**Verdict:** KEEP.

**Recommended judgment:**

- Bitcoin ownership alone does not create a trust requirement.
- Start with the problem: incapacity, probate, minors/special needs, family control, privacy, business/multi-state property, creditor issue, or tax planning.
- Minor children trigger attorney design advice, not an automatic trust purchase.
- A revocable trust is generally about management/probate/privacy, not automatically estate-tax or creditor protection.
- “Irrevocable trust” is a category, not one strategy.
- “Trust not currently indicated; review after a trigger” is a complete result.

## A6.2 · Coordinate trust, beneficiaries, and custody

**Verdict:** KEEP, but treat most implementation as professional work.

**Recommended judgment:**

- A signed trust does not move an asset.
- Legal ownership, provider record, tax record, custody threshold, and tested access must agree.
- Possessing a key does not prove legal authority; legal authority cannot sign without a key path.
- Retirement accounts are not casually retitled to a living trust.
- The Academy teaches the ownership/authority matrix; attorney, CPA, provider, and custody practitioner execute it.

**Production treatment:** Keep the 1.50 BTC legal/technical mismatch as the worked example and move detailed state/tax implementation to the professional layer.

# Architecture conclusions

## Keep in Advanced

All 18 current lessons remain conditional Advanced material. None is required for every learner.

## Keep out of Core

- Monte Carlo mechanics beyond interpreting confidence
- Bitcoin-loan sizing, provider selection, and survival operations
- Lot-identification execution
- Conversion sizing
- Harvesting execution
- Relocation implementation
- Account-access exceptions
- Marketplace/MAGI optimization
- Passphrases and multisig implementation
- Trust design and funding

Core should identify the gate and prepare the question. Advanced works the conditional decision.

## Professional-only boundary

The following are never finally executed from the Academy script alone:

- specific lender agreement interpretation,
- tax-return positions or exact conversion/harvest amounts,
- Marketplace enrollment or Medicare timing,
- device/provider secret-recovery procedure,
- trust drafting, funding, tax treatment, or fiduciary authority,
- and provider acceptance of ownership, beneficiary, or estate records.
