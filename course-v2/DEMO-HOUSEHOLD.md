# Continuing fictional household — Alex and Morgan Reed

**Purpose:** one fictional household evolves through the entire core course so every lesson connects to the same plan. The names, numbers, accounts, debts, and family facts are invented. They are not Austin's family and are not copied from any client.

## Household

- Alex, age 44
- Morgan, age 42
- Two children, ages 13 and 9
- Work-optional target: Alex at 52
- Planning horizon: through age 95
- One stable W-2 income and one variable self-employed income
- High Bitcoin conviction, with different levels of technical comfort between spouses

## Current income and spending

- Alex W-2 gross income: $155,000
- Morgan self-employed gross income: $78,000, variable by year
- Normal living spending: $10,800 per month after income taxes and before required debt payments
- Bare-bones household spending: $7,200 per month
- Current monthly surplus: calculated during Session 2 after taxes and required debt payments are verified

The figures are teaching fixtures. The course uses them to show the mechanism, then the member applies the same process to their own numbers.

## Accounts and holdings

### Cash and flexible money

- Joint checking: $18,000
- High-yield savings / Reserve: $32,000
- Taxable brokerage: $115,000 in broad stock funds and short-term Treasury exposure

### Bitcoin exposure

- Direct Bitcoin in self-custody: 3.4 BTC with mixed purchase history
- Bitcoin at a professional custodian: 0.7 BTC
- Bitcoin ETF exposure inside a Roth IRA

The three forms of exposure let the course teach direct custody, professional support, account wrappers, tax treatment, and the risk of relying on one method.

### Retirement and special-purpose accounts

- Alex Traditional 401(k): $410,000
  - employer match: 50% of employee contributions up to 6% of pay
  - plan offers Traditional and Roth employee contributions
  - plan offers broad stock funds and a brokerage window with a Bitcoin ETF
- Morgan Solo 401(k): $95,000
- Alex Roth IRA: $145,000
- Morgan Traditional IRA: $82,000
- HSA: $41,000
- 529 education account: $58,000

These accounts create the decisions the course must teach: employer match, Traditional versus Roth, investment-menu limits, money needed before age 59½, tax diversification, and holdings inside each account.

## Home and debts

- Primary residence value: $590,000
- Fixed mortgage: $318,000 at 3.25%
- HELOC balance: $46,000 at a variable rate, currently interest-only
- Auto loan: $29,000 at 6.7%
- Credit-card balance from a past business slowdown: $13,500 at 20.9%
- Small business equipment loan: $38,000 at 7.4%
- Unused HELOC capacity remains available
- No current Bitcoin-backed loan in the saved baseline

The course later models a Bitcoin-backed loan, home-equity financing, and cash as alternatives for a large purchase or asset acquisition. None is treated as the automatic answer.

## Expected life events

- College support begins in five years and lasts four years
- A vehicle replacement is expected in three years
- A home renovation is being considered in two years
- Morgan's income may drop during the first retirement years
- Healthcare must be funded before Medicare
- Social Security begins later than the work-optional date

Expected events belong in the saved plan. A larger renovation, a move, a business acquisition, or borrowing to buy more Bitcoin may be tested as a scenario until the household actually commits.

## Planning tensions used throughout the course

1. The household wants to keep meaningful Bitcoin exposure and avoid a forced sale.
2. The Reserve is below the final target.
3. The high-rate card is clearly damaging, while the low-rate mortgage may deserve to remain.
4. The HELOC creates both flexibility and risk because the home secures it.
5. The work-optional date comes before age 59½, so accessible Bridge funding matters.
6. A large Traditional balance creates a future tax-planning window.
7. The 401(k) offers both a match and a path to Bitcoin exposure, making the next-dollar decision realistic rather than obvious.
8. The spouses need a custody and family-access system both can explain.
9. The plan must compare selling Bitcoin, borrowing, and using other assets during retirement.
10. The final plan must be simple enough to explain in one family conversation.

## Course arc

- **Session 1:** turn the scattered facts into the first working plan.
- **Session 2:** verify cash flow, size the Reserve, and enter expected life events.
- **Session 3:** eliminate the high-rate fragility, decide what to do with every other debt, and set household leverage rules.
- **Session 4:** set the whole-plan allocation and route every new dollar through the right account into the intended holdings.
- **Session 5:** clean basis and identify the low-income tax window.
- **Session 6:** build the retirement paycheck and compare selling, borrowing, and holding.
- **Session 7:** choose an intentional custody mix and prove the direct-custody recovery.
- **Session 8:** build the family handoff without storing secrets.
- **Session 9:** establish the monthly and annual review rhythm.
- **Session 10:** read, explain, and save the completed plan.

## Editorial rule

The continuing household demonstrates the process. It never becomes a recommendation that the member copy the exact allocation, debt level, account mix, healthcare path, custody method, or retirement strategy.
