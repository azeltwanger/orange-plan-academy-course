# Austin custody decision-framework notes — 2026-08-26

These notes came from Austin's review of the custody teaching after a client conversation. They are **Austin direction and planning judgment**, not a line-by-line dictation transcript.

## The problem with the old shape

The old progression implied:

**Exchange → hardware wallet → passphrase → multisig / collaborative custody.**

That reads like a wealth ladder: as the amount grows, the correct answer becomes one increasingly advanced self-custody setup.

Austin's actual position is more mature:

- Every custody method has trade-offs.
- Self-custody protects against provider failure and loss of direct control, but creates operational, physical, and family-recovery risk.
- Institutional custody gives up direct control, but can provide support, simpler administration, and a legal or contractual process.
- Once the amount becomes life-changing, concentration in one custody method becomes a risk of its own.
- An intentional combination of direct self-custody, collaborative multisig, and institutional custody can be valid.
- The decision depends partly on how strongly the household values direct control and which risks it is willing to own.

## Central teaching position

> **Every custody method protects you from one type of failure by making you responsible for another.**

> **The goal is not perfect custody. The goal is to make sure one mistake, one company, one device, or one bad day cannot destroy the family's plan.**

## Preserve

- Custody is not a purity test.
- Document the process. Never document the secrets.
- Name the job of the Bitcoin before deciding how it should be held.
- Prove recovery before meaningful money depends on a wallet.
- Find the top single point of failure and fix one thing at a time.
- The setup must be maintainable, explainable, and recoverable by the family.

## Required changes

1. Replace the wealth ladder with readiness outcomes:
   - accounts secured;
   - direct custody proven;
   - family continuity established;
   - catastrophic concentration removed.
2. Put institutional custody in the main trade-off comparison.
3. Make direct-control preference explicit.
4. Never say multisig has no remaining single point of failure. State the exact failure it removes and what can still fail.
5. Add one custody method and one provider to the single-point-of-failure hunt.
6. Use the One-Failure Test:
   - Could one lost seed, frozen account, provider failure, home disaster, coercion event, or incapacity materially damage the family plan?
7. Use rough scale rather than exact balances on the worksheet:
   - replaceable;
   - meaningful;
   - life-changing.
8. End the lesson with one custody direction and one next action, not a large implementation list.
9. Keep vendor-specific mechanics in versioned implementation guides and exact-device demonstrations, not the evergreen decision lesson.

## Final teaching position

Self-custody matters because it gives direct control. Direct control is not the only risk that matters. As Bitcoin becomes a larger part of the family's wealth, the job is to balance sovereignty, recovery, support, recourse, and simplicity, then make sure no single failure can take out too much of the plan.
