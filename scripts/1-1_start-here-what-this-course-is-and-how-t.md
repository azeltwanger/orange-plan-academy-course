Welcome to the course. In this first lesson, I'm going to cover what this course is, what you're going to walk out with, and how to get the most out of it.

It took me 4 years of holding Bitcoin before I realized I was missing something. I had the asset figured out. What I didn't have was a plan.

== WHAT FREEDOM ACTUALLY IS ==

When I think about freedom, I'm not thinking about material things. I'm thinking about the number you hit where no one can tell you what to do anymore. The space to say no to things you don't want to do, the room to take risks and pursue new ideas, and the time to spend with the people that you care about.

You get one life. And most people are going to work their entire life for maybe 15 years of freedom at the end, if they're lucky. Bitcoin gives us a much better shot at a full life where you actually have a choice. But before you get there, you have to decide how much you need to reach that level. That's what this course is going to help you figure out.

== WHY I BUILT THIS ==

I spent years working in finance, and honestly, I got jaded with how the industry worked. Overpriced advice to manage index funds and bonds, and commissions on insurance products that clients didn't need.

When I found Bitcoin, it solved the investment side for me. But it created a new problem. I was checking the price 20 times a day. I was stressed on the down days and euphoric on the up days. Holding a significant portion of my net worth in Bitcoin was way harder than it needed to be.

So I dug into my own numbers. I needed to know the exact date that I could safely live on Bitcoin in retirement. The problem was that the tools that existed were just basic calculators — growth rate in, spending out. None of them answered the real questions. What about taxes? What about the house we want in a few years? Which accounts should I be contributing to? Should I sell Bitcoin or borrow against it?

I knew from my background that there are dozens of variables you have to solve to get an accurate retirement date. So I built a system that does it. It models every income, expense, debt, asset, life event, and tax liability. It runs simulations on how likely your plan is to succeed, and it tells you how much you can spend. That system has given me a level of confidence around my family's finances that I hadn't felt since finding Bitcoin. And this course is the roadmap that walks you through building yours.

== WHAT YOU'RE GOING TO WALK OUT WITH ==

You're going to walk out with a plan that answers one question in one line: you can stop working at age X, and the plan holds up in Y percent of futures.

And then it keeps answering it. When you get a raise, the date moves. When Bitcoin rips, the date moves. When your spending drifts in a good year, the date moves. This is a living document that you check in with, not a spreadsheet you build once and never open again.

== HOW THE COURSE IS STRUCTURED ==

The course is 10 modules, and they're in the order that I would take a real client through. You're going to start with your baseline — your numbers, your assumptions, and your first confidence read. Then cash flow, which is your surplus and how to protect it. Then where the money goes, which is allocation and debt. Then how you use it, which is tax strategy and retirement income. Then how you protect it, with custody and estate planning. And then how you keep the whole thing alive, which only takes about 10 to 15 minutes a month.

Each module has two kinds of lessons. Teach lessons, like this one, explain the concept with a real worked example. And walkthroughs put it into the app step by step. Some modules also include worksheets, and for the sensitive ones, like the custody map and the heir letter, I'd recommend printing those and filling them out with pen and paper.

Go in order the first time. After that, you can come back to any lesson as your life changes.

== THE RULE THE WHOLE COURSE RUNS ON ==

Rough and honest beats precise and late.

A plan built on real numbers that are approximately right, this week, beats a perfect plan a year from now. The most common failure mode I see is people spending 3 months reconstructing their exact cost basis before they ever run their first confidence check. Don't do that. Get the plan standing first, and refine after.

I think a module a week is a good pace when you're starting. I wouldn't binge them back to back — each one ends with a decision or an action, and the plan builds on itself one piece at a time.

== BEFORE THE FIRST MODULE ==

Before you start the first module, have these three things in front of you:

1. Roughly what your household income is in a month.
2. Roughly what you spend in a month.
3. A list of what you own and what you owe — including how much Bitcoin, and where it's held.

Rough is fine. The first module walks you through gathering all of it accurately.
