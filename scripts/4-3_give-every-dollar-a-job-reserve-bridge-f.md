4.3 Give every dollar a job: Reserve / Bridge / Legacy

In today's lesson, we're going to cover the 3 buckets that give every dollar you own a job.

Every dollar sits in one of 3 buckets, and the bucket tells you three things: what that money is for, how it should behave, and where it should sit.

== THE 3 BUCKETS ==

The table on the screen lays this out, but let me talk you through each one.

The first bucket is the Reserve. This is your zero-to-three-year money, and it needs to be low volatility. High-yield savings, T-bills, short-term Treasuries, money market. Nothing that moves. The Reserve's job is to protect your ability to hold Bitcoin without being forced to sell it in a bad month. We sized this in the reserve lesson.

The second bucket is the Bridge. This is your four-to-ten-year money, and moderate volatility is fine here. Taxable brokerage, an HSA, a 529, I-Bond ladders, a balanced mix of stocks and bonds. The Bridge is your accessible, flexible capital for things that come up before retirement, and it's also the funding lane for known dated costs that are 4 to 10 years out.

The third bucket is Legacy. This is your ten-plus-year money, and this is where the high-growth, high-volatility assets live. Bitcoin, in appropriate custody. Your retirement accounts, the 401(k), the IRA, the Roth. Business equity you're holding long-term. At this timeframe, volatility is a feature, not a bug, because you have the time to ride out the swings.

== MATCHING EACH DOLLAR TO A BUCKET ==

Every account you own goes into one of the three. Let me show you how the couple lands.

Their $30,000 of cash is Reserve. That's the liquid safety net. Their $15,000 invested HSA is Bridge, because it's accessible before 65 and spendable in retirement. The $50,000 taxable brokerage is Bridge, earmarked for planned spending 5 to 8 years out. The $25,000 in the 529 is Bridge, because it's dated tuition money, 8 years out. Their $175,000 of Bitcoin on the hardware wallet is Legacy. That's the long-term core. And the $90,000 across the 401(k) and IRA is Legacy too, because they're 15-plus years from retirement.

Every dollar has a job. There's nothing sitting around waiting for someone to figure it out.

== THE REFILL RULE ==

Now, these buckets aren't static. The Bridge and the Reserve get drained by life, and Legacy gets built over time. So there's a rule for refilling, and it matters most in retirement.

The rule is: refill from the bucket that's up. Never from the bucket that's down.

So if stocks are up 15% and Bitcoin is down 60%, you refill the Bridge by trimming stocks. Not by selling Bitcoin. That's exactly how you avoid selling Bitcoin at a bad time just to fund your life.

And if everything is down at once, in a coordinated drawdown, you draw from the Reserve. That's literally what it exists for. The Reserve is the shock absorber that keeps the refill rule alive when nothing is up.

== SET A TARGET PERCENTAGE FOR EACH BUCKET ==

Each bucket gets two numbers: the current balance, and a target, which is the percentage of your net worth it should be.

For a working, Sovereign-tier household, something like 5% Reserve, 15% Bridge, and 80% Legacy can make sense. As you approach retirement, that shifts. Something like 10% Reserve, because you want a bigger buffer against sequence risk, 30% Bridge, and 60% Legacy.

The target is what the projection actually uses. When you change it, the plan re-solves. And then your job is just to route new dollars and refill decisions toward the target.

== COMMON MISTAKES ==

There are three mistakes I see over and over here.

The first is Bitcoin in the Bridge. Bitcoin belongs in Legacy. If you're planning to spend your Bitcoin inside of 10 years, then either the bucket is wrong or the plan is wrong, and you need to figure out which.

The second is cash in Legacy. Cash has no place in a ten-plus-year bucket, because inflation is eating it the entire time. A 401(k) sitting in a stable-value fund is exactly this mistake: it's Bridge risk sitting in a Legacy slot.

And the third is a Reserve that's too small in retirement. Once you're drawing income instead of earning it, the Reserve is doing more work. Its job in retirement is sequence-risk protection, not just emergencies.
