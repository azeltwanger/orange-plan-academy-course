# CHAPTER B — Beneficiaries and the executor

---

## □ B1 · Assign beneficiaries

**DO** **Protect → Needs attention → Beneficiaries row → Assign**

**SEE** Each name, relationship, allocation percentage, and the approximate dollars of the estate

**⚠ Say the rule at this exact moment: for an account with a valid beneficiary, POD, or TOD designation, the provider's form generally controls instead of the will.** Insurance policies work the same way. This is the cheapest fix in the module and the one most often skipped — and it isn't finished until it's confirmed with the provider, not just entered here.

**SEE** Read the panel's own copy out loud: Orange Plan holds the *intent*, and you mirror it in each account's own beneficiary designation, because **the institution holds the binding record**.

**⚠** Do not let this look finished when only the app is filled in. The app is your checklist; the custodian's form is the legal fact.

## □ B2 · The executor, off-app, with the actual person

**DO** Hold up the printed **Executor Packet**. This step does not happen on a screen

**SEE** Walk it section by section, about 30 minutes in real life: their role · their first week · the team · the Bitcoin process · asset handling · the acceptance page

**⚠** The Bitcoin process page is four nevers: never type or share recovery words, never accept unsolicited help, never move coins before the tax order is settled, never improvise.

**⚠ The acceptance page is the point.** An executor who signed it has been briefed and knows where the documents live. An executor who didn't sign hasn't been briefed at all.

**DO** Show the three toolkit documents together in one folder: **Executor Packet · Heir Letter · Family Custody Map**. Say that the executor knows where that folder is.

# CHAPTER D — The heir letter

---

## □ D1 · Contacts first

**DO** **Protect → Needs attention → Heir letter row → Finish**

**SEE** Four status rows: **Emergency contacts** (Added/Missing) · **Executor** (Named/Missing) · **Letter content** (Written/Missing) · **PDF saved** (Yes/Not yet)

**DO** **Emergency contacts → Edit** → **Add another contact** → **Save contacts**

**⚠ The executor you actually asked goes in here.** An executor who hasn't been told can't do the job. If the conversation hasn't happened, don't fill this row yet — and say that on camera.

## □ D2 · Write it, with the rule on screen

**DO** Any **Edit** link → dialog **Edit heir letter**

**SEE** **Read the red banner verbatim.** It is the best script in the app:

> *"NEVER include seed phrases, private keys, passwords, or PINs in this letter. It may be read by multiple people. Only describe what exists and who to contact."*

**DO** Work the three sections in order:

- **Key contacts** — Executor · Estate Attorney · CPA / Tax Advisor · Bitcoin Advisor
- **Where to begin** — its own guardrail: *"Never list where keys or recovery material are stored."* Describe where the **documents** live
- **Your letter** — the main textarea. **The first two lines from the lesson go here**: the first phone call, and the first thing not to do

**⚠ Key contacts are NOT emergency contacts.** Emergency contacts are the switch's recipients. Key contacts are the letter's professionals. Both lists are required, and people conflate them.

**⚠** Say why "Letter content" may still read Missing: template text doesn't count until you customize it.

## □ D3 · AI — Draft with AI

**DO** **Heir letter panel → Heir letter assistant → Draft with AI**

**SEE** Its own guardrail line: *"Uses your saved plan inventory and asks one question at a time. No seed words, passwords, or backup contents."*

**DO** Click, then stop typing while it drafts. Read one line back and say whether it's true for your family.

**⚠ When to run it:** when the blank page is what's stopping you. It drafts the inventory and the call order, never the secrets. **The first two lines are still yours to write.**

## □ D4 · Save the artifact

**DO** **PDF saved row → Download PDF**. Toast: *"Heir letter PDF downloaded."*

**DO** Check **Letter downloaded or printed**

**SEE** Footer: *"store it with your important documents. Separate from your seed phrases and hardware wallets."*

# CHAPTER G — The trust gate (optional, most households stop above)

---

## □ G1 · Run the gate, then stop or continue

**⚠ Say the correction out loud, because people expect a net-worth number here.** Net worth is *an* input, not the gate. A family with $400,000, two young kids, a business and a blended household can need more planning than a single person with $3 million and a simple life. Run the triggers, not the balance.

**DO** Read the trigger list and count how many are true

**⚠ The triggers:** a blended family · minor children · multiple heirs · business ownership · heavy Bitcoin concentration · probate or privacy concerns · incapacity planning · assets or property in more than one state · an advanced custody setup somebody has to operate after you.

**⚠ Then place yourself on the four levels, and say the question the level actually answers: which level deserves a professional conversation?**

1. **Basic cleanup** — beneficiary forms, an executor asked out loud, a letter. No attorney strictly required.
2. **Local estate plan** — will, POA, healthcare directive, drafted by an attorney licensed in your state.
3. **Trust or coordinated plan** — a trust, coordinated with the custody design and the CPA.
4. **Advanced estate, tax, and custody planning** — specialists, plural, working together.

**⚠ Say plainly that most households land at 1 or 2 and are finished.** A will, current beneficiary forms, a briefed executor, a letter, and an armed switch is a complete estate plan for the large majority.

**⚠ And say that "no" is a finished answer.** *"A basic estate plan is sufficient right now. A trust is not currently indicated."* That's a completed decision, not an open item. Write it down with the date, because next year you're checking whether a trigger changed, not starting over.

**⚠** If the gate does light up, point at **Advanced Estate Planning → A8.1**, and say the one thing that lesson exists for: the **prudent-investor duty can force a trustee to sell the exact asset the trust was built to hold**, and the waiver that prevents it has to be drafted in deliberately.

---

## □ G2 · The family handoff review

**⚠** Last thing, and it isn't in the app. The documents don't work if the family has never heard about them. Book a sitting with whoever would actually have to act.

**⚠ Say what this meeting is not: it is not where secrets get revealed.** No seed words, no PINs, no passphrases, no device handling. It's where the family learns what exists and how to start.

**⚠ The agenda, seven items:**

1. Why this plan exists
2. Who to contact first
3. Where the legal documents live
4. Where the no-secrets access map lives
5. What not to do
6. What is still confusing
7. Who owns each follow-up

**⚠** Item 6 is the one that earns the meeting. If nobody says anything is confusing, you asked wrong. Item 7 is what keeps it from being a conversation everybody forgets.
