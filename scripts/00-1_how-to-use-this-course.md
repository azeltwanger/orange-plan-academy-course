So in this lesson I'm going to cover what this course is, what you're going to build, and the best way to work through it without getting overwhelmed.

== WHY I BUILT THIS ==

It took me about four years of holding Bitcoin before I realized that I was missing something.

I understood the investment side. I knew why I wanted to own Bitcoin, and I had a high level of confidence in where it was going over the long term. But I did not have a financial plan where I could see everything in one place and understand how all of the pieces worked together.

My background is in financial planning, and I knew there were a lot of variables that had to be included if I wanted a useful answer.

How much are we actually spending? When do the debts get paid off? What taxes are going to be due when Bitcoin gets sold? What happens when Social Security starts? Which accounts should we contribute to? What about a new house, college, healthcare, or another large cost in the future? And how does all of that affect the date when work becomes optional?

Most of the tools I found either treated Bitcoin like a normal stock portfolio or reduced the entire plan to one growth rate and one spending number.

So I built Orange Plan to bring the full financial plan together while still accounting for the things that are specific to Bitcoin holders.

The app is going to model your income, spending, debts, assets, life events, taxes, retirement income, and the strategies you save. Then it puts the plan through 1,000 test runs so you can see how often the money lasted as long as you planned.

The app does the math. This course is going to help you understand the math, make the decisions the app cannot make for you, and put those decisions into a plan you can actually use.

== WHAT YOU'RE GOING TO WALK OUT WITH ==

By the end, you should be able to answer a few questions clearly.

When could I retire at the confidence target I chose?

How much does the plan need to support every year?

What needs to happen with my cash flow, reserve, debt, contributions, and allocation before I get there?

Where is the retirement paycheck going to come from?

How are taxes going to affect the plan?

And can my family access and inherit everything if something happens to me?

You are also going to understand where the important numbers came from.

If the app shows a monthly surplus, you should know which income, tax, spending, and debt numbers produced it.

If the retirement date moves, you should know whether it was spending, an assumption, a life event, a contribution, or another decision that changed it.

If a number looks wrong, you should know where the source is instead of trying to fix the output until it looks better.

That was one of the biggest things I heard while working with clients. People could see the number, but they did not always know why it was there. So throughout the course, we're going to keep answering four questions: what does the number mean, what is it calculated from, where do you edit the source, and what else does it affect?

== HOW THE COURSE IS STRUCTURED ==

There is a Start Here module and then nine planning modules.

Module 1 is your baseline. We're going to gather the information, separate the real plan from life events and scenarios, set the assumptions, choose a confidence target, and read the first retirement result.

Module 2 is cash flow and reserve. We're going to find what you actually spend, what you could cut down to in a bad period, what is left over every month, and how much cash should protect the plan.

Module 3 is debt. Every debt is going to get a deliberate treatment, and you're going to set the amount of debt you are comfortable carrying through a full Bitcoin cycle.

Module 4 is allocation and the next dollar. We're going to decide how much Bitcoin you can actually hold through a major drawdown, what each account is for, and where new contributions should go.

Module 5 is tax strategy. We're going to organize cost basis, understand the taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth parts of the plan, and identify the tax decisions that are relevant now versus later.

Module 6 is retirement income. We're going to confirm spending, build the income floor, calculate the part the portfolio has to cover, choose the withdrawal strategy, and set the rules that adjust spending over time.

Module 7 is custody. We're going to match the custody setup to the amount you hold, prove that recovery works, and remove the largest single points of failure.

Module 8 is estate and inheritance. We're going to connect the legal documents, the people, and the custody process so your family has a clear place to start.

And Module 9 is maintenance. We're going to set a quick monthly pass, one full annual review, learn how to test a decision in Scenarios, and read the finished report.

The core course is the work required to build a usable plan.

There is also an Advanced Library. Those lessons are for situations that do not apply to everybody, like Bitcoin-backed borrowing, Roth conversion optimization, a healthcare bridge before Medicare, complex custody, or trusts.

Every advanced lesson is going to tell you who it is for. If the condition does not apply to your plan, skip it. Finishing the core course does not require you to become an expert in every edge case.

== HOW THE LESSONS AND THE APP WORK TOGETHER ==

Each planning area has two parts.

The first part is the education. I'm going to explain the concept, walk through the trade-offs, and show you how I think about the decision.

The second part is the walkthrough. I'm going to use one demo household through the whole course so the numbers carry forward instead of changing every lesson. You'll see us make the decision in the demo account, explain what changed, and then complete the related work in Orange Plan.

The walkthroughs are going to track the Build Your Plan flow in the app. The course modules do not have to copy every app step exactly, because sometimes a concept is easier to understand in a different order. But the implementation should always connect back to the work the app is asking you to complete.

The checkmark in the app tells you the required data is there. The module is finished when the decision is also made and you can explain it.

== HOW TO WORK THROUGH IT ==

I would go in order the first time.

You do not need every number to be perfect before you start. Use the best numbers you can support today, get the first plan standing, and then refine it as you go.

The mistake I do not want you to make is spending three months trying to reconstruct every transaction before you have ever seen the plan. A rough number that is clearly marked as rough can be improved. A plan you never build cannot help you make a decision.

At the same time, do not knowingly guess at a number just because it gives you a result you like. If you do not know cost basis, leave it unknown and start gathering records. If spending is still a rough estimate, say that. The point is to know which inputs are solid and which ones still need work.

A module a week is a reasonable pace, but there is no deadline built into this. Watch the concept lessons, review the lesson text, complete the walkthrough, and then make the same decision in your own plan.

Some modules include worksheets. For custody and inheritance, the documents are going to describe the process without containing any secrets. Seed phrases, private keys, passphrases, PINs, and passwords never go into Orange Plan, a worksheet, a photo, an email, or an AI tool.

== US AND NON-US USERS ==

One thing to say once up front: the course is built around US financial-planning rules.

The tax module uses US brackets and account types. The estate module discusses US legal documents and probate. Parts of retirement income use Social Security, Medicare, and US retirement-account rules.

The broader planning work still applies outside the US: cash flow, reserve sizing, debt, allocation, Bitcoin custody, planning assumptions, retirement projections, and decision testing.

Orange Plan currently models in US dollars and uses US federal and state tax logic. So if you live outside the US, you can still build the plan and use the framework, but you will need a qualified professional in your country to map the tax accounts, public benefits, and legal documents to your local rules.

== A QUICK DISCLAIMER ==

This course is financial education. It is not a replacement for a tax professional, estate attorney, insurance professional, or another licensed professional who is responsible for your specific situation.

The app is also a modeling tool. It runs projections from the information and assumptions you give it. The result is not a guarantee, and it is not a prediction that every year is going to happen in the order shown.

Use the course to get organized, understand the trade-offs, make better decisions, and show up to professional conversations with a much clearer picture of your plan.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Your decision is how you're going to work through the course and where you're going to keep the information you still need to gather.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

You understand that the app does the calculations, the course teaches the decisions, and the walkthroughs connect those decisions to Build Your Plan.

You also know the one security rule before you enter anything: no Bitcoin secret ever goes into the app, a document, or an AI tool.
