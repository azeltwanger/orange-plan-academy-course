> **Watch this only if the household is genuinely considering a move or expects a large taxable event that makes state residence material. Otherwise keep the current state in the Baseline.**

A state-tax difference can be large.

It is still not the whole moving decision.

The useful question is: **after tax, housing, insurance, work, family, residency, and the years the household expects to stay, does the move improve the life and the plan?**

== START WITH A REAL DECISION DATE ==

Do not change the Baseline state because another state has no individual income tax today.

Create a Scenario with:

- expected move date,
- destination state,
- housing change,
- income change,
- spending change,
- property purchase or sale,
- and the taxable event being compared.

The state affects more than one tax line, and the move affects more than tax.

== WORK AN ILLUSTRATIVE GAIN ==

Assume the household expects to realize a $500,000 long-term gain after a legitimate move.

In a purely illustrative comparison, a 4% state tax on the gain would be $20,000 while a 0% state income-tax result would be $0.

That $20,000 is not a promise about Colorado or another named state. The current state law, sourcing, deductions, local rules, residency, and type of income control the actual result.

The example only shows why the question can matter.

Now add the rest of the move:

- housing costs $12,000 more per year,
- insurance and property tax cost $4,000 more,
- travel back to family costs $4,000,
- and the household expects to stay 3 years.

The extra non-income-tax cost is about $60,000 over those 3 years before moving costs.

A $20,000 one-time tax difference does not pay for that move.

If the tax difference repeats, housing is lower, work improves, or the household already wants the destination, the answer can change.

== RESIDENCY HAS TO BE REAL ==

Buying a mailbox, changing one account address, or spending a few weeks elsewhere does not automatically change domicile or sourcing.

States can look at home, time, work, family, licenses, registration, voting, business ties, property, and intent.

Some income can remain sourced to the former state or another state even after a move.

The state CPA or attorney supplies the departure and arrival checklist. The course does not provide a universal number of days or one proof that works everywhere.

== TIME THE TAX EVENT AFTER THE LEGAL MOVE ==

When a move and a large sale are connected, sequencing matters.

The household should know:

- when old residency ends,
- when new residency begins,
- which state can tax the income,
- what records prove the change,
- and whether a sale, conversion, business event, or employer income follows different sourcing rules.

Do not execute the taxable event from a planning Scenario without professional confirmation.

== COMPARE THE FULL PLAN ==

Read both states across:

- current and retirement tax,
- housing and insurance,
- healthcare,
- wages or business income,
- estate and inheritance rules,
- travel and family support,
- retirement timing,
- and quality of life.

A lower state-tax estimate can improve confidence and ending assets while producing a worse life. The model cannot choose that trade-off for the household.

== WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM ==

### State-tax difference

**What it means:** change in modeled state tax between the Baseline and relocation Scenario.

**Calculated from:** state, income type, amount, year, and current app tax rules.

**Edit source:** Scenario state, move date, income, and taxable event.

### Net move effect

**What it means:** tax difference plus the financial effects of housing, insurance, work, healthcare, and other changed costs.

**This affects:** surplus, withdrawals, confidence, earliest date, and estate.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose whether the move is real enough to model, the date and assumptions to compare, and the residency and tax questions that need professional confirmation.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Keep the current state in the Baseline. Build one relocation Scenario with the full life change, not merely a different tax field.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

You can explain the tax difference, total move cost, years needed for the decision to pay off financially, and why the move still fits the household's life.
