A1.2 When should a holding use its own return or income assumptions?

> **Watch this only if one holding in your plan would be modeled incorrectly by its broad asset-class default. Otherwise leave the holding on the Plan assumption and move on.**

The question is not, “Can I create a custom return for every asset?”

You can.

The useful question is: **does this specific holding genuinely behave differently from the broad class Orange Plan is using?**

Most of the time, the answer is no. I would leave most holdings on the Plan default.

== THE PLAN ASSUMPTION IS THE DEFAULT ==

The Plan assumptions set the broad view for Bitcoin, stocks, bonds, cash, real estate, and other assets.

That keeps the model understandable. If every holding has its own private growth story, nobody can explain what is driving the retirement result.

A holding override is there to correct a mismatch, not to create a better forecast.

== WORK THE BITCOIN ETF MISMATCH ==

This came up in a client review with FBTC.

The account record identified the holding as an ETF. If the app treats the word ETF as the same thing as a broad stock fund, that position can inherit the stock return assumption.

But the economic exposure is Bitcoin.

If the household owns $100,000 of FBTC and the model grows it like a diversified stock holding while the direct Bitcoin uses the Power Law view, the plan contains two different return models for essentially the same underlying exposure.

The fix is not a custom optimistic return.

The fix is to make the holding follow the Plan's Bitcoin assumptions.

Now the current allocation, drawdown exposure, and future projection all describe the same asset consistently.

== RETURN AND INCOME ARE DIFFERENT ==

A second common mistake is treating growth and cash yield as one number.

A rental property can appreciate in value and also produce rental income.

A bond fund can have a value return and an income yield.

A business interest may grow while distributing cash irregularly.

The return assumption changes the future value of the holding. The income assumption creates cash flow that may be spent, taxed, or reinvested.

Entering an 8% return and then another 8% yield because the investment “earns 8%” doubles the economic result.

== USE A PERIOD ONLY WHEN THE HOLDING REALLY CHANGES ==

Some holdings genuinely have stages.

A private note may pay interest for 5 years and then mature.

A rental may produce income until the planned sale date.

A business distribution may end when the owner retires.

A custom period can represent that real contract or decision.

Do not use periods to give Bitcoin 35% for the first 5 years, then change the next band every time the retirement date disappoints you. A declining return view belongs in the broad Bitcoin model unless the holding has a real separate condition.

== THE THREE-QUESTION OVERRIDE TEST ==

Before adding an override, ask:

1. **Classification:** is the holding attached to the wrong broad asset class?
2. **Economics:** does it have a documented return, income, or end date that the broad class cannot represent?
3. **Materiality:** would correcting the mismatch meaningfully change a planning decision?

If none apply, leave the default.

If one applies, document the source and the date the assumption should be reviewed.

== WHAT TO CHECK AFTER THE CHANGE ==

A holding override can move more than the future value.

It can change:

- the household asset mix,
- projected income,
- taxable cash flow,
- retirement confidence,
- the earliest target-qualified date,
- and the amount attributed to a holding in a future withdrawal.

After the change, ask whether those downstream results moved in the direction you expected.

If an FBTC correction increases Bitcoin exposure but the Allocation page does not change, the classification may still be wrong somewhere else.

== WHERE THE NUMBER COMES FROM ==

**What it means:** the return and cash-income behavior assigned to one holding rather than its broad asset-class default.

**Calculated from:** the saved override periods, rates, and income settings for that holding.

**Edit source:** the holding's advanced projection assumptions.

**This affects:** future value, cash income, tax, allocation, confidence, and withdrawal sources.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Identify the holdings that truly fail the three-question test and leave every other holding on the broad Plan assumption.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Open the holding's advanced projection assumptions, select the correct broad model or enter only the documented override, and record the source and review date.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

Every override corrects a real mismatch, return and income are not double-counted, and the downstream plan result moves for a reason you can explain.

**Return to Core:** go back to Baseline and Allocation, confirm the holding is classified correctly, and rerun the one decision the override was meant to inform.
