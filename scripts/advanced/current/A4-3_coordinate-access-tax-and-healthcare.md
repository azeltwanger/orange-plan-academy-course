> **Watch this only if the early-retirement year uses more than one funding source or the source changes the household's healthcare cost. Otherwise use the simpler Core funding strategy.**

Three plans can fund the same lifestyle and create three different tax and healthcare results.

The question is: **which mix supplies the cash without draining the Bridge or creating a larger tax and healthcare problem?**

== USE THE SAME CASH NEED ==

Assume the household needs $100,000 of spendable cash during one full pre-Medicare year.

These figures are illustrative. The point is to compare the sources together—not promise a tax result or Marketplace premium.

== PATH 1: TAXABLE FIRST ==

Use:

- $30,000 of cash,
- and $70,000 from taxable sales.

Assume the sale contains $25,000 of supported long-term gain and the current quote produces an $8,000 net premium.

The household gets the required cash with lower ordinary income. It also uses more of the taxable Bridge and may sell Bitcoin.

== PATH 2: ADD A ROTH CONVERSION ==

Keep the same spending sources and add a $40,000 conversion.

The conversion does not fund extra lifestyle spending. It moves money from traditional to Roth and adds ordinary income.

Assume the net premium rises from $8,000 to $13,000.

The current cost now includes conversion tax, the $5,000 premium difference, and the cash used to pay both. The possible benefit is a smaller future traditional balance.

== PATH 3: USE AN ELIGIBLE EMPLOYER PLAN ==

Assume the plan permits a $40,000 distribution under the household's facts.

Use:

- $30,000 of cash,
- $40,000 from the traditional plan,
- and $30,000 from taxable sales.

Assume the combined income produces a $15,000 net premium.

This path preserves more taxable Bridge but creates more current ordinary income and healthcare cost. It may still fit when accessible assets are the real constraint.

Replace every quote and tax number with the current household result.

I would reject a strategy that lowers one year's tax while making the Bridge fail before later income starts. I would also question a strategy that protects one year's premium while allowing a future traditional balance to grow unchecked.

== WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM ==

### Spendable cash

**What it means:** dollars available to fund the household.

**Calculated from:** cash, sales, withdrawals, and qualified reimbursements.

### Planning income

**What it means:** the applicable income measures used by tax and healthcare rules.

**Calculated from:** each source under current law. It is not the same as cash received.

**This affects:** tax, healthcare cost, Bridge use, confidence, and future balances.
