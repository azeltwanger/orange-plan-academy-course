> **Watch this only if the household is still considering a specific lender after completing the LTV, survival, and sell-versus-borrow comparisons.**

A low rate does not make a lender safe.

The question is: **what legal, custody, liquidation, and operational structure is the household accepting—and how does it leave?**

== COMPARE STRUCTURE BEFORE RATE ==

Two providers can advertise the same loan and create very different risks.

One may take full custody and have broad rights to use or transfer collateral.

Another may use a segregated or multi-party structure with different key and liquidation controls.

The agreement—not the product name—defines the borrower's rights.

A slightly lower rate is not automatically better when the collateral treatment, insolvency claim, or liquidation process is worse.

== THE QUESTIONS TO GET IN WRITING ==

### Collateral and legal claim

- Who legally holds or controls the collateral?
- Is it segregated for this borrower?
- Can it be rehypothecated, lent, transferred, or pledged again?
- What is the borrower's claim if the lender, custodian, or service provider fails?
- Which jurisdiction and law govern the agreement?

### LTV and liquidation

- What exact LTV creates notice or action?
- How is price measured and how often?
- How much time, if any, does the borrower have?
- Is liquidation partial or full?
- Can the lender act without a successful notification?
- How are proceeds, fees, and remaining collateral handled?

### Interest, fees, and repayment

- Is the rate fixed, variable, or reset at renewal?
- Is interest paid, withheld, or capitalized?
- Are there origination, custody, transfer, late, renewal, or prepayment fees?
- Can the loan be repaid at any time?
- What documentation is required to release collateral?

### Top-up and release

- Is top-up manual or automatic?
- Which account or collateral source can be used?
- Is there a target after top-up?
- When can excess collateral be released?
- Is release automatic, discretionary, or requested?

### Incapacity and death

- Who can speak for the borrower?
- What documents will the lender accept?
- What happens to the collateral and balance after death?
- Can an executor repay or refinance before liquidation?

== WORK THE TWO-PROVIDER COMPARISON ==

Provider A offers a lower rate but takes full custody, permits broad collateral use under the agreement, and uses full liquidation.

Provider B charges more, uses a different collateral-control structure, prohibits or limits collateral reuse, and liquidates only what the agreement requires.

That does not prove Provider B is safe or better. It shows why rate alone cannot answer the decision.

The household should price the extra rate against the difference in legal claim, custody concentration, liquidation outcome, and operational support.

If the borrower cannot clearly explain those differences after reading the documents, the loan is not ready to enter the plan.

== WRITE THE EXIT BEFORE OPENING THE LOAN ==

The exit can be triggered by:

- a scheduled repayment date,
- a planned sale or liquidity event,
- an LTV or Bitcoin-price policy,
- a change in rate or lender terms,
- loss of income or reserve,
- provider downgrade or custody concern,
- retirement or estate transition,
- or the strategy no longer improving the plan.

Write which source closes the loan and how long release of collateral is expected to take under the agreement.

Do not assume refinancing will always exist during a market or lender crisis.

== KEEP THE OPERATING RECORD ==

Store the agreement, current balance, rate, collateral quantity, contact process, notices, repayment instructions, and estate contact under the household document plan.

Orange Plan tracks the planning record. It is not the lender ledger and does not replace the signed agreement.

No credential, seed phrase, private key, or signing secret belongs in the plan file.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose whether the specific provider structure is acceptable and write the conditions that close or reduce the loan.
