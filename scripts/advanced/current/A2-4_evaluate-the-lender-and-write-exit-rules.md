A2.4 Evaluate the lender and write the exit rules

> **Watch this only if the household is still considering a specific lender after completing the LTV, survival, and sell-versus-borrow comparisons.**

A low rate does not make a lender safe.

The question is: **what legal, custody, and liquidation structure is the household accepting—and how does it get out?**

== COMPARE STRUCTURE BEFORE RATE ==

I would compare the lender structure before comparing a small difference in interest rate.

Two providers can advertise the same loan and create very different risks.

One may take full custody, permit broad collateral reuse, and use full liquidation. Another may limit reuse, separate control, or liquidate only what the agreement requires.

That does not prove the second lender is safe. It shows why the signed agreement matters more than the product name.

== ANSWER FIVE QUESTIONS IN WRITING ==

### 1. Who controls the Bitcoin?

Is collateral segregated? Can it be lent, transferred, or pledged again? What claim does the borrower have if the lender or custodian fails?

### 2. What starts lender action?

Which price source is used? What LTV creates notice, top-up, or liquidation? Is there time to respond, and is liquidation partial or full?

### 3. What does the loan really cost?

Is the rate fixed, variable, or reset? Is interest paid or capitalized? Which origination, custody, renewal, transfer, late, or release fees apply?

### 4. How do top-up and release work?

Is top-up manual or automatic? Which assets qualify? When can excess collateral be released, and is release automatic or discretionary?

### 5. What happens during incapacity or death?

Who may speak for the borrower? Which documents are accepted? Can the authorized person repay and release collateral before the lender liquidates?

The clause-by-clause detail belongs in the redacted agreement walkthrough. The concept lesson owns these five decisions.

== WORK THE TWO-PROVIDER COMPARISON ==

Provider A offers a lower rate, takes full custody, permits broad collateral use, and uses full liquidation.

Provider B charges more, limits collateral reuse, and has a different liquidation structure.

The household should compare the extra interest with the difference in legal claim, collateral control, liquidation outcome, and support.

When the borrower cannot explain those differences after reading the documents, the loan is not ready to enter the plan.

== WRITE THE EXIT BEFORE OPENING THE LOAN ==

The exit needs a funding source and a trigger.

Possible triggers include:

- the planned repayment date or liquidity event,
- a Bitcoin-price or LTV line,
- a rate or agreement change,
- loss of income or reserve,
- a provider or custody concern,
- retirement or estate transition,
- or the loan no longer improving the plan.

Do not assume refinancing will exist during a market or lender crisis. A future refinance is an option, not the survival plan.

== KEEP THE OPERATING RECORD ==

Keep the signed agreement, current balance, rate, collateral quantity, notices, repayment instructions, release process, and estate contact with the household records.

Orange Plan holds the planning record. The lender agreement and ledger control the actual position.

No credential or Bitcoin secret belongs in the plan file.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose whether the provider structure is acceptable and write the source and events that reduce or close the loan.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Enter the agreement's actual terms and record the lender, custody concentration, exit, and estate action in Debt, Income, and Protect.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

The household can explain who controls the collateral, what triggers liquidation, what the loan costs, how collateral is released, and how the loan ends without requiring a higher Bitcoin price.
