> **Watch this only if you own taxable Bitcoin with missing lot history or you expect a taxable disposition. Otherwise the Core basis lesson is enough.**

Core got the quantity and known lots organized.

This lesson answers the harder question: **how do you finish the missing record and make sure the lot modeled in Orange Plan is the lot actually documented in the real transaction?**

== RECONSTRUCT QUANTITY BEFORE BASIS ==

The demo household owns 1.75 BTC.

- 1.25 BTC has complete lots and $48,000 of known basis.
- 0.40 BTC has an exchange export available but still needs reconstruction.
- 0.10 BTC comes from an old account with missing records.

The current quantity already reconciles. That is important.

If the transaction history says 1.90 BTC should exist while the accounts contain 1.75, basis work is not ready. A sale, fee, gift, transfer, or missing wallet is still unexplained.

First explain every unit. Then attach dates and dollars.

== BUILD ONE CHAIN OF OWNERSHIP ==

For each acquisition, record:

- date and time when available,
- quantity,
- US-dollar value,
- acquisition fees,
- original exchange or wallet,
- and the transfer trail to the current location.

Keep transfers between accounts owned by the same taxpayer connected to the original lot. A withdrawal from an exchange and deposit to self-custody should not become a fake sale and a new purchase.

When ownership changes—a gift, entity, IRA, trust, or another taxpayer—the analysis can change. Do not force every movement into the same-transfer rule.

== WORK THE 0.40 BTC RECONSTRUCTION ==

Suppose the exchange export shows:

- 0.15 BTC acquired for $4,500,
- 0.10 BTC acquired for $5,000,
- 0.20 BTC acquired for $12,000,
- and 0.05 BTC later sold.

The export contains 0.45 BTC of purchases and one 0.05 BTC disposition, leaving 0.40 BTC.

The reconstruction is not complete merely because the remaining quantity matches.

We still need to identify which lot supplied the 0.05 BTC sale under the records used at the time. That determines which basis remains in the current 0.40 BTC.

If the old return used FIFO, the sale may have come from the first lot. If the taxpayer made and documented a valid specific identification, another lot may have been used.

The prior filing record controls what remains. Do not rewrite history now because HIFO produces a better current result.

== UNKNOWN DOES NOT MEAN ZERO ==

The remaining 0.10 BTC has no reliable acquisition record yet.

Orange Plan should keep it unresolved.

Using zero basis can be a conservative planning stress test. It is not automatically the filing answer and it should not silently become the lot record.

Search original exchange records, bank statements, emails, wallet history, tax software, prior returns, and transaction IDs. Document what was found and what could not be found.

A CPA or tax attorney decides whether a reconstructed position is supportable.

== PLAN THE LOT, THEN EXECUTE THE SAME LOT ==

Orange Plan can compare FIFO, high-basis-first, or another method.

That comparison is useful only when the provider or wallet process and the taxpayer's records support the same identification.

Under current federal digital-asset rules, the timing and evidence differ depending on whether the units are held by a broker or outside a broker. The maintained tax reference and CPA packet own the exact current procedure.

The durable rule is:

> **Choose the lot before or at the disposition under the applicable rules, preserve the identification evidence, and make the tax record match the transaction.**

A note created months later saying “we meant HIFO” is not the same thing.

== BUILD THE SALE PACKET BEFORE SELLING ==

For a planned disposition, prepare:

1. Account or wallet being used
2. Quantity to sell
3. Intended lot or lots
4. Acquisition date, quantity, and basis
5. Provider selection or wallet record required
6. Transaction confirmation and ID
7. Proceeds and fees
8. CPA handoff

When Bitcoin is sent from self-custody to an exchange immediately before sale, preserve the chain connecting the units sent with the lots being identified.

Do not rely only on the exchange's new deposit date.

== WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM ==

### Basis covered quantity

**What it means:** current Bitcoin quantity supported by complete acquisition and disposition records.

**Calculated from:** reconciled lots and remaining units after prior dispositions.

**Edit source:** transaction and tax-lot records.

**This affects:** estimated gain, lot comparisons, tax, and net proceeds.

### Selected-lot gain

**What it means:** amount realized minus basis in the units actually identified and sold.

**Calculated from:** sale proceeds, fees, selected quantity, and selected lot basis.

**This affects:** current tax and which basis remains afterward.
