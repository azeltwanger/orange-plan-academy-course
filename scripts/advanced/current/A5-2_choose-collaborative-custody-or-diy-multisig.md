A5.2 Choose collaborative custody or DIY multisig

============================================================

> **Watch this only if one-key risk is unacceptable and the household is capable of maintaining a multi-key wallet. Otherwise a tested family-ready single-signature setup is complete.**

The question is not, “Which option is more sovereign?”

It is: **who holds the keys and configuration, who helps during recovery, and can the household still move the Bitcoin when one person, key, device, or provider is unavailable?**

== START WITH THE THRESHOLD ==

A 2-of-3 wallet has three keys and any two can authorize a transaction.

That creates two useful properties when the setup is built correctly:

- loss of one key can be survivable,
- and one key alone cannot spend.

It does not automatically solve every problem.

The wallet may still depend on configuration information, compatible coordinator software, physical key distribution, and people who understand the process.

Three keys stored in the same home are still one physical failure domain.

Three keys understood by only one owner are still one human failure domain.

== COLLABORATIVE CUSTODY ==

In one common structure, the household controls two keys and a provider controls one. The actual provider agreement and design must be verified.

When the household possesses two working keys and the required wallet information, it can generally meet the threshold without the provider.

The provider can supply:

- one independently stored key,
- wallet setup and verification,
- configuration or descriptor backup,
- transaction assistance,
- recovery guidance,
- and an inheritance process.

The costs can include annual fees, identity and privacy trade-offs, provider dependence, and changes in company policy.

The family may also assume help will exist decades later when the agreement does not guarantee it.

A provider-held key does not make the arrangement automatically noncustodial or safe. Read who controls the keys and what the provider can sign. Also confirm what data it holds and whether the household can recover without it.

== DIY MULTISIG ==

DIY removes the service relationship.

The household generates, distributes, tests, and maintains every key and the wallet configuration.

That can improve privacy and provider independence.

It also means the household owns:

- device compatibility,
- backup quality,
- configuration and descriptor records,
- software maintenance,
- key replacement,
- transaction coordination,
- privacy decisions,
- and family support after the technical owner is gone.

DIY is not cheaper when the family cannot operate it. The real cost may be an untested recovery process.

== WORK THE DEMO HOUSEHOLD ==

Alex and Jordan hold 1.50 BTC as long-term Legacy capital.

Alex understands the hardware wallet. Jordan is learning recovery through a practice wallet. Their children are 10 and 12.

Compare two 2-of-3 designs.

### Collaborative

- Alex and Jordan control two separated family keys under the actual design.
- A provider controls the third.
- The family has a copy of the required configuration.
- The provider has a documented process for incapacity and death.

The family gains technical help and a separately managed key. It accepts fees, identity disclosure, and provider dependency.

### DIY

- The family controls all three keys in separate physical and human domains.
- It stores redundant protected configuration records.
- Jordan and a backup helper can complete a practice recovery.

The family gains more independence and privacy. It also accepts the full maintenance and heir-support burden.

For this household, I would not choose DIY simply because Alex can set it up today. Jordan's ability to recover without Alex matters more than Alex's ability to build it.

Collaborative custody may be worth the fee when provider-independent recovery works and the family values guided help. Tested single signature may still be better when the multi-key process has not earned its complexity.

== ASK THE PROVIDER-INDEPENDENCE QUESTIONS ==

Before choosing collaborative custody, get written answers:

1. Can we spend or recover if the company disappears?
2. Do we possess enough keys and the required configuration?
3. Which compatible software can reconstruct the wallet?
4. What happens if we stop paying?
5. What identification and legal documents are required after incapacity or death?
6. Can a provider or employee sign with another party without the household's intended approval?
7. How are keys replaced and the wallet migrated?

A service that cannot explain provider-independent recovery has not solved the long-horizon risk.

== DISTRIBUTE KEYS BY FAILURE DOMAIN ==

Key distribution should survive:

- one fire or theft,
- one unavailable person,
- one failed device,
- and, when claimed, one failed provider.

Do not write a universal “owner gets two, executor gets one” rule. Legal roles, trust ownership, family conflict, provider design, and living-access policy can change the right distribution.

The custody practitioner and estate attorney coordinate the technical threshold with legal authority.

== WHERE READINESS COMES FROM ==

**What it means:** the authorized threshold works, the setup survives the failure it claims to survive, and the family can reconstruct the wallet.

**Calculated from:** no app formula. It comes from key distribution, configuration records, provider terms, and successful recovery tests.

**Edit source:** custody design, provider agreement, Family Custody Map, and test record.

**This affects:** theft risk, permanent-loss risk, privacy, fees, family recovery, and estate execution.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Choose tested single signature, collaborative 2-of-3, or DIY 2-of-3 based on family capability and provider-independent recovery—not status.
