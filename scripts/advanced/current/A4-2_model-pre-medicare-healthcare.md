> **Watch this only if retirement begins before Medicare and durable employer or spouse coverage will not cover the full gap. Otherwise record the current coverage and return to Core.**

Retiring at 55 does not only create an investment Bridge.

It creates roughly a decade before Medicare eligibility for most people, unless an earlier eligibility rule applies.

The question is: **which coverage will the household use, what is the full annual cost, and how do withdrawals, gains, and conversions change that cost?**

== PRICE THE COVERAGE THAT ACTUALLY EXISTS ==

Do not use a national average premium as the plan input.

Get current quotes for the household's age, ZIP code, family size, tobacco status when applicable, and expected coverage year.

Compare the real paths available:

- spouse or retiree employer coverage,
- COBRA as a temporary continuation option,
- a Marketplace plan,
- private coverage when available,
- and a non-insurance health-sharing arrangement only when the household understands that different legal and payment protections apply.

COBRA generally preserves the employer-plan coverage for a limited period and can prevent disruption. The household often pays much more of the full cost after employment ends.

Marketplace eligibility and savings depend on the current rules, household size, and expected annual income.

I use CrowdHealth in my own family, and it has saved us meaningful money. I would never present that experience as proof that health sharing is equivalent to insurance. The membership agreement, pre-existing-condition treatment, payment process, HSA compatibility, legal protections, and household reserve all have to be reviewed under the current terms.

== PREMIUM IS NOT THE FULL COST ==

For every path, record:

- annual premium or membership cost,
- deductible or member responsibility,
- expected out-of-pocket spending,
- maximum contractual out-of-pocket exposure when one exists,
- network and prescription restrictions,
- excluded or non-guaranteed expenses,
- and the reserve required to carry the risk.

A Bronze plan can have a lower premium and a larger cost when the household actually uses care.

A higher-premium plan may be better for a household with known prescriptions, specialists, or recurring treatment.

The cheapest monthly number is not automatically the cheapest healthcare plan.

== MAGI CONNECTS HEALTHCARE TO THE WITHDRAWAL STRATEGY ==

Marketplace savings use expected household income under the current Marketplace rules.

Traditional withdrawals, Roth conversions, taxable interest and dividends, realized gains, wages, and other income can increase the amount used in that calculation.

Cash spending, return of basis, qualified Roth distributions, qualified HSA reimbursements, and bona fide loan proceeds can affect spendable cash differently from taxable income—but every source still needs the current tax and program treatment verified.

That creates a real competition for the low-income years.

A large Roth conversion can improve the future tax plan and increase the net healthcare premium in the same year.

A Bitcoin sale can fund spending while only the gain portion enters taxable income, but unresolved basis makes the income estimate unreliable.

The healthcare decision and tax decision cannot be modeled in separate rooms.

== HSA IS A HEALTHCARE BRIDGE, NOT A MAGIC ACCOUNT ==

The demo HSA is assigned to qualified healthcare.

The account can reimburse supported qualified expenses under current rules, including prior eligible expenses when the records and timing support it.

It does not make premiums or every health-related payment automatically tax-free.

Keep receipts, proof that the expense was qualified, and evidence it was not reimbursed elsewhere. Confirm which premiums are eligible under the household's circumstances.

== MODEL THE END DATE AND THE MEDICARE HANDOFF ==

The pre-Medicare cost should have a start and end date.

At Medicare eligibility, the Marketplace or other bridge expense ends and a different set of premiums, deductibles, supplements, drug coverage, and income-related costs begins.

Medicare enrollment timing depends on the person's situation, current coverage, and eligibility. COBRA is not automatically treated the same as active-employment coverage for delaying enrollment.

The maintained reference and insurance professional own the current enrollment steps.
