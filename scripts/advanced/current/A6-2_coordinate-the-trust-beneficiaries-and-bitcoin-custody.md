> **Watch this only if a trust already exists or an attorney has recommended one. Otherwise finish the baseline estate and custody work first.**

A signed trust does not move an asset.

The question is: **does the legal owner, provider record, tax record, and technical Bitcoin access design all point to the same intended people and process?**

== BUILD AN OWNERSHIP AND AUTHORITY MATRIX ==

For every meaningful asset, record:

- legal owner today,
- intended owner after implementation,
- beneficiary or successor record,
- person with management authority during life,
- person with authority after incapacity or death,
- technical access needed,
- professional or provider holding the binding record,
- and proof the change was accepted.

The matrix can describe the process. It never contains a seed phrase, private key, passphrase, PIN, password, or complete spending path.

== WORK THE LEGAL-TECHNICAL MISMATCH ==

Assume the attorney drafts a revocable trust intended to own the household's 1.50 BTC.

The document says the trust owns digital assets and names Jordan as successor trustee.

But the Bitcoin remains in Alex's individual wallet. The wallet backup and device are still known only to Alex. The Family Custody Map still describes Alex as the sole owner, and no assignment, schedule, custody record, or other implementation step shows how the trust acquired the Bitcoin.

Legally, the document and actual ownership may not match.

Operationally, Jordan has trustee language but no tested way to identify or recover the wallet.

The family has not completed the plan. It has created two conflicting stories.

The fix is not putting the seed phrase into the trust.

The attorney, CPA, and custody practitioner must coordinate:

- whether and how the Bitcoin is transferred or assigned,
- how the transfer is documented,
- whether it creates a gift or other tax event,
- who controls the keys during life,
- who can act after incapacity or death,
- and which custody threshold reflects the trust terms.

== DIRECT BITCOIN AND PROVIDER ASSETS NEED DIFFERENT IMPLEMENTATION ==

For an exchange, broker, IRA custodian, collaborative provider, bank, or insurer, the provider's ownership, beneficiary, authorized-user, and claim records control the operational process under the agreement and applicable law.

A trust document does not automatically update those records.

For direct Bitcoin, there may be no administrator to correct the mismatch. The household needs defensible legal ownership records and a custody system the authorized fiduciary can use.

Colorado has adopted a fiduciary-access framework for digital assets, but access to electronic records and provider accounts still depends on consent, legal authority, provider terms, and the actual asset structure.

Possession of a key can move Bitcoin without proving authority. Authority in a document cannot sign a transaction without a key path.

The plan must solve both.

== DO NOT USE ONE UNIVERSAL KEY DISTRIBUTION ==

A trust does not mean the trustee should hold a complete backup or unilateral spending path.

A multi-key design may allow the trustee, trust director, family member, or provider to participate without any one party controlling everything. The actual distribution depends on:

- who owns the Bitcoin,
- who may invest or retain it,
- who may approve distributions,
- who can act during incapacity,
- who succeeds each role,
- and the desired living-access policy.

For a 2-of-3 wallet, three keys exist and any two sign. The legal document must match who can produce each authorized combination.

Do not write the threshold first and force the trust into it later.

== RETIREMENT ACCOUNTS AND TRUST BENEFICIARIES NEED SEPARATE REVIEW ==

A retirement account generally remains in the individual's name rather than being casually retitled to a living trust.

A trust may be named as beneficiary in some plans, but the distribution, beneficiary, look-through, minor, spouse, tax, and administration consequences can be materially different from naming individuals.

The plan administrator holds the binding beneficiary record. The attorney and CPA review the actual account before changing it.

The same caution applies to life insurance, transfer-on-death accounts, business interests, and property with separate titling rules.

== BASIS AND ESTATE INCLUSION MATTER ==

Transferring appreciated Bitcoin to a trust can affect gift reporting, income taxation, estate inclusion, and whether basis adjusts at death.

Grantor-trust income treatment and estate inclusion are different questions.

A trust asset outside the gross estate does not automatically receive the same basis result as an asset included in the estate.

Orange Plan can show the projected asset and estate value. It cannot determine the legal ownership, completed-gift status, or filing result from the word “trust.”

The attorney and CPA own those implementation conclusions.

== TEST THE PROCESS WITHOUT EXPOSING THE MAIN BALANCE ==

Use a practice wallet or provider test process to prove:

1. The trustee or successor knows where the legal and process records are.
2. The authorized technical combination works.
3. An unauthorized single participant cannot spend when the design intends dual control.
4. One specified unavailable key or provider does not permanently block recovery when redundancy is claimed.
5. The executor, trustee, and beneficiaries understand their different roles.
6. The provider's claim and authority process is current.

Do not make the first test the main wallet after death.

== CLOSE THE LOOP WITH PROOF ==

For every implementation item, keep evidence:

- signed legal document,
- accepted provider or beneficiary confirmation,
- assignment or titling record,
- tax and basis record,
- custody threshold and non-secret configuration process,
- successful test date,
- people who participated,
- and next review date.

Orange Plan can track completion and reminders. The proof remains with the attorney, provider, tax file, and custody process.
