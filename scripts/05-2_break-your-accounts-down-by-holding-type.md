5.2 Break your accounts down by holding type, then by timeframe
~9 min at 155 wpm · SPOKEN-PROSE VERSION (calibrated)
============================================================

In today's lesson, we're going to cover the two ways you have to look at your allocation, and why you need both of them.

Allocation is really two questions, asked in order. The first one is: what do you actually hold, account by account, by type of asset? The second one is: when are you going to need each of those dollars? The first question gives you your mix. The second one gives you your buckets. Orange Plan tracks both, and you need both, because a portfolio can have exactly the right mix and still be sitting in completely the wrong timeframes.

== STEP 1: WHAT EACH ACCOUNT HOLDS ==

Most people have one big pile of money and one vague feeling about it. So when the market drops, every dollar feels at risk, including the dollars they won't touch for 30 years.

So the first thing we do is open it up. In the app that's Strategy, then Allocation, and you scroll down to the section called "What each account holds." Every account you own, with the holdings inside it broken out by type.

Orange Plan sorts every holding you have into one of 6 asset classes. Bitcoin, stocks, bonds, real estate, cash, and other.

🎬 GRAPHIC: the couple's 6 accounts as rows, 4 asset-type columns (Bitcoin, stocks, bonds, cash), amounts in the cells, column totals across the bottom. The point of the graphic is that accounts are rows and assets are columns.

Let me run the couple. Their investable total is $295,000.

Their checking and savings is $30,000, and that's all cash. The invested HSA is $10,000 of stocks. The taxable brokerage is $30,000 of stocks. The 529 is $15,000, split $12,000 stocks and $3,000 bonds. The 401(k) and IRA together are $35,000, split $23,000 stocks and $12,000 bonds. And the Bitcoin, on the hardware wallet plus a little on the exchange, is $175,000.

Now read down the columns instead of across the rows, and you've got your mix. $175,000 of Bitcoin, $75,000 of stocks, $15,000 of bonds, $30,000 of cash. That's 59% Bitcoin, 25% stocks, 5% bonds, 10% cash.

Two things come out of this view that you genuinely cannot see any other way.

The first one is that an account is not one asset. Their 401(k) is stocks and bonds at the same time, and the name of the wrapper tells you nothing about what's inside it. The second is that a single asset gets spread across accounts. Their stocks are living in four different places.

== STEP 2: SET YOUR TARGET MIX ==

Same page, the section called "Your target mix." You set a target percentage for each of the 6 classes, and they have to add up to 100% before it'll save.

Underneath that there's a control that says "Flag drift beyond plus or minus," and it defaults to 5 percentage points. That's your tolerance band. So if you set a 60% Bitcoin target with a 5-point band, the app tells you when Bitcoin runs past 65% or drops under 55%.

I think the band is the useful part here. Without one, every price move looks like a reason to go do something. With one, you only hear from the app when the mix has actually moved enough to matter.

And one thing to be clear about: the target sets the priority for new dollars, and it's the reference the app measures drift against. It is not an instruction to go sell something today.

== STEP 3: SORT THE SAME ACCOUNTS BY WHEN YOU'LL NEED THEM ==

Now the second question. Same money, completely different cut.

Every dollar sits in one of 3 buckets, and the bucket tells you three things: what that money is for, how it should behave, and how much volatility it can carry. It's all laid out on the screen, but let me talk you through each one.

The first bucket is the Reserve. This is your zero-to-three-year money, and it needs to be low volatility. High-yield savings, T-bills, short-term Treasuries, money market. Nothing that moves. The Reserve's job is to protect your ability to hold Bitcoin without being forced to sell it in a bad month. We sized this in the reserve lesson.

The second bucket is the Bridge. This is your four-to-ten-year money, and moderate volatility is fine here. Taxable brokerage, an HSA, a 529, I-Bond ladders, a balanced mix of stocks and bonds. The Bridge is your accessible, flexible capital for things that come up before retirement, and it's also the funding lane for known dated costs that are 4 to 10 years out.

The third bucket is Legacy. This is your ten-plus-year money, and this is where the high-growth, high-volatility assets live. Bitcoin, in appropriate custody. Your retirement accounts, the 401(k), the IRA, the Roth. Business equity you're holding long-term. At this timeframe, volatility is a feature, not a bug, because you have the time to ride out the swings.

In the app it's "Funding your timeframes," and then "Sort accounts into buckets," and you drag each account into the bucket that matches when you'll use it. You're moving whole accounts here, not individual holdings.

Let me run the couple's six accounts through it.

Checking and savings is Reserve, the liquid safety net. The taxable brokerage and the 529 are Bridge, both earmarked for dated costs inside a decade. Bitcoin and the retirement accounts are Legacy, because they're fifteen-plus years out.

The HSA is the interesting one, because it could go either way. Theirs is Bridge, since they intend to spend it on medical costs before retirement. If they were leaving it invested to grow as a Roth-like medical fund for later, it would be Legacy. The bucket follows the job you give it, not the account type, and that's true of every row on this page.

That lands them at $30,000 in Reserve, $55,000 in Bridge, and $210,000 in Legacy. Roughly 10%, 19%, and 71%.

Now put the two cuts next to each other, because this is the whole reason you do both. Their mix is 59% Bitcoin, which their tier supports. Their timeframes say 71% of everything they own is untouchable for a decade. For a couple in their forties, both of those are fine. For a couple three years out from retiring, that same mix with those same timeframes would be a real problem, and only the second cut would ever show it to you.

One thing to watch: anything you don't sort yourself gets classified for you. The app infers a bucket from the account type when you haven't set one, and it'll tell you which mode you're in. An inferred bucket is a reasonable starting guess. It isn't your decision. Go sort them.

== SET A TARGET PERCENTAGE FOR EACH BUCKET ==

Each bucket gets two numbers: the current balance, and a target, which is the percentage of your investable assets it should be.

Two things set that target. Your Reserve target you already decided, in months of spending, back in the cash flow module. Your Bridge target is the bridge you priced in the retirement module, the years between your last paycheck and your income floor. Legacy is what's left. So you're not picking these percentages out of the air, and they move as you get closer to retiring, because the Reserve and the Bridge both get bigger relative to everything else.

The target is what the projection actually uses. Change it and the plan re-solves, and from then on your job is routing new dollars toward it.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Your decision here is two things. A target mix across the 6 asset classes, and a target size for each of your three buckets.

Set the mix first, because it decides what you actually buy, and give it a drift band you can live with. 5 points is the default and a reasonable starting place.

Then the buckets, in order. Reserve you already sized in the reserve lesson. Bridge is driven by the years between your last paycheck and your income floor: small if you're decades away, the biggest number on the page if you're close. Legacy is whatever's left, and that's where Bitcoin belongs, because it's the only bucket with enough time to survive a drawdown.

Then compare your targets to what you actually hold. The gap between those two is your routing instruction for the next year.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Strategy → Allocation → sort every account into Reserve, Bridge or Legacy, then set each bucket's target.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

No account is left on the app's inferred guess, and you can say where each bucket's target came from.
