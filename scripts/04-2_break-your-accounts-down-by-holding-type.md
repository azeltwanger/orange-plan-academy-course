============================================================

So in this lesson we're going to look at the same money in two different ways.

First, what does each account actually hold?

Second, when is the household going to need the money?

The first view gives us the asset mix. The second gives each account and holding a job in time.

You need both because a portfolio can have the right overall percentages and still leave the money for a near-term goal exposed to a Bitcoin drawdown.

== AN ACCOUNT IS A WRAPPER, NOT AN ASSET ==

A 401(k) is not an investment. A Roth IRA is not an investment. A taxable brokerage account is not an investment.

Those are account wrappers.

Inside the account, you may hold stocks, bonds, cash, a Bitcoin ETF, or something else.

Two people can both say they have a $200,000 401(k) and have completely different portfolios inside it.

So the first job is to open every account and identify the holdings.

Orange Plan groups the holdings into broad asset classes such as Bitcoin, stocks, bonds, cash, real estate, and other.

That lets you read down the asset columns across all accounts and see the real household mix.

== RUN THE DEMO HOUSEHOLD BY HOLDING TYPE ==

Our demo household has $295,000 of investable assets.

They have $30,000 in checking and savings, which is cash.

They have $75,000 of stocks spread across the HSA, taxable brokerage, 529, and retirement accounts.

They have $15,000 of bonds across the 529 and retirement accounts.

And they have $175,000 of Bitcoin across self-custody and a smaller exchange holding.

Across the whole household, that is roughly 59% Bitcoin, 25% stocks, 5% bonds, and 10% cash.

You cannot get that answer from the account names. You get it from the holdings inside them.

== CURRENT MIX AND TARGET MIX ARE DIFFERENT ==

The current mix is what the household owns today.

The target mix is the percentage the household decided fits the plan.

The difference between them is drift.

A target does two useful things.

It gives new contributions a direction, and it gives the household a reference for deciding whether the current mix has moved enough to review.

It does not automatically mean every account should hold the same percentages.

A cash account may be 100% cash. A Roth may hold long-term growth. A taxable Bridge account may hold something different because the money has to remain accessible.

The household target is the combined result across all of the accounts.

== THE THREE TIME-HORIZON JOBS ==

Now we take the same accounts and ask when the money is going to be used.

The first job is Reserve.

This is the money protecting the next 0 to 3 years, depending on the household and the reason the cash exists. The working emergency reserve, near-term known bills, and the first retirement spending buffer belong here.

It needs liquidity and low volatility because the household cannot wait for a market recovery before using it.

The second job is Bridge.

This is flexible money for roughly the next 4 to 10 years, including costs before retirement, early-retirement years before other income begins, college, a home purchase, or another dated goal.

The Bridge can take more investment risk than the Reserve, but it still has a deadline. The amount firmly committed inside the next few years should become more stable as the date approaches.

The third job is Legacy.

This is the 10-plus-year growth capital and the money the household does not expect to need soon. Bitcoin, long-term retirement holdings, and other high-growth assets can fit here when the custody and risk tolerance support them.

Legacy does not mean the money can never be spent. It means the plan is giving it enough time to survive volatility before it has a job.

== AN ACCOUNT CAN HAVE ONE PRIMARY JOB WITHOUT EVERY HOLDING LOOKING THE SAME ==

For planning simplicity, you may give an account one primary timeframe.

Checking and savings are Reserve.

A taxable account being built for early retirement may be Bridge.

A Roth IRA may be Legacy.

But the account label does not override what is inside it.

A Roth IRA holding a money-market fund still has cash-like risk, even if the wrapper is long-term.

A taxable account holding Bitcoin still has Bitcoin volatility, even if the account is supposed to fund a goal in 5 years.

So review the account job and the holdings together.

== RUN THE DEMO HOUSEHOLD BY TIMEFRAME ==

The $30,000 checking and savings balance is Reserve.

The taxable brokerage and 529 are Bridge because they are accessible and connected to goals inside the next decade.

The retirement accounts and long-term Bitcoin are Legacy.

The HSA depends on the household's intention.

If they expect to spend it on healthcare before or early in retirement, it may be part of the Bridge. If they plan to leave it invested for long-term qualified medical costs, it may be treated as Legacy.

The account type gives us tax rules. The household's intended use gives it the job.

== TARGETS COME FROM DOLLAR NEEDS ==

Do not start by guessing that the Reserve should be 10%, the Bridge 20%, and Legacy 70%.

Start with the dollar need.

The Reserve target comes from the spending basis and target months.

The Bridge comes from known costs and the retirement-income gap that has to be funded before the full income floor begins.

Legacy is what remains available for long-term growth after those nearer-term needs are protected.

Then convert the dollar amounts into percentages so the app can compare the current funding with the targets.

As retirement gets closer or a known cost approaches, Reserve and Bridge may increase relative to Legacy. That is expected. The time horizon changed.

== HOLDING-SPECIFIC ASSUMPTIONS STILL MATTER ==

A broad asset-class assumption is the default, but a specific holding may need its own return or income setting.

A spot Bitcoin ETF should generally follow the Bitcoin assumptions rather than a broad stock assumption.

A rental property may have its own appreciation and rental-yield inputs.

A preferred security or income holding may need a separate return and yield.

Use a holding-level override only when the specific holding would otherwise be modeled incorrectly. Do not customize every position simply because the control exists.

== WHERE THESE NUMBERS COME FROM ==

For current asset mix:

What it means is the combined household exposure by holding type.

It is calculated from every holding across every account.

The edit source is the underlying holding and account assignment.

And it affects allocation drift, projections, tax placement, and drawdown exposure.

For timeframe funding:

What it means is how much money is assigned to near-, medium-, and long-term jobs.

It is calculated from the accounts and holdings assigned to Reserve, Bridge, and Legacy compared with their dollar targets.

The edit source is the account job, known costs, reserve settings, and retirement-income needs.

And it affects where new contributions go and whether a future bill depends on selling at the wrong time.

== YOUR DECISION ==

Your decision is the target asset mix and the primary timeframe job for every account, with dollar targets for Reserve and Bridge that come from real needs.

== PUT IT IN ORANGE PLAN ==

Verify every holding inside each account, review the combined current mix, and set the household target.

Then assign the account and goal funding to Reserve, Bridge, or Legacy. Review any holding-specific return or yield override that is genuinely needed.

== YOU ARE DONE WHEN ==

You can describe what every account holds, what job it serves, and where the Reserve and Bridge targets came from without relying on an inferred account label.
