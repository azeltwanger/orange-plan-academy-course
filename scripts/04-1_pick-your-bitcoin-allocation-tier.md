4.1 Pick your Bitcoin allocation tier

============================================================

In today's lesson, we're going to cover the four Bitcoin allocation tiers, and how to figure out which one you're actually in.

Your allocation is just the share of your money that's in Bitcoin. But the part that trips people up is that the percentage changes depending on what you divide by. The same household can look aggressive or conservative without owning a single different coin.

== WHAT YOU DIVIDE BY ==

Let me show you what I mean with our couple. They're holding 1.75 Bitcoin, and at $100,000 a coin, that's $175,000. Outside of Bitcoin, they have $90,000 in index funds, $30,000 in cash, and a $450,000 house.

If you divide by their investable assets only, that's $175,000 divided by $295,000, which is about 60%. But if you divide by everything they own, including the house, that's $175,000 divided by $745,000, which is about 24%.

Both of those numbers are legitimate. Net of their debts their actual net worth is $447,000, so Bitcoin is about 39% of that. The investable number shows you how the growth engine is running. The gross number shows you how exposed your whole balance sheet is. Use the investable number for the tier, and treat the other two as context.

== WHAT SETS A RESPONSIBLE ALLOCATION ==

So what actually sets a responsible allocation for you? I think it comes down to three things.

The first one is education. How much do you actually know about what Bitcoin is and why it works?

The second one is conviction, which is what that knowledge turns into once you've studied enough to defend the position in an argument.

And the third is your comfort with volatility. And I mean what you would actually do in a 70% drawdown, not what you'd like to think you'd do. Those are two very different things.

Together, those three set the size of the position that you can survive. And that's a different thing than the position you might want.

I'll give you my own experience here. Bitcoin has returned somewhere around 37% a year on average over the last 5 years, and I've also watched my net worth drop 75% in a single drawdown and held through the whole thing. The reason I could hold is that I understood what I owned. If I hadn't done the homework first, I don't think I make it through that.

== THE 4 TIERS ==

Let me walk you through the 4 tiers.

The first tier is Foundation, which is 1 to 5%. This is where you start if you're new and curious. Bitcoin is a small hedge at this level. Your custody can be an exchange or an ETF, and your job at this tier is learning why Bitcoin exists. Read the whitepaper. Understand what makes it different from everything else.

The second tier is Integration, which is 10 to 20%. At this point you believe Bitcoin is a real part of your plan. Your job here is to start learning self-custody. Buy a hardware wallet, send a test transaction, and move a small amount off the exchange first before you move anything big. The common mistake at this tier is having Integration-level allocation with Foundation-level custody, which means your coins are still sitting on an exchange while you're claiming to be 15% in.

The third tier is Optimization, which is 25 to 50%. You believe the thesis and you've actually held through volatility. Bitcoin is one of your main growth drivers now. Your custody needs to match that: coins on hardware wallets, the recovery test done, and you understand what a passphrase is, which is the optional extra word layered on top of your seed words, even if you're not using one yet. Your job at this tier is operational discipline, which is beneficiaries, the heir letter, the dead man's switch, all of which we cover in the estate module. The stakes are real here, because one custody mistake can meaningfully damage the plan.

And the fourth tier is Sovereign, which is 50% or more. At this level you see Bitcoin as future money, and holding anything else starts to feel like the risk. Your custody is optimized, and that could be single-sig, multisig, which just means more than one key is required to spend, collaborative custody, or a mix. The question at this tier isn't "should I buy Bitcoin" anymore. The question is "how do I hold it and pass it on."

== PAIR ALLOCATION WITH CUSTODY ==

So there are really two numbers that describe your Bitcoin situation. Your allocation level, which is the percentage, and your custody level, which is where the coins actually sit.

When both of those are at the same tier, the position is supported. When your allocation is a tier ahead of your custody, that gap is your homework. Your custody has to catch up, because otherwise the position is bigger than the setup can safely hold.

== PLACING THE COUPLE ==

Let's place our couple. Their allocation is 60% of investable, 24% of everything they own, so they're in Optimization-to-Sovereign territory. Their custody is $150,000 on a hardware wallet and $25,000 still on an exchange, which is hardware-wallet level, appropriate for Optimization. And their conviction is high. Bitcoin is a main growth driver for them.

So I'd call them Sovereign, working on it. And their homework is clear: move that $25,000 off the exchange and close the gap.

== NO POSITION SHOULD SURPRISE YOU ==

Last thing on this. If a 70% drop in your largest position would meaningfully break your plan, then the position is too big for your comfort, regardless of what the percentage says. Match the size to what you can actually hold through, because the person who sells at the bottom loses to the person who sized it right and held.

== HOMEWORK ==

Your homework for this lesson is to:

1. Place yourself honestly across the three drivers. Education: what have you actually studied? Conviction: could you defend the position against a well-informed skeptic? And custody: what's your setup today, not what you're planning?
2. Then name your tier. The percentage is what falls out of those three answers. It's not what you pick first.
