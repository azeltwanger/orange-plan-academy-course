# Optional: build the college funding stack

Skip this lesson when the household has no education goal.

## Define the commitment

Choose what the family is promising to provide rather than beginning with the largest published price.

Possible commitments include:

- Four years of in-state tuition
- A fixed dollar amount
- The 529 plus annual parent cash flow
- A percentage of the final net cost

## Funding sources

- Current 529
- New contributions
- Parent cash flow during enrollment
- Scholarships and grants
- Student or family contribution
- Planned taxable or Bitcoin sale
- Deliberately accepted borrowing

## Demo stack

| Source | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Current 529 | $25,000 |
| Parent cash flow during college | $20,000 |
| Student contribution, aid, or defined borrowing | $10,000 |
| Amount still to accumulate or fund from assets | $25,000 |
| **Family commitment** | **$80,000** |

The goal is a complete funding stack, not necessarily a 529 equal to the full commitment.

## Time-horizon rule

Use the future-cost lanes from 2.3. As college approaches, update the actual commitment and move near-term required payments toward a reliable source.

## Where the gap comes from

- **What it means:** commitment still needing a source
- **Calculated from:** commitment minus savings, cash flow, aid, student contribution, and accepted borrowing
- **Edit source:** college event, education holding, and route
- **This affects:** contributions, Bridge, withdrawals, taxes, confidence, and earliest date

## Done when

The funding sources add to the commitment and the next payments are protected from a forced Bitcoin sale.
