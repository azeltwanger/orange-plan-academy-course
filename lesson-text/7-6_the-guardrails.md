# The guardrails: turning confidence into a paycheck

## Why fixed withdrawals fail on Bitcoin

Once withdrawals start, **a bad year early costs far more than the same bad year late** (sequence-of-returns risk) — selling more shares at low prices permanently removes them. The 4% rule is blind to this; point it at an asset that drops 60% and the rule itself is the risk: sell $80k out of a crashed $400k portfolio = 20% of everything, at the bottom, twice. The problem lasts 1–3 years — you need a spending rule that flexes exactly then.

## The 60/80/95 bands

Once a year, read your confidence number. Every adjustment steers toward 80:

| Reading | Move |
|---|---|
| ~60 (lower guardrail) | Cut spending toward 80 |
| ~80 | Hold — inflation adjustment only |
| ~95 (upper guardrail) | Raise spending toward 80 |

Every change capped at ±10%; inflation adjusts every year regardless. (Guyton-Klinger research, sized for Bitcoin volatility — these are Orange Plan's shipped defaults. You read the policy; you don't build it.)

## Cut, don't skip the raise

In a lower-guardrail year: take the 10% cut (then inflation resumes compounding from the new baseline) rather than skipping the inflation raise (a permanent leak in every future year).

## What the number really means

Failures at 80% aren't random — they're almost all a deep drawdown in year 2 instead of year 15. 80% = 8 in 10 futures needed no change; in the other 2 the plan survives *if you cut for a while*. Early-warning signal, not a grade. And the upper guardrail matters too: most retirees under-spend out of fear — 95+ is the math telling you to raise the paycheck.

## The reserve closes the loop

18-month target never moves; refill fully in a held year, partway (~12 months) in a cut year.
