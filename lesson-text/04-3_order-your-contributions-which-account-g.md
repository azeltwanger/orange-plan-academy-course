# Order your contributions: which account gets funded first

Where the next dollar goes, and what changes the answer. **This is a default order, not a command.** Most months it is the right answer. It adjusts per situation based on your allocation targets, and the conditions that adjust it are named below.

## Three things come first

| # | What | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reserve | If it's still short. A reserve gap is what turns a bad month into a forced sale |
| 2 | Employer match | If you have one. 50–100% instant return. Contribute the full match % |
| 3 | Extra debt | If your debt strategy says this debt wins. Module 3 already marked which ones |

Each completes by being skipped deliberately — no match, no reserve gap, and nothing marked for extra principal are finished decisions, not unanswered ones.

## Then it forks by time horizon

The question stops being *which account is next* and becomes *when do I need this money*.

| Path | Timeframe | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| **Forever** | 10+ yrs | HSA → Roth IRA → back to the 401(k) → taxable or self-custody Bitcoin above the limits |
| **Bridge** | 4–10 yrs | Taxable or another flexible account |

**Default: the Forever path.** Dollars you won't need for a decade belong in retirement accounts, in that order.

## When the path changes

> **You have little or nothing in taxable, and you want to retire early.** Then the Bridge path moves up. Filling every retirement account first locks the money behind an age you aren't planning to wait for.

Two more: **the account can't hold what you want to hold** (a plan with no Bitcoin option isn't the better wrapper just because the tax treatment is better), and **income is uncertain** (a deduction is worth less in a year that turns out worse than planned; access is worth more).

**Splitting is a decision, not a failure to decide.** One dollar can answer two legitimate needs. Write down which part and why.

## Account notes

**HSA** — HDHP only. Triple-tax-advantaged; the only shelter that gets all three. Its bucket follows its job: Legacy if you invest it as a Roth-like medical fund, Bridge if you'll spend it on medical costs before retirement. **Roth** — tax-free forever, no RMDs; strong for Bitcoin-heavy plans expecting a higher future bracket. **Traditional** — deduction now, ordinary income later; best if you expect a lower retirement bracket. **Income too high for a direct Roth IRA?** Look up the Backdoor Roth (watch the pro-rata rule).

## The couple's $4,000/mo

Reserve already full, nothing marked for extra principal. $750 match capture (6% of the $150,000 salary) + $300 HSA + $583 Roth IRA (maxed) + $250 into the 401(k) past the match + $2,117 taxable/Bitcoin = $4,000, spent exactly once.

## The routing card

Post the order where you make money decisions. Is the reserve whole? Am I capturing the full match? Does anything my debt strategy marked still want extra principal? Then: 10+ years, or sooner? Forever goes HSA, Roth, 401(k), then taxable or Bitcoin. Bridge goes taxable. One decision routes every future dollar until one of the named conditions changes.

## Two questions about the same dollar

The order and your buckets answer two different questions, and both get answered every time you save.

- **The order: which account?** 401(k), HSA, Roth, taxable. The **wrapper**, which decides how it's taxed.
- **Buckets: which timeframe?** Reserve, Bridge, Legacy. The **job**, which decides what you buy inside.

A dollar into your Roth is a Forever-path decision *and* a Legacy dollar. Same dollar, two labels.

When you have a choice inside an account, **check which bucket is behind and fund that one**. **Strategy → Allocation** shows each timeframe as Funded or Behind, so it's a glance, not a calculation.

⚠ An account is not a bucket. A 401(k) in a stable-value fund is a Legacy *account* holding Bridge-shaped risk.

## Your decision

Your saved default route, the conditions that would change it, and whether this month's answer is one destination or a deliberate split.

Take free money first, because an employer match is the only guaranteed return you'll ever be offered. Settle the reserve and the debts your strategy already marked. Then ask when you need the money, not which account is prettiest — that's the fork, and it's where early retirement changes the answer. Then let the buckets break the tie: once you know the account, check Allocation and put the flexible money toward whichever timeframe reads behind.

You make this decision once and it routes every future dollar without re-deciding every month. The bucket check is the only part that repeats, and it takes about 10 seconds during your monthly pass.
