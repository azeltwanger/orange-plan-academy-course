# The healthcare bridge

Medicare starts at 65 (current law). Retire earlier and you buy your own coverage — usually the largest single line in the bridge years.

## The short-term option

**COBRA** keeps your exact employer plan up to ~18 months — you pay the full premium. A first-year option, not a decade solution.

## Path 1: the ACA marketplace

Metal tiers are cost-sharing levels, not quality levels (Bronze low premium/high deductible; Silver the subsidy benchmark; Gold for heavy users). Almost nobody who plans well pays sticker: **the subsidy scales with your MAGI — and in early retirement, your MAGI is partly a choice.** Spend from cash/basis → low paper income → bigger subsidy. Realize gains or convert → higher MAGI → smaller subsidy.

**The tension:** a big Roth conversion can kill that year's subsidy. Same low-income window, competing moves — you can max it for subsidies or conversions, not both. A conversion decision is also a healthcare decision. Flagship CPA question.

## Path 2: health-sharing

Monthly membership + fixed member responsibility per event; bills above it crowdfunded at cash-pay pricing. (Austin's family uses CrowdHealth — saved ~$1,000/mo.) Trade-offs: **not insurance** (no legal guarantee), limited pre-existing participation, no network, typically not HSA-qualified. Best fit: healthy households with a solid reserve who'll manage bills directly. Verify current terms.

## The decision frame

Bridge years × annual cost of each path, side by side — then health status, risk comfort, HSA plans, providers.

## In the plan

The bridge premium is an **Expense Change life event with a duration** (retire at 60 → 5 years). The app shows conversion taxes in full but does *not* price the subsidy hit of higher MAGI — that's your worksheet. At 65 the income rules swap: subsidies out, IRMAA in.

## Homework

Price your bridge three ways — COBRA (year one), a Silver plan at your realistic MAGI, and current health-sharing pricing. The comparison is the decision.
