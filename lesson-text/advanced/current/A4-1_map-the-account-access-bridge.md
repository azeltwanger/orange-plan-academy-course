# Map the account-access Bridge before 59½

> **Watch this only when retirement begins before the household can explain its early account access.**

## Separate three questions

- Can the money be distributed?
- Is the distribution taxable?
- Does an additional early-distribution tax apply?

Do not label an entire account “locked” or “tax-free.”

## Demo starting point

The first retirement calendar year requires **$101,948 from accounts**. The Core strategy uses taxable assets, which are accessible but may create gains and a large Bitcoin sale.

Map every early year with:

- cash need,
- primary source,
- backup source,
- tax treatment,
- additional-tax treatment,
- and required records.

## Current-employer plan after separation

Current federal rules can provide an exception to the 10% additional tax for qualified-plan distributions after separation in or after the year the employee reaches 55. It generally does not apply to IRAs, and the plan document controls distribution options.

Verify before rolling the plan to an IRA.

## Roth IRA basis

Current ordering rules treat regular contributions before conversions and earnings. Only supported remaining contribution basis should be treated as the early Bridge.

## 72(t) / SEPP

A designed series can provide an exception under current rules, but calculation, duration, and modification rules make it a professional-only tool.

## HSA and other accounts

HSA funds can support qualified healthcare with records. Other accounts such as governmental 457(b), pension, or inherited plans require their own documents.

## Done when

Every early year has a documented primary and backup source and no rollover removes an access option by accident.
