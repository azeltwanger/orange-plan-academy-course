# State taxes and relocation: what the lever is actually worth

**Gate:** watch this if you are actually considering a move, or your Tax page shows an unrealized gain large enough that your state's rate would change what you do. If moving is not on the table, your tax plan is complete without it.

## The one dial that pays every year

You sell, you realize a gain, you owe federal tax. Then your state can tax the same gain again at its own rate. Most states tax a capital gain as ordinary income with no special long-term rate; a handful do not tax income at all. **The state that charges you is the one you are a resident of in the year you sell**, not the one you lived in when you bought.

| Sale | ~9% state | No-tax state |
|---|---|---|
| $115,000 gain (the couple today) | ~$10,700 | $0 |
| $500,000 gain (a normal retirement-year sale for a Bitcoin-heavy household) | ~$46,500 | $0 |

Same Bitcoin, same sale, same federal bill. A $46,500 swing on one transaction.

Those saved dollars compound rather than disappear. One California-versus-Florida comparison came out at about $5,800 a year, which over five years was $57,000 routed into Bitcoin instead of a state treasury.

**And unlike almost every other move in the tax module, this one pays every year.** Most tax moves are one-shot, made in a particular year. A lower state rate applies to every sale you make for as long as you live there.

## Three honest warnings

1. **Moving is more than tax.** Family, work, community, roots. Those are not things a tax number should decide, and no tax saving is worth a life you do not want.
2. **Residency is a legal standard, not an address change.** States look at domicile: where your days are spent, where your home is, where your work and family are, where your life actually happens. You cannot buy this lever with a mailbox.
3. **Sequence matters.** High-tax states audit large exits, especially a big sale landing right after a move. They know the pattern. If the sale is large enough: state-tax advice first, then the move, then the sale. In that order.

Treat this as lifestyle plus residency plus legal planning that happens to save tax, not the other way around.

## Your decision

**Whether the state lever is worth acting on for you, and if so, in what order.**

Price it on your own largest realistic sale first, because the number is either large enough to be part of a life decision or it is not. If it is, the order is advice, then move, then sale.

## Homework

1. Look up your state's treatment of long-term capital gains: special rate, or ordinary income?
2. Run your largest realistic retirement-year sale against your current state's rate, then against a no-tax state. That difference is what the lever is worth to you.
3. If the number is big enough to matter, write down what would have to be true about family, work, and community for a move to make sense at all, before you look at the tax number again.
