# Read your retirement date and confidence number

The retirement page shows four related values:

1. **Planned retirement age** — the age entered by the learner
2. **Confidence target** — the minimum standard selected by the learner
3. **Confidence result** — the share of 1,000 test runs that lasted through planning age
4. **Earliest target-qualified date** — the first date that reaches the selected target

## Planned age and spending

The planned age is the age being tested. Baseline spending is annual living spending, not gross income and not debt payments already modeled separately.

The demo household uses age 55 and $100,000 of retirement living spending in today's dollars. That spending is deliberately higher than its $80,000 current living spending because the household expects a different active-retirement lifestyle.

## Confidence target

At an 80% target, the app looks for the first date where at least 800 of 1,000 runs lasted through planning age.

- Higher target generally means more cushion and a later earliest date.
- Lower target may mean an earlier date and more adjustment risk.

Eighty percent is a starting point, not a universal correct answer.

## Confidence result

An 83% result means roughly 830 of the 1,000 runs lasted under the saved plan. It is not a literal 17% probability of bankruptcy.

The test holds the saved plan constant. Real households can respond by changing spending, work, savings, timing, or strategy.

## Earliest date

Read confidence at the planned age and the earliest date meeting the target together. Both come from the same test-run process; there is no separate deterministic user result.

The Plan target is separate from the starting-spending choices and annual spending guardrails on the Income page.

## Where confidence comes from

- **What it means:** share of runs lasting through planning age
- **Calculated from:** the entire saved plan
- **Edit source:** the underlying input or decision being tested
- **This affects:** the plan verdict and earliest target-qualified date

## Done when

The learner can explain all four numbers, defend the spending input, and name one lever to test rather than changing several inputs at once.
