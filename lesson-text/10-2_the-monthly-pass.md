# The monthly pass

Four inputs, six steps, about five minutes. It catches data drift — not strategy.

## The four inputs

1. **Money moves** — buys, sells, transfers that actually happened
2. **Income/expense changes** — new *baselines* only (a raise counts; a hot restaurant month is noise)
3. **Life events** — move, marriage, kid, job change
4. **Assumptions & targets** — only if something real shifted; most months, glance and go

## The six steps

Open the plan → check the four inputs → enter transactions → review last month's spending vs plan → flag anything needing a real decision → pick 1–3 actions.

**The spending test:** one month over plan is nothing; six months over in the same direction is a new spending level. **Flagging is not deciding** — write the question; it becomes an action or waits for the annual review. Most months the honest answer is nothing changed, and the pass exists to prove it.

## What never touches the plan

The price on the day · a loud news week · a market swing · a gut feeling · **a number that looks nicer** (nudging assumptions moves your freedom date with zero new information).

## The one-to-three action rule

At most three actions, all finishable before the next pass. The long to-do list is the #1 reason reviews stop happening — the compounding comes from *finishing*.

## Homework

Pick a specific recurring day. Set the calendar event. Five minutes a month, one afternoon a year, for the rest of your life.
