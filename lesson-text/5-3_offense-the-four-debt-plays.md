# Offense: the four debt plays

Leverage is a power tool — it builds faster and can take your hand off. Every play comes with its failure mode.

## Play 1: the spread

Borrow low, stay invested higher, compound the difference. Prepaying cheap debt doesn't earn a return — it locks in the debt's rate and gives up what the capital could do. **Fails when:** the payment strains cash flow, the asset underperforms for years, or a variable rate resets. Only counts with a comfortable payment and a solid reserve.

## Play 2: borrow, don't sell

Borrow against Bitcoin instead of selling — loan proceeds generally aren't taxable, and the position stays intact. Strong with a low-basis stack, a real expense, a solid reserve, and conservative LTV. **Fails as** "borrow instead of *ever* selling": liquidation risk + interest + counterparty risk + a loan you can't repay in a long bear. The honest version buys you *choice of when* to sell.

## Play 3: strategic Bitcoin-backed borrowing

Borrow into strength, deliberately. Two guardrails: **cap concentration** (10–20% max with any single lender; some coin never sees one) and **written operating rules before money moves** — purpose, max LTV, top-up trigger, repay trigger, no-go conditions, monitoring, and the exact 50–80% drawdown plan. Every hard call made in advance. **Fails when** started at high LTV — a normal bear becomes a crisis. New leverage to buy Bitcoin demands a far higher standard than keeping cheap existing debt.

## Play 4: keep low-rate debt on purpose

A cheap mortgage is an asset you hold on purpose. Related move: open a HELOC/credit line while income is strong — lenders' enthusiasm evaporates exactly when you'd want it.
