# Spending, floor, the gap, and the bridge years

Your retirement paycheck runs on three numbers, and on the stretch of years where those numbers press hardest on the portfolio.

Your retirement paycheck runs on three numbers.

## 1. A realistic spending number

Four pieces: baseline (housing, food, insurance, utilities, transport) + flexible (travel, gifts: what pauses in a bad year) + healthcare + lumpy costs (roof, car, HVAC). Two honesty checks: is it realistic for the go-go early years, and is healthcare actually in it? Land on one annual number.

## 2. Your income floor

Durable income before selling anything: Social Security, pension, other recurring income, *verified* portfolio income, real part-time income. The guaranteed core is your floor: the part of life already paid for whether markets cooperate or not.

## 3. The gap

**Spending − floor = the portfolio's job.** Then two ratios:

- **Gap ÷ portfolio**: how hard the portfolio works ($80k on $2M = 4%; on $1M = 8%, twice the work at half the size)
- **Reserve ÷ gap**: your buffer measured in time ($160k ÷ $80k = 2 years without selling)

## The numbers move

The floor doesn't turn on at once. The gap is **largest right after your last paycheck** and shrinks as Social Security arrives, so the biggest demand on the portfolio lands in the first few years. Those are the bridge years.

The stretch between your last paycheck and your full income floor. Most early-retirement failures happen here.

## Three milestones

| Milestone | What changes |
|---|---|
| Retirement day | Paycheck stops; portfolio carries the whole gap |
| 59½ | Retirement accounts unlock (no 10% penalty; verify) |
| Social Security starts | The floor rises |

## Count them, then price them

**Bridge years × early-year gap = the bridge's price tag**: what the portfolio must produce with no help, and what your Bridge bucket is sized against.

The couple retires at 60 with an $84k gap:

- Claim SS at 67 → 7 years × $84k = **$588,000** carried alone
- Claim at 62 → 2 years × $84k = **$168,000**

$420,000 on or off the portfolio from one claiming decision.

## Why the bridge is hard

Three problems stack: the biggest gap of retirement, no floor underneath yet, and retirement accounts still penalized (if under 59½).

## When to claim

Delaying grows the check ~8%/yr, and that part is true. But waiting isn't free: every waiting year, the portfolio carries the full gap, and for a Bitcoin-heavy plan, claiming earlier can rationally mean fewer forced sales in a drawdown year. Don't default. Compare 62, FRA, and 70 against your own gap (the app has one-click scenarios for both ends). Personal decision; confirm with your advisor.

## Two bridges, not one

The phrase gets used two ways, and they're different lengths:

| Bridge | Runs from | Decides |
|---|---|---|
| **To your income floor** | Retirement → Social Security starts | How much the portfolio carries alone |
| **To your retirement accounts** | Retirement → age 59½ | Which accounts you can touch cheaply |

Retire at 64 and the second is zero while the first may be 3 years. Retire at 50 and both are long, with the account bridge biting first. In this course "bridge years" means the bridge to your income floor; the other is called the 59½ bridge. **Count both.**

## Build the reserve before you need it

If Bitcoin's cycle roughly holds, the year you retire may land near a cycle high, which means your retirement balance is flattered by timing. It also means an opportunity: **build the retirement-sized reserve during the strong years leading into your date**, not after you've stopped working.

The alternative is retiring at a high, hitting a 70% drawdown in year one with no reserve, and selling Bitcoin at the bottom for groceries. Inside 5 years of retiring, treat filling that reserve as a goal with a deadline, funded out of strength while the strength is there.

## Homework

1. Write down spending, floor, gap.
2. No floor number yet? Pull your Social Security estimate this week (the app takes it monthly).
3. Count your bridge years.
4. Multiply by your early-year gap.
5. Ask honestly: is your Bridge bucket sized for that number?

## Look it up

- **Your Social Security estimate**: [ssa.gov/myaccount](https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount/). Create an account and pull your own benefit estimate; the figure is monthly, at Full Retirement Age. This is the same place the app's helper text points you.
