# Test a decision and read the finished plan

Keep three types of information separate:

- **Baseline:** the current working plan
- **Life event:** a change genuinely expected in the plan
- **Scenario:** a question tested without changing the baseline

## Begin with one question

Examples:

- What happens if I retire 3 years earlier?
- What happens if Bitcoin returns are lower?
- What happens if retirement spending rises?
- What happens if we move?
- What happens if Social Security starts earlier?
- What happens if a drawdown begins near retirement?
- What happens if I borrow instead of sell?

## Change one primary lever first

Test retirement age, spending, returns, state, allocation, contribution, debt, or borrowing separately before combining them.

A combined Scenario becomes useful after the individual effects are understood.

## Stress test versus choice

- **Stress test:** would the plan remain workable under an unfavorable condition?
- **Choice:** does a deliberate action improve the household's priorities enough to justify its cost and risk?

A stress test does not become the baseline. A choice becomes the baseline only after the household decides and updates the source page.

## Read the comparison in five steps

1. Confirm exactly what changed.
2. Read planned age, confidence target, confidence result, and earliest target-qualified date together.
3. Review cash flow, taxes, contributions, and withdrawals.
4. Review accounts, Bitcoin sold or retained, borrowing, collateral, and debt.
5. Review ending assets, estate, reserve, and the risks carrying the result.

Do not reorganize the plan around a small difference that does not change what the household would do.

## Keep a small recurring stress-test set

Possible tests include:

- Weaker Bitcoin returns
- Major drawdown near retirement
- Higher spending or major cost
- Loan liquidation risk when a Bitcoin-backed loan exists

Re-run them after updating the annual baseline.

## Read the report in calculation order

1. Data freshness and assumptions
2. Current position
3. Retirement result
4. Cash Flow and Reserve
5. Debt and Allocation
6. Tax and Retirement Income
7. Custody, Estate, and Insurance
8. One to three actions

A report metric is changed at its source row or strategy, not inside the PDF.

## Six-sentence plan summary

Explain:

1. Where the household stands today
2. What life costs and what is available monthly
3. When the confidence target is first reached and what spending is supported
4. Where the next dollar goes and what happens in a drawdown
5. How retirement is funded and which tax or borrowing questions remain
6. How the family recovers, inherits, and begins the process

## Save the annual record

- Dated PDF for readable snapshot
- Encrypted backup for restorable plan data

Share only the relevant sections and supporting records with each professional. Never include Bitcoin secrets.

## Where Scenario deltas come from

- **What it means:** difference between baseline and defined Scenario
- **Calculated from:** the same projection framework with changed Scenario inputs
- **Edit source:** Scenario definition until a real decision updates the source page
- **This affects:** retirement, cash flow, tax, holdings, debt, and estate comparison metrics

## Done when

You can explain every saved Scenario, summarize the plan in six sentences, and identify the one to three next actions.
