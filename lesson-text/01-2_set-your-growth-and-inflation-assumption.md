# The three layers of a plan, and setting your assumptions

## 1. Baseline and foundation

This has two parts:

- facts that are true now
- assumptions the projection uses to carry those facts into the future

Assumptions include returns, inflation, life expectancy, spending, savings, and expected income growth.

## 2. Future life events

These are changes you genuinely expect: tuition, a car, a house, a job change, retirement spending, or long-term care.

## 3. Scenarios

These are questions: retire earlier, buy a bigger house, use lower Bitcoin returns, or move states. They stay separate so testing a question does not change the real plan.

**Facts and saved assumptions belong in the baseline. Expected changes belong in life events. Questions belong in scenarios.**

## Choose assumptions deliberately

One return assumption can move the retirement date by years. Use a declining Bitcoin model rather than a flat return forever. Treat inflation seriously. Pick settings you can defend, not settings that produce the date you prefer.

The preset selected during onboarding is only a starting choice. The Foundation walkthrough reviews and saves the final selection.

## Your decision

Which growth model and inflation rate your baseline will use, and why.

## Put it in Orange Plan

Hold the decision for the Foundation walkthrough. You will review the onboarding preset, inflation, life expectancy, and the other asset assumptions there.

## You are done when

You have chosen a growth model and inflation rate you could explain to your family.
