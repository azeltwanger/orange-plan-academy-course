# The three layers of a plan, and setting your assumptions

## Baseline, life event, or Scenario

- **Baseline:** true today
- **Life event:** a future change genuinely expected in the saved plan
- **Scenario:** a question tested beside the baseline

For the demo household:

- $190,000 household income is baseline data.
- A vehicle replacement in 5 years is a life event.
- Retiring 3 years earlier is a Scenario.

## Assumptions

Assumptions are the rules used to project the plan through time. They include returns, inflation, and planning age.

Choose a starting view you can explain rather than the one that creates the most attractive date. Test weaker or stronger views through Scenarios.

A declining Bitcoin return shape can avoid assuming one high percentage forever. Power law is Austin's current personal starting view, not a guarantee.

Inflation compounds the spending need. Small differences can materially change a long retirement.

## Holding-specific overrides

Use the broad Plan assumption unless a holding would otherwise be modeled incorrectly.

Examples include:

- A spot Bitcoin ETF using Bitcoin rather than stock assumptions
- Rental property with separate appreciation and income
- An income security with distinct value return and cash yield

Return and income/yield are separate parts of the projection.

## Saved input, preview, or Scenario

- A direct saved input is part of the plan.
- A strategy preview can move the displayed result before it is applied.
- A saved Scenario remains separate from the baseline.

Use the state displayed by the page rather than assuming one save rule applies everywhere.

## Trace the result

Correct the source input or deliberately change the decision. Do not manipulate the result until it looks better.

## Done when

The learner can sort information into the correct layer, explain the saved assumptions, identify legitimate holding overrides, and recognize whether a page shows a saved plan, preview, or Scenario.
