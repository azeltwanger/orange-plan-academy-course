# Fund known future costs: college, cars, a house, repairs

A known cost is anything on the calendar with a date: tuition, a roof, a car, a wedding, taxes on a planned conversion. **Your reserve is not for these.** Reserves cover the unplanned. Confuse the two and you either drain the reserve for planned costs or sell Bitcoin at the worst moment.

Every known cost gets a lane: where those dollars sit between now and the due date.

## Match the lane to the timing

| Timeframe | Where the money sits | Not here |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 year | HYSA, T-bills, CDs | Anything volatile |
| 1–3 years | T-bills, short Treasuries, HYSA | Stocks, Bitcoin |
| 3–10 years | Balanced stocks/bonds, I-Bonds | Bitcoin |
| 10+ years | A planned Bitcoin sell schedule can start here | Cash (drag too high) |

For 10+ year costs, plan the sales into low-bracket years (tax module).

## Pre-fund vs pay from flow

- Small dated costs ($2,000 trip next summer): pay from flow: $200/mo starting six months out.
- Big dated costs ($100,000 college in 8 years): pre-fund a lane with small contributions instead of one giant sale.

**The test:** if paying it all at once from monthly flow would break your surplus, pre-fund it.

## The college example ($100k, 8 years out)

- **Pay from flow:** $25k/yr eats over half the surplus for four straight years.
- **Pre-fund:** ~$1,000/mo into an intermediate lane → ~$110k with growth; tuition years become a routing move.
- **Sell Bitcoin:** works if tuition lands in low-bracket years; expensive in high-bracket working years.

There's no single right answer, but the plan must know the cost is coming and have a lane for it.

## You are done when

Every known cost inside ten years either has a funding lane or a deliberate decision that monthly cash flow absorbs it. A cost you've decided to absorb is finished, not skipped.
