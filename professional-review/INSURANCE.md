# A. Coverage-gap method

## I-01 · Start with the survivor need, not an income multiple

**File:** `scripts/08-4...`

**Current course position:** A life-insurance estimate begins with the surviving household's spending, debts, transition costs, childcare/education/healthcare commitments, and desired reserve, then subtracts survivor income, verified benefits, deliberately usable assets, and existing coverage.

**Review:** Confirm the method and identify omitted items such as final expenses, inflation, investment return, taxes, Social Security survivor benefits, existing group coverage, or timing.

## I-02 · Rebuild the survivor budget

**Current course position:** The surviving household does not automatically need the same current outflow. Some costs fall; others may rise. The demo therefore uses a separately built $95,000 all-in survivor need rather than simply adding current $80,000 living spending and $22,000 debt.

**Review:** Confirm this is a sound teaching distinction.

## I-03 · Demo calculation

```text
$95,000 survivor need
− 40,000 survivor earned income
= $55,000 annual gap

$55,000 × 15 years
+ 180,000 debts / education / transition commitments
= $1,005,000 first-pass need

$1,005,000
− 175,000 existing coverage + usable assets deliberately assigned
= $830,000 first-pass remaining gap
```

**Review:** Confirm arithmetic and whether a simple non-discounted first pass is pedagogically acceptable when clearly labelled for licensed refinement.

## I-04 · Do not automatically count all Bitcoin

**Current course position:** The household decides how much of the Bitcoin or other assets is genuinely available to support survivors after considering collateral, tax, liquidity, custody, and the desire to preserve Legacy assets.

**Review:** Confirm and identify whether life-insurance need should use gross assets, liquid assets, or a more formal capital-needs model.

## I-05 · Coverage shrinks or changes over time

**Current course position:** Life need can fall as debts end, children become independent, and assets grow. Disability need changes when earned income ends. Umbrella need can rise as assets/exposure rise. Review rather than allowing accidental lapse or automatic lifetime continuation.

**Review:** Confirm and recommend the right review triggers.

# B. Term life

## I-06 · Product description

**Current course position:** Term life generally provides a death benefit for a defined coverage period and does not build a policy cash value intended to remain after the term.

**Review:** Confirm durable wording and exceptions such as return-of-premium or convertible term without overcomplicating the core.

## I-07 · Compare term first for temporary need

**Current course position:** For a temporary family-protection gap, Austin would generally compare term coverage first because it is often the simpler and lower-premium way to buy a given death benefit.

**Review:** This is Austin's planning judgment, not a product fact. Confirm the factual premise and identify cases where the qualification must be stronger.

## I-08 · Laddering / staged coverage

**Current course position:** Multiple term lengths can be compared when the need is expected to fall over time.

**Review:** Confirm whether this belongs in core or Advanced and what lapse/replacement warning is needed.

## I-09 · Underwriting and timing

**Current course position:** Age and health can affect availability and pricing, so a household with a real uncovered dependency should not postpone obtaining quotes solely because the future Bitcoin balance may grow.

**Review:** Confirm wording and avoid urgency that sounds like a sale.

# C. Permanent life

## I-10 · Product description

**Current course position:** Permanent life can provide coverage intended to continue for life when premiums and policy requirements are met and may include cash value. It has more costs, assumptions, surrender/lapse risk, and management complexity than basic term coverage.

**Review:** Confirm across whole life, universal life, indexed universal life, variable universal life, and guaranteed universal life. Recommend a neutral umbrella description.

## I-11 · Legitimate uses

**Current course position:** Permanent coverage can have legitimate uses for a lifelong need, estate liquidity, business planning, special-needs planning, or another reviewed strategy.

**Review:** Confirm and identify any other common use that should be named.

## I-12 · Sales illustrations

**Current course position:** Do not buy solely because an illustration uses “bank” language or shows an attractive projected value. Compare guaranteed and non-guaranteed values, fees, surrender charges, lapse risk, loans, financing, and lower crediting outcomes.

**Review:** Confirm product-neutral wording and whether policy loans need a separate warning.

## I-13 · Life-insurance tax statement

**Current course position:** Death proceeds received by a beneficiary are generally excluded from federal gross income, while interest, transfer-for-value, ownership, estate-tax, and other exceptions can change the result.

**Review:** Coordinate with CPA/attorney and confirm the minimum core qualification.

# D. Disability income

## I-14 · Two-part impact

**Current course position:** Disability can reduce earned income while increasing care or assistance costs and stopping contributions, so the plan compares the after-tax benefit with bare-bones and normal spending.

**Review:** Confirm.

## I-15 · Policy fields to verify

The course asks the learner to verify:

- benefit percentage,
- monthly cap,
- premium payer and tax treatment,
- elimination period,
- benefit period,
- disability definition,
- excluded income such as bonuses/commission/business income,
- offsets and integration with other benefits.

**Review:** Add or remove material fields for a core consumer checklist.

## I-16 · Own occupation / any occupation

**Current course position:** The disability definition is contractual. “Own occupation,” “regular occupation,” “modified own occupation,” and “any occupation” should not be reduced to one universal meaning without reading the policy.

**Review:** Confirm and supply durable wording.

## I-17 · Tax treatment

**Current course position:** Disability-benefit tax treatment can depend on who paid premiums and whether premiums were paid pre-tax or after-tax; use the after-tax amount in the gap.

**Review:** Confirm with CPA and identify common group-policy complications.

## I-18 · Reserve versus disability coverage

**Current course position:** The reserve may cover an elimination period but is not automatically designed to replace years of earnings.

**Review:** Confirm.

# E. Personal umbrella liability

## I-19 · Product purpose

**Current course position:** A personal umbrella policy can provide added liability and legal-defense coverage above or outside portions of underlying home, auto, or renters policies, subject to terms and exclusions. It generally does not insure damage to the household's own property.

**Review:** Confirm and identify excess-liability versus umbrella distinctions.

## I-20 · Underlying limits

**Current course position:** The household verifies the underlying limits required by the umbrella carrier. A gap in the base policy can leave the insured responsible for part of a loss.

**Review:** Confirm exact wording.

## I-21 · Amount and risk factors

**Current course position:** Review assets and future income at risk, drivers, property, rentals, business activity, public profile, watercraft/recreational risks, and other exposures. The course does not state one universal amount.

**Review:** Confirm and identify exclusions that commonly surprise consumers.

## I-22 · Bitcoin/public profile

**Current course position:** Publicly discussing wealth or Bitcoin can affect privacy/security risk, but it does not by itself determine an umbrella limit.

**Review:** Confirm this should remain a brief contextual point rather than an insurance claim.

# F. Long-term care

## I-23 · Distinct risk

**Current course position:** Long-term care expenses and custodial assistance are not the same as ordinary acute medical expenses and may not be covered by standard health insurance or Medicare in the way households assume.

**Review:** Confirm wording and public-program qualifications.

## I-24 · Funding options

**Current course position:** Possible sources include family support, portfolio self-insurance, traditional LTC insurance, hybrid products, public benefits where eligible, or a combination.

**Review:** Confirm product-neutral description.

## I-25 · Decision factors

The course lists age, health, family support, state, desired care setting, premium, inflation protection, elimination period, benefit period, and risk of changed premiums/needs.

**Review:** Add material policy fields such as benefit triggers, shared care, nonforfeiture, home-care coverage, tax-qualified status, partnership programs, and insurer strength where appropriate.

## I-26 · Review timing

**Current course position:** A younger household may model the risk and set a future review date rather than buying immediately; someone closer to the decision window gets current quotes and compares them with spouse-protection and self-insurance capacity.

**Review:** Confirm this is balanced and not a recommendation tied to one age.

# G. Policy ownership, beneficiaries, and claims

## I-27 · Provider record

**Current course position:** The carrier holds the binding policy and beneficiary record; Orange Plan is a checklist. Primary and contingent beneficiaries and owner information are confirmed directly with the carrier.

**Review:** Confirm.

## I-28 · Minors and trusts

**Current course position:** Naming a minor or trust as beneficiary requires attorney and carrier review; the core does not prescribe a structure.

**Review:** Supply a concise warning.

## I-29 · Family discovery

**Current course position:** The family knows the policy exists, the carrier, and where claim information is stored without putting account credentials into the heir letter.

**Review:** Confirm and recommend minimum documentation.

## I-30 · Replacement and cancellation

**Current course position:** Do not cancel needed existing coverage until replacement is in force, the new contract is reviewed, and the professional confirms the change. Do not allow an important policy to lapse accidentally.

**Review:** Confirm state replacement rules, contestability/suicide periods, new underwriting, surrender charges, and exchange consequences that merit a warning.
