# A. Cost basis and digital-asset records

## T-01 · Digital assets are treated as property for US federal income-tax purposes

**Files:** `scripts/05-1...`, `lesson-text/05-1...`

**Current course position:** A taxable disposition creates gain or loss based generally on amount realized minus the basis of the units disposed of.

**Review:** Confirm terminology, material exceptions, and whether “amount realized” and “basis” are introduced accurately for a core lesson.

## T-02 · Lot-level records

**Current course position:** Each acquisition can carry its own date, time, quantity, basis, and holding period; the basis in units still owned depends on the surviving lots.

**Review:** Confirm the record fields and whether date/time needs qualification for current broker versus unhosted-wallet rules.

## T-03 · Transfers between the taxpayer's own accounts

**Current course position:** Moving the same Bitcoin between accounts or wallets owned by the same taxpayer is generally a transfer rather than a sale; basis and holding period continue with the units.

**Review:** Add qualifications for network fees, transfers involving entities or IRA/LLC ownership, wrapped assets, exchange conversions, and any circumstance where “same owner” is not straightforward.

## T-04 · Fees

**Current course position:** Permitted acquisition costs can affect basis; fees paid in digital assets may create separate tax consequences.

**Review:** Supply durable core wording that does not overstate which fees are capitalized under current law.

## T-05 · Unknown basis

**Current course position:** Unknown basis remains visibly unresolved. The course does not silently enter zero. A zero-basis assumption may be conservative for a planning estimate but is still an assumption and not automatically the filing answer.

**Review:** Confirm whether this is the safest educational wording and what warning should accompany a reconstructed estimate.

# B. Taxable, traditional, Roth, and HSA accounts

## T-08 · Taxable account description

**Files:** `scripts/04-4...`, `05-2...`, `06-2...`

**Current course position:** Interest and dividends may create current income; taxable dispositions may create capital gain or loss; access is generally not subject to a retirement-account age restriction.

**Review:** Confirm concise wording and material exceptions.

## T-09 · Traditional account description

**Current course position:** Contributions may reduce current taxable income or be excluded from wages depending on account and eligibility; taxable withdrawals and Roth conversions generally create ordinary income.

**Review:** Confirm account-type qualifications, deductible versus nondeductible IRA basis, and whether any core wording could imply every traditional contribution is deductible.

## T-10 · Roth account description

**Current course position:** Roth contributions are after-tax; qualified distributions can be tax-free. Under current federal rules, Roth IRA and designated Roth owners generally do not have lifetime RMDs, while beneficiary rules still apply.

**Review:** Confirm current treatment and whether Roth 401(k)/403(b) wording needs a plan-year or rollover qualification.

## T-11 · HSA description

**Current course position:** An eligible HSA can offer unusually favorable tax treatment, but eligibility, contribution limits, qualified expenses, and state treatment must be checked.

**Review:** Confirm a durable one-paragraph explanation and whether California/New Jersey or other state differences deserve a lesson-text note.

## T-12 · Asset location

**Current course position:** Tax-inefficient income-producing assets may benefit from shelter; high expected growth may be valuable in Roth space; tax-efficient holdings may fit taxable when access is needed. These are planning principles, not tax rules.

**Review:** Flag any statement that sounds like a guaranteed tax optimum or ignores loss, basis, charitable, estate, or state considerations.

# C. Tax roadmap and retirement windows

## T-13 · Roth conversion

**Files:** `scripts/05-2...`, `06-2...`

**Current course position:** A taxable Roth conversion generally adds ordinary income in the conversion year. A lower-income period after work and before Social Security/RMDs may create a useful window, but the comparison includes state tax, Medicare-related costs, cash to pay tax, future rates, and other income.

**Review:** Confirm wording, pro-rata considerations, withholding/estimated-payment issues, five-year rules, and whether any additional core warning is material.

## T-14 · RMD applicable age

**Current lesson text currently references:** age 73 for people reaching 73 before 2033 and age 75 for people reaching 74 after 2032.

**Review:** Confirm precise birth-year wording under current law. Recommend whether exact ages should remain only in maintained lesson text rather than spoken video.

## T-15 · RMD calculation and downstream effects

**Current course position:** RMDs generally use prior year-end balance and an IRS life-expectancy factor and can increase taxable income whether or not the household needs the cash.

**Review:** Confirm material first-year, still-working, inherited-account, QCD, and plan-type qualifications that should be mentioned or intentionally left to Advanced.

## T-16 · Long-term capital gains

**Current course position:** Federal long-term capital-gain rates use a separate structure that stacks with taxable income; state tax and NIIT may change the result; the same gain can cost differently in a working versus lower-income year.

**Review:** Confirm the teaching model and identify any wording that could imply the entire gain is taxed at one marginal rate.

## T-17 · Gain and loss harvesting

**Current course position:** Harvesting is advanced. The core only identifies a possible window and requires current digital-asset/security rules, identification, state law, and the taxpayer's full return to be reviewed.

**Review:** Confirm whether the course should mention current wash-sale differences at all or avoid the issue until the Advanced lesson is maintained for the recording year.

## T-18 · Social Security taxation and Medicare-related costs

**Current course position:** Conversions, gains, and withdrawals can change Social Security taxation and Medicare-related costs.

**Review:** Confirm the durable terminology and which detailed thresholds should remain out of evergreen video.
