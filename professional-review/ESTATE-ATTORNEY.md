# A. Roles and authority

## E-01 · Executor / personal representative

**Files:** `scripts/08-1...`, `lesson-text/08-1...`

**Current course position:** A will generally nominates an executor or personal representative. The applicable court or legal process establishes authority over probate assets; telling someone they are the executor does not by itself grant authority.

**Review:** Confirm terminology and whether the core needs to distinguish independent administration, small-estate procedures, trust administration, and non-probate assets.

## E-02 · Financial power of attorney

**Current course position:** A financial agent acts during the principal's life within the document's authority; a power of attorney generally ends at death.

**Review:** Confirm state-specific wording, durability, effectiveness, successor agents, acceptance by financial institutions, and digital-asset authority.

## E-03 · Healthcare decision-maker

**Current course position:** Healthcare directives and medical powers name a person for healthcare decisions; that role is different from managing financial accounts.

**Review:** Confirm durable evergreen wording and state-document naming differences.

## E-04 · Trustee

**Current course position:** A trustee manages property owned by or passing to a trust under the instrument and applicable law. A trust is not automatically required merely because the household owns Bitcoin.

**Review:** Confirm the trust gate and any important incapacity/probate/privacy distinction.

## E-05 · One person serving multiple roles

**Current course position:** A spouse or heir may also serve as executor, agent, or trustee when the legal and custody design supports it. There is no universal course rule that an heir and executor must be different.

**Review:** Confirm and identify conflict, bond, creditor, family, fiduciary-duty, or state-law situations that deserve qualification.

# B. Documents and beneficiary records

## E-06 · Will scope

**Current course position:** A will directs probate property, nominates the personal representative, and can address guardianship and other state-law matters. It does not control every asset the household owns.

**Review:** Confirm concise wording.

## E-07 · Beneficiary / POD / TOD records

**Current course position:** Retirement plans, IRAs, insurance policies, and accounts with valid beneficiary or death-transfer designations may pass under the provider record rather than the will's probate instructions.

**Review:** Confirm the correct degree of certainty and qualifications for ERISA plans, IRAs, insurance, community property, spousal consent, divorce, predeceased beneficiaries, disclaimers, trusts, and invalid or missing designations.

## E-08 · “The form beats the will” shorthand

**Current course position:** The course deliberately avoids saying the form always beats the will. It instructs the learner to review provider designations and legal documents together and confirm the provider's current record.

**Review:** Confirm this is the safest core formulation.

## E-09 · Orange Plan is not the binding record

**Current course position:** Orange Plan is a checklist and planning record. The attorney, court, plan administrator, custodian, insurer, bank, or broker owns the legal or contractual record.

**Review:** Confirm wording and any risk of calling a status “complete” before outside confirmation.

## E-10 · Stale workplace beneficiary demo

**Demo:** One workplace retirement account still names a parent from before marriage; the will names the spouse.

**Review:** Confirm this is a realistic teaching example without stating a guaranteed result before plan type, state, and spousal rights are known.

# C. Digital-asset authority and provider access

## E-11 · Legal authority versus possession of a key

**Files:** `scripts/08-1...`, `08-2...`, `08-3...`

**Current course position:** Possessing a device, backup, or key does not by itself establish lawful authority or beneficial ownership. Being executor does not by itself provide a key.

**Review:** Confirm the educational wording and whether it should mention fiduciary duty, unauthorized access statutes, conversion, or provider terms.

## E-12 · Digital-asset powers in documents

**Current course position:** The attorney should review whether the will, trust, and financial power of attorney grant the authority needed to deal with digital assets, custodians, online accounts, and associated electronic communications under the state's law.

**Review:** Confirm RUFADAA/state-law terminology, “catalogue” versus content issues where relevant, online-tool priority, and provider agreements.

## E-13 · Exchange / custodian death-claim process

**Current course position:** For custodial Bitcoin, the provider's death-claim, beneficiary, authentication, and document process is part of the estate plan. Household instructions cannot replace it.

**Review:** Confirm what should be verified and how the course should describe delays or provider discretion without making unsupported claims.

## E-14 · Self-custody process documents

**Current course position:** Legal and process documents can identify what exists, who should act, where the process begins, and which professional/provider to contact. They do not contain seed phrases, private keys, passphrases, PINs, or passwords.

**Review:** Confirm whether the attorney would want any additional non-secret information and how storage should be coordinated with the legal plan.

## E-15 · Technical helper

**Current course position:** A named Bitcoin technical helper can assist the legally authorized person but receives no automatic legal authority and no secret merely because the person is listed in the plan.

**Review:** Confirm and identify engagement, compensation, conflict, privacy, and fiduciary issues worth noting.

# D. Dual control, redundancy, and multisig coordination

## E-16 · Dual control and legal authority are separate tests

**File:** `scripts/08-2...`

**Current course position:** A custody threshold describes what technical components can sign. The estate documents describe who is legally authorized to act. Both must point toward the same intended outcome.

**Review:** Confirm this framing.

## E-17 · 2-of-3 description

**Current course position:** A 2-of-3 multisig has three keys; any two sign. No fourth key belongs in that arithmetic.

**Review:** Confirm the estate lesson should also address ownership, key-holder agreements, provider succession, replacement keys, and incapacity.

## E-18 · Executor and heir holding components

**Current course position:** There is no universal rule requiring the executor and heir to be different. The design must be reviewed if one person's combined legal role and custody components would allow unilateral action contrary to the intended process.

**Review:** Confirm and supply a more legally precise formulation if needed.

## E-19 · Configuration records / descriptors

**Current course position:** Some multi-key wallets require non-secret configuration information or descriptors to reconstruct and monitor the wallet. These are not private keys but can reveal addresses and balances, so the plan keeps redundant protected copies.

**Review:** Confirm how an estate plan should refer to such records without embedding changing technical detail in legal instruments.

## E-20 · Practice wallet

**Current course position:** The family tests the process with a small practice wallet rather than exposing the main wallet backup. The test does not replace legal review.

**Review:** Identify any concern about who holds or transacts with the practice funds, documentation, or fiduciary misunderstanding.

# E. Heir letter, executor packet, and delivery

## E-21 · Heir letter is not a legal instrument

**File:** `scripts/08-3...`

**Current course position:** The heir letter is a plain-language starting guide. It does not amend the will, create ownership, appoint a fiduciary, or grant legal authority.

**Review:** Confirm and recommend a disclaimer that is clear without sounding like legal boilerplate.

## E-22 · First-call and stop-sign instructions

**Current course position:** The letter states whom to call, what not to do, what types of assets exist, and where process/legal documents are stored. It tells the reader not to share secrets or move Bitcoin before authority, custody process, and tax consequences are understood.

**Review:** Confirm this is useful and whether “do not move Bitcoin” needs an emergency/security exception or attorney phrasing.

## E-23 · Executor packet

**Current course position:** The packet can identify attorney, CPA, technical helper, providers, document locations, and claim/recovery starting points without containing a Bitcoin secret.

**Review:** Supply a state-appropriate minimum contents list and identify information that should be held only by the attorney or fiduciary.

## E-24 · Dead-man switch

**Current course position:** A dead-man switch or cloud notification is an additional discovery path. It does not move Bitcoin, create authority, replace the will/trust, or serve as the only copy of the plan.

**Review:** Confirm legal/privacy/e-signature concerns, false-trigger risk, service failure, recipient consent, and whether the term itself should be softened in client-facing material.

## E-25 · More than one discovery path

**Current course position:** The family should know the plan exists through at least two reliable paths, such as attorney/executor storage plus a separate notification or family review.

**Review:** Confirm and recommend a practical standard.

# F. Trust gate and advanced material

## E-26 · Trust is conditional, not automatic

**Current course position:** Trust planning remains Advanced and is triggered by a real probate, incapacity, privacy, management, tax, special-needs, family, or business need—not merely by owning Bitcoin.

**Review:** Confirm the gate and identify other common reasons.

## E-27 · Funding and titling

**Current course position:** A signed trust that does not own or receive the intended property may not accomplish the transfer plan. Bitcoin ownership, entity structure, custodian records, and key control must match the attorney's design.

**Review:** Provide safe core/Advanced wording; identify prohibited overgeneralizations.

## E-28 · Retirement accounts and trusts

**Current course position:** Naming a trust as retirement-account beneficiary is a specialized decision requiring attorney/CPA review; it is not a default estate technique.

**Review:** Confirm and identify what belongs in the Advanced lesson.
