# Orange Plan Academy — licensed insurance professional review packet

**Purpose:** verify the insurance education in the core course without turning the Academy into product sales or individualized recommendations.  
**Reviewer output:** mark each item **accurate**, **qualify**, or **remove / replace**, with replacement wording where needed.

The course should teach how to calculate a protection gap, what questions to ask, and when professional review is required. It should not prescribe a carrier, policy, rider, coverage amount, or product structure.

## A. Gap-first framework

The course starts with the financial need rather than an income multiple or product.

For life insurance, it adds:

- Survivor spending and lost support.
- Debts the family intends or needs to pay.
- Childcare, education, healthcare, final, and transition costs.
- Reserve and other specific commitments.

It then subtracts:

- Surviving income.
- Liquid assets deliberately available to the need.
- Verified survivor benefits or pensions.
- Existing insurance.
- Expenses or debts that genuinely disappear.

Please review whether this is a sound educational framework and what important need it omits, such as inflation, return on proceeds, taxes, college timing, business obligations, special-needs support, or survivor retirement funding.

The course explicitly says the result is taken to a licensed professional and is not the final coverage recommendation.

## B. Bitcoin and self-insurance

Review the guidance not to subtract the entire Bitcoin balance automatically.

The household should consider:

- Whether the Bitcoin is pledged as collateral.
- Tax and liquidity consequences of a sale.
- Custody and legal access.
- Whether the family can tolerate a drawdown at the time of need.
- Whether preserving the Legacy asset is one reason insurance is being considered.
- Whether part of the portfolio is deliberately available for self-insurance.

Confirm that the course does not overstate Bitcoin’s ability to replace guaranteed coverage or ignore volatility and access risk.

## C. Term life insurance

Please verify the product-neutral description that term insurance generally:

- Provides a death benefit during a defined coverage period.
- Usually does not provide a cash value the policyholder expects to retain after the term.
- Is often the first comparison for a temporary family-protection gap.
- May allow the household to buy a larger death benefit for a lower initial premium than many permanent designs, subject to underwriting and policy terms.

Review whether “fixed premium” or “level term” needs to be distinguished from annual renewable or other term structures.

Review the teaching use of laddering multiple term policies as a way to match a declining need. Identify lapse, administration, conversion, underwriting, or replacement cautions.

## D. Permanent life insurance

The course avoids a blanket claim that permanent insurance is always bad. It says permanent coverage may have legitimate uses for:

- Lifelong protection needs.
- Estate liquidity.
- Business planning.
- Special-needs planning.
- Other professionally reviewed needs.

Please review the required comparison language around:

- Guaranteed versus non-guaranteed values.
- Premium schedule and lapse risk.
- Cash-value access and policy loans.
- Surrender charges.
- Fees and expenses.
- Crediting or dividend assumptions.
- Modified endowment contract risk.
- Loan interest and the effect of an outstanding loan at lapse or death.
- Ownership, beneficiary, and estate consequences.

Flag any wording that could be read as an anti-product slogan rather than a decision framework.

## E. Life-insurance tax language

Please verify the limited statement that life-insurance death proceeds received by a beneficiary are generally excluded from federal gross income, while interest, transfer-for-value, policy ownership, estate inclusion, and other exceptions can change the result.

Coordinate replacement wording with the CPA and estate-attorney reviewers.

## F. Disability income insurance

Review the course’s questions:

- Percentage of income replaced.
- Monthly cap.
- Who paid the premium and how that affects benefit taxation.
- Elimination period.
- Benefit period.
- Own-occupation, modified own-occupation, any-occupation, or another contractual definition.
- Coverage of salary, bonus, commission, business income, and retirement contributions.
- Residual / partial disability.
- Inflation or future-purchase options.
- Employer-plan portability and coordination with individual coverage.

The course compares the **after-tax benefit** with both bare-bones and normal spending, then includes the loss of future contributions as a planning effect.

Please verify this framing and identify any statement that is too broad across employer and individual contracts.

## G. Reserve versus disability coverage

The course says the reserve can help cover the elimination period but is usually not designed to replace years of earnings.

Please review how to qualify this for:

- Short-term versus long-term disability.
- Sick leave and employer benefits.
- Social Security Disability Insurance.
- Business overhead coverage for self-employed owners.
- A household that deliberately self-insures part of the risk.

## H. Umbrella liability insurance

Review the product-neutral description that a personal umbrella policy may provide additional liability and legal-defense protection above or outside parts of underlying home, auto, or renters coverage, subject to terms, exclusions, and required underlying limits.

Confirm the course should say it generally does not insure damage to the policyholder’s own home or vehicle.

Please identify important gaps:

- Business activity.
- Rental property.
- Professional liability.
- Intentional acts.
- Defamation / personal injury coverage differences.
- Uninsured / underinsured motorist umbrella options where available.
- Public profile or social-media exposure.
- Household drivers and youthful drivers.

The course should teach the questions, not a universal “one million per X dollars of net worth” formula.

## I. Long-term care

Review the course’s neutral framework comparing:

- Family support.
- Portfolio self-insurance.
- Standalone long-term-care insurance.
- Hybrid products.
- Public benefits when eligible.
- A combination of sources.

Please review references to:

- Age and health.
- State and partnership programs.
- Care setting.
- Elimination period.
- Daily or monthly benefit.
- Benefit period or pool.
- Inflation protection.
- Premium-change risk.
- Spousal and family effects.
- Medicaid planning being legal and state-specific rather than a casual core strategy.

The course may tell a younger household to model the risk and set a future review date rather than implying immediate purchase.

## J. Coverage review and replacement

Review the annual and event-driven triggers:

- Marriage or divorce.
- Birth or adoption.
- Job or business change.
- Major debt.
- Significant net-worth change.
- Retirement.
- Estate-plan change.
- Health change.
- Expiring term.

The course says:

- Do not let needed coverage lapse accidentally.
- Do not cancel existing coverage until replacement is in force and the professional confirms the transition.
- Recalculate rather than automatically shrinking coverage because the investment portfolio rose.

Please add any replacement, contestability, suicide-period, underwriting, conversion, or state-regulation warning necessary for safe evergreen wording.

## K. Beneficiary and ownership records

Review the distinction among:

- Insured.
- Policy owner.
- Primary beneficiary.
- Contingent beneficiary.
- Trust or business ownership when applicable.

The app tracks the review status; the carrier holds the contract and beneficiary record.

The heir letter may tell the family that a policy exists and where the carrier information is stored. It should not contain login credentials.

Coordinate beneficiary language with the estate-attorney review.

## L. Demo-household arithmetic

The demo uses:

- Annual survivor gap: $55,000.
- First-pass protection period: 15 years.
- Debts and specific commitments: $180,000.
- Liquid assets and existing coverage deliberately available: $175,000.
- First-pass gap: `$55,000 × 15 + $180,000 − $175,000 = $830,000`.

Please review:

- Whether the arithmetic is presented clearly as a simple first pass.
- Which items should be discounted, inflated, or modeled by year in a professional calculation.
- Whether the example double-counts or omits survivor Social Security, childcare, debt payoff, or taxes.
- Whether the course should use the $830,000 only as a quote / review starting point.
