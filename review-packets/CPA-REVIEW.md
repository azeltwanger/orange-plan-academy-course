## A. Digital-asset basis and records

Please review these concepts and the exact wording used in Lesson 5.1:

- Basis as the taxpayer’s investment in the asset, including acquisition costs only when permitted.
- Capital gain or loss as amount realized minus the basis of the units disposed.
- Bitcoin acquisitions as separate tax lots with date, time, quantity, fair market value, basis, and holding period.
- The difference between a quick average-basis planning estimate and filing-grade lot records.
- Quantity reconciliation before basis reconstruction.
- Transfers between accounts or wallets owned by the same taxpayer generally carrying basis and holding period rather than creating a sale.
- Treatment of network, transfer, and transaction fees where the course currently tells the learner to preserve the record and obtain tax review instead of guessing.
- Keeping unknown basis visibly unresolved rather than entering a convenient value.
- Whether a zero-basis planning assumption should be described as conservative, incomplete, or potentially inappropriate.
- Records needed to support a reconstructed basis when an exchange history is missing.
- Gifting, inherited digital assets, mining, compensation, business receipts, forks, staking, and other acquisition types being outside the Core reconstruction lesson unless they apply.

## B. Taxable, traditional, and Roth accounts

Please verify:

- Taxable-account treatment of interest, dividends, and realized gains.
- Traditional contribution language: “may reduce current taxable income or be excluded from wages depending on account and eligibility.”
- Traditional withdrawals and Roth conversions generally creating ordinary income.
- Roth contributions being after-tax and qualified distributions potentially tax-free.
- Current lifetime RMD treatment for Roth IRA owners and designated Roth accounts.
- Beneficiary distribution rules being distinct from the original owner’s lifetime RMD treatment.
- Current RMD applicable ages by birth year and how briefly the Core lesson should describe them.
- Whether the lesson should distinguish plan-year law from long-range projections that may use later rules.

## C. Low-income windows and Roth conversions

Please review the course’s explanation of the period after work stops but before Social Security, pension income, or RMDs increase ordinary income.

Confirm the wording around:

- Conversion amount generally entering gross income in the year converted.
- Comparing current marginal cost with future withdrawals and RMDs.
- State tax.
- Medicare IRMAA and the timing of the income lookback.
- Taxation of Social Security.
- ACA premium-tax-credit effects before Medicare, if this belongs in Advanced rather than Core.
- Cash used to pay the conversion tax.
- Five-year rules and early-distribution rules where relevant.
- The difference between a Roth conversion and a traditional-account withdrawal used for spending.

The Core lesson should identify a window and one CPA question. It should not prescribe a conversion amount.

## D. Long-term capital gains and harvesting

Please verify the wording that:

- Long-term gains use a federal rate structure that interacts with taxable income.
- A gain can fall across more than one capital-gain rate band.
- State tax and net investment income tax can change the result.
- The same gain may cost more in a high-income working year than in a lower-income year.
- Gain harvesting and loss harvesting require current-year review rather than an evergreen transaction recipe.

Please identify any digital-asset wash-sale, constructive-sale, related-party, straddle, or other rule that the Core wording risks oversimplifying. Detailed execution can be moved to Advanced.

## E. Withdrawal sequencing and bracket-aware funding

Lesson 6.2 teaches that a strict taxable-first policy can leave low ordinary-income brackets unused while a traditional balance grows, and that a bracket-aware strategy may combine taxable sales with traditional income.

Please review:

- Whether “bracket-aware” is described accurately without promising lower lifetime tax.
- Ordinary-income versus capital-gain stacking.
- Interaction with Social Security taxation, RMDs, NIIT, state tax, deductions, and IRMAA.
- When a Roth conversion is preferable to a taxable traditional withdrawal and when neither should be forced.
- Early-retirement access rules, including the need to avoid presenting age 59½ as the only path or the Rule of 55 as universally available.
- Whether qualified dividends, tax-exempt interest, capital-loss carryforwards, charitable giving, and QCDs need a Core mention or belong in Advanced.

The desired Core conclusion is: use the app to identify the year and comparison, then execute the amount with current tax data and professional review.

## F. Sell, hold, and borrow against Bitcoin

Please review the tax statements implied by the sell-versus-borrow section:

- A genuine loan advance generally not being gross income merely because cash was received.
- A collateral sale or liquidation potentially creating a taxable disposition.
- Interest deductibility depending on use of proceeds and other limitations.
- Refinancing, accrued interest, capitalized interest, and repayment not being interchangeable for tax purposes.
- Loan balance at death, estate administration, collateral sale, and basis-at-death issues requiring attorney / CPA coordination.
- A borrowing strategy deferring a sale rather than eliminating economic cost or future tax.
- The app comparison being a projection rather than a tax opinion.

Flag any statement that could be read as “borrow forever and never pay tax.” That is not the intended teaching.

## G. Social Security, insurance, and estate-adjacent tax references

- Taxable versus tax-free retirement income labels in the app.
