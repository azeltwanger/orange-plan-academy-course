## A. Fiduciary roles and timing

Please review the distinction among:

- Executor / personal representative after death.
- Financial agent under a durable power of attorney during life and incapacity.
- Healthcare agent or surrogate.
- Trustee.
- Guardian or conservator when relevant.
- Technical Bitcoin helper or custody provider with no automatic legal authority.

Confirm the wording that:

- A will generally nominates an executor, while applicable court or legal process establishes authority over probate property.
- A power of attorney generally terminates at death.
- One person may serve in more than one role when the legal and family design supports it.
- A key holder is not automatically legally authorized to act.
- A legally appointed fiduciary does not automatically possess the technical means to move Bitcoin.

Please flag state terminology that should be avoided in evergreen video or paired with “sometimes called…” language.

## B. Core document categories

Review the course’s national framing around:

- Will.
- Durable financial power of attorney.
- Healthcare directive / medical power documents.
- Beneficiary, payable-on-death, and transfer-on-death designations.
- Revocable trust when it solves a real administration, incapacity, privacy, probate, or family need.
- Guardianship nominations where dependent children make that relevant.

The course should not imply that every Bitcoin holder needs a trust or that one standard form works in every state.

## C. Provider beneficiary records versus the will

Please verify the current language:

- Retirement plans, IRAs, insurance policies, and accounts with valid beneficiary or death-transfer designations may pass under the provider record rather than the will’s probate instructions.
- Plan terms, state law, spousal consent, community-property rights, and validity matter.
- “The form always beats the will” is too absolute.
- Orange Plan is the checklist; the administrator, insurer, custodian, or bank holds the operative account record.

Identify the minimum evergreen wording that is accurate without turning the lesson into a state-law lecture.

## D. Digital-asset authority

Review what the course should ask an attorney to address in the will, power of attorney, trust, and related documents:

- Authority to access, manage, transfer, or obtain information about digital assets.
- Fiduciary access to exchange, broker, retirement-custody, collaborative-custody, and online-provider records.
- Interaction with federal and state digital-asset / electronic-communications laws.
- Provider terms of service and required proof of authority.
- Authority to hire technical assistance.
- Privacy and disclosure limits.
- Avoiding seed phrases, private keys, passphrases, PINs, or passwords in public or broadly distributed legal documents.

The desired course rule is: **the legal plan identifies authority and process; it does not publish the secret.**

## E. Executor and heir role design

The course currently rejects a universal rule that an heir and executor must always be different people.

Please review the narrower design principle:

> When one person’s combined legal role and custody components would give them every element needed to move funds before the intended process, the attorney and custody professional should reconsider the roles or component distribution.

Confirm how to discuss:

- Conflicts of interest.
- Co-fiduciaries.
- Independent trustees or professional fiduciaries.
- Bonds, waivers, compensation, and succession.
- Spouse as executor or agent.
- Technical helper versus fiduciary.

Keep implementation state-specific.

## F. Multisig and custody coordination

The course correctly describes a **2-of-3** wallet as three keys where any two can sign. It also distinguishes key material from a non-secret descriptor or wallet-configuration record.

Please review only the legal coordination, not the technical design:

- Who owns the Bitcoin or account.
- Who is authorized to act during incapacity and after death.
- Whether a key holder is an agent, custodian, escrow holder, co-owner, trustee, fiduciary, or merely a technical component holder.
- How the legal documents and provider agreement identify the intended parties.
- What documentation the family needs to prove authority.
- How a provider’s failure or changed procedure affects the estate plan.
- Whether the plan creates unintended gifts, ownership, agency, or control issues.

The course should send complex implementation to an attorney and custody professional rather than prescribe a key distribution.

## G. Heir letter and executor packet

Review the distinction:

- The **heir letter** is a plain-language starting guide.
- The **executor packet** contains process and professional information.
- Neither replaces the will, trust, power of attorney, beneficiary record, or provider claim process.
- Neither contains Bitcoin secrets.

Please review recommended contents:

- Call order.
- Attorney, CPA, executor, technical helper, insurer, and provider contacts.
- General asset and account categories.
- Locations of legal and process documents.
- Stop signs against unsolicited help or premature transfers.
- Statement that legal and tax authority should be clear before moving Bitcoin.
- Personal explanation of what the plan is intended to do.

Flag any content that could contradict a legal document or create an unintended directive.

## H. Dead-man switch / cloud delivery

The course treats a dead-man switch as an **additional notification path**, not the only estate plan.

Please review the language that it:

- Can send a no-secrets notice or letter after a missed check-in and warning process.
- Does not move Bitcoin.
- Does not create fiduciary authority.
- Does not replace the legal documents or custody process.
- Can fail because of email changes, spam filtering, service outage, account compromise, or a false trigger.
- Requires independent storage and family awareness.

Please identify any consent, privacy, record-retention, electronic-delivery, or state-law issue that needs a brief qualification.

## I. Incapacity and death are different events

Confirm the course should separately address:

- Temporary unavailability.
- Long-term incapacity.
- Death.
- Simultaneous death or unavailable spouse.
- Minor or financially immature beneficiaries.
- A provider or technical helper becoming unavailable.

The technical process may be similar, but the legal authority and people acting can differ.
