---

Your allocation is the share of your money in Bitcoin. That percentage changes depending on what you divide by, so the same household can look aggressive or conservative without owning a single different coin.

### What you divide by

Take the couple. They hold 1.75 Bitcoin at $100,000/coin, or $175,000. Their non-Bitcoin: $90,000 index funds, $30,000 cash, $450,000 home equity.

- **Investable only:** $175k ÷ $295k = about **60%**.
- **Total net worth:** $175k ÷ $745k = about **24%**.

Both are legitimate. Investable shows how the growth engine is running. Net worth shows how exposed your balance sheet is.

### What sets a responsible allocation

What actually sets it is three things:

1. **Education.** How much you know about what Bitcoin is and why it works.
2. **Conviction.** What that knowledge turns into once you've studied enough to defend the position.
3. **Comfort with volatility.** What you'd actually do in a 70% drawdown, not what you'd like to think you'd do.

Together they set the size of the position you can survive. That's different from the position you might want.

Austin's own: ~37% average annual Bitcoin returns over the last five years, and he's watched his net worth drop 75% in a single drawdown and held. Because he understood what he owned.

### The four tiers

**Foundation: 1 to 5%**

New and curious. Bitcoin is a small hedge.

- **Custody.** Exchange or ETF.
- **The job.** Learning why Bitcoin exists. Read the whitepaper. Understand what makes Bitcoin different.

**Integration: 10 to 20%**

You believe Bitcoin is a real part of the plan.

- **Custody.** Start learning self-custody. Buy a hardware wallet, send a test transaction, move a small amount off exchange first.
- **The job.** Get comfortable with the mechanics.
- **Common mistake.** Integration-level allocation with Foundation-level custody. Coins on an exchange while claiming to be 15% in.

**Optimization: 25 to 50%**

You believe the thesis and have held through volatility. Bitcoin is a main growth driver.

- **Custody.** Confident self-custody. Coins on hardware wallets. Recovery test done (Module 7). Passphrase understood — the optional extra word layered on top of your seed words — even if not used yet.
- **The job.** Operational discipline. Beneficiaries, heir letter, dead man's switch (Module 8).
- **The stakes.** One custody mistake can meaningfully damage the plan.

**Sovereign: 50%+**

Bitcoin as future money. Holding anything else feels like the risk.

- **Custody.** Optimized. Could be single-sig, multisig (more than one key required to spend), collaborative, or a mix.
- **The job.** The risks are operational, legal, and political now. "Should I buy Bitcoin?" is done. "How do I hold and pass it on?" is the question.

### Pair allocation with custody

Two numbers describe your Bitcoin situation:

- Your **allocation level** (%)
- Your **custody level** (exchange, ETF, hardware wallet, multisig)

When both are at the same tier, the position is supported. When allocation is a tier ahead of custody, that gap is homework. Custody has to catch up, or the position is bigger than the setup can safely hold.

### Placing the couple

- **Allocation:** 60% investable / 24% net worth. Optimization-to-Sovereign territory.
- **Custody:** $150k hardware + $25k exchange. Hardware-wallet level. Appropriate for Optimization.
- **Conviction:** high. Bitcoin is a main growth driver.

Tier: Sovereign, working on it. Homework: move the $25k off the exchange to close the gap.

### No position should surprise you

If a 70% drop in your largest position would meaningfully break your plan, the position is too big for your comfort, regardless of what the percentage says. Match the size to what you can actually hold through.

### Homework

Place yourself honestly across the three drivers:

- **Education.** What have you actually studied?
- **Conviction.** Could you defend the position in an argument with a well-informed skeptic?
- **Custody.** What's your setup today, not what you're planning?

The percentage is what falls out of those three. Not what you pick first.

- **Stress test.** Run once, when you set your allocation.
- **Price context.** Run every time before a Bitcoin-heavy move.

### Check 1: the stress test

The stress test finds out whether the allocation you picked is one you could actually live through.

Running it now moves the decision to a day when you're calm.

#### A 70 to 80% drawdown is the normal case

A drawdown is the fall from a price peak down to the bottom that follows it, measured as a percentage of that peak.

For Bitcoin, 70 to 80% is normal, not a worst case:

- **2018:** dropped **84%**, from ~$20k to $3k.
- **2022:** dropped **77%**, from $69k to just under $16k.

You're not stress-testing a worst case. You're stress-testing the normal case.

#### The math

Your net-worth hit = the drawdown × your Bitcoin allocation.

| Allocation | 75% drawdown means |
|---|---|
| 10% | 7.5% net-worth hit |
| 25% | 19% hit |
| 50% | 37.5% hit |
| 75% | 56% hit |

You don't control the drawdown. You do control the allocation.

#### The couple's stress test

$175,000 in Bitcoin at 75% down leaves $43,750. Their $295,000 investable becomes about $164,000. $131,250 came off the screen and not one bill got smaller. The mortgage is still $2,300/mo. The car is still $360. Groceries still $1,200.

That's the pressure that makes people sell at the bottom, which is the single thing that ruins long-term growth. The stress test asks: could you sit there and not sell?

If the answer is no, you don't stop investing in Bitcoin. You adjust the allocation until you could sleep through the drawdown. You can also run this live in the app against the **Bear Market** preset (Bitcoin -60%, stocks -40%, 3-year recovery).

### Check 2: the price context check

This one runs before any Bitcoin-heavy move. Its job is to name the emotion in the room.

#### Two lookbacks

- **Recent (3, 6, 9, 12 months):** what you're feeling. Bitcoin up 40% in three months, you're feeling FOMO. Down 40%, you're feeling fear.
- **Long (2 to 5 years):** what's actually true. The direction of the trend, not the mood of last week.

Run both. Recent tells you which emotion you're carrying into the decision. Long tells you whether it's aligned with reality or reacting against it.

#### What the check does and doesn't do

It doesn't decide the move. It names the emotion so you can act on the plan instead of the mood.

Before a big move (buying a large position, selling to lock in gains, taking out a Bitcoin-backed loan), if the recent 3-6 month price move is dramatic, you're probably reacting to it. Wait a beat. Is this the plan making the decision, or the price?

Every dollar you own sits in one of three buckets. The bucket tells you what the money is for, how it should behave, and where it should sit.

### The three buckets

| Bucket | Timeframe | Volatility | What sits here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reserve** | 0-3 years | Low | High-yield savings, T-bills, short Treasuries, money market. Nothing that moves. |
| **Bridge** | 4-10 years | Moderate | Taxable brokerage, HSA, 529, I-Bond ladders. Balanced mix of stocks and bonds. |
| **Legacy** | 10+ years | High | Bitcoin (in appropriate custody), retirement accounts (401k, IRA, Roth), business equity you hold long-term. |

**Reserve** protects your ability to hold Bitcoin without being forced to sell in a bad month.

**Bridge** is accessible, flexible capital for pre-retirement needs. Also the funding lane for known dated costs 4 to 10 years out.

**Legacy** is your highest-growth assets, held long enough to ride out swings. Volatility is a feature at this timeframe, not a bug.

### Matching each dollar to a bucket

Every account you own goes into one of the three. Here's how the couple lands:

| Account | Balance | Bucket | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash | $30,000 | Reserve | Liquid safety net |
| HSA (invested) | $15,000 | Bridge | Accessible before 65, spendable in retirement |
| Taxable brokerage | $50,000 | Bridge | Planned spending 5-8 years out |
| 529 | $25,000 | Bridge | Dated tuition, 8 years out |
| Bitcoin (hardware) | $175,000 | Legacy | Long-term core |
| 401(k) + IRA | $90,000 | Legacy | 15+ years to retirement |

Every dollar has a job.

### The refill rule

Buckets aren't static. Bridge and Reserve get drained. Legacy gets built. The rule matters in retirement:

**Refill from the bucket that's up. Never from the bucket that's down.**

If stocks are up 15% and Bitcoin is down 60%, refill Bridge (or the paycheck coming out of it) by trimming stocks. Not by selling Bitcoin. That's how you avoid selling Bitcoin at a bad time to fund life.

If everything is down in a coordinated drawdown, draw from the Reserve. That's what it exists for. The Reserve is the shock absorber that keeps the refill rule alive.

### Set a target percentage for each bucket

Each bucket has both a current balance and a target: the percentage of your net worth it should be at.

- **Working, Sovereign-tier household:** 5% Reserve / 15% Bridge / 80% Legacy.
- **Approaching retirement:** 10% Reserve (bigger buffer for sequence risk) / 30% Bridge / 60% Legacy.

The target is what the projection uses. Change it and the plan re-solves. Route new dollars and refill decisions toward the target.

### Common mistakes

- **Bitcoin in Bridge.** Bitcoin belongs in Legacy. If you're planning to spend it inside 10 years, either the bucket or the plan is wrong.
- **Cash in Legacy.** Cash has no place in a 10+ year bucket. A 401(k) in a stable-value fund is misclassified: Bridge risk in a Legacy slot.
- **Reserve too small in retirement.** Once you're drawing income, the Reserve does more work. Its job in retirement is sequence-risk protection, not just emergencies.

---

You have a surplus. You have buckets. This lesson covers the order the next dollar should flow, and why.

Every rung above the next has to be full (or maxed) before you move on. Money flows down the ladder, not sideways.

### The standard waterfall

| # | Rung | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Employer match** (401k or similar) | 50 to 100% instant return. Nothing else you do with the money gets close. |
| 2 | **High-interest debt** | Above ~7%. A guaranteed 20% return on paying down a 20% credit card is unbeatable. |
| 3 | **HSA** (if eligible) | Triple-tax-advantaged. Only tax shelter that gets all three. |
| 4 | **Roth IRA or Roth 401(k)** | Tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawal, no RMDs — no required minimum distributions, the withdrawals the government forces out of traditional accounts once you reach a certain age. Best if you expect a higher retirement bracket. |
| 5 | **Traditional 401(k) or IRA** (up to the limit) | Tax deduction now, taxed on withdrawal. Best if you expect a lower retirement bracket. |
| 6 | **Taxable brokerage or Bitcoin** | Once tax-advantaged buckets are maxed, the rest goes here. |

Free money first. Guaranteed high returns second. Tax shelters in order of quality after that.

### Rung notes

**Rung 1 (match).** If the match is 50% up to 6% of pay, contribute 6%. Not 5%. Not 3%. Six.

**Rung 2 (high-interest debt).** ~7% is a rough threshold. Any 20%+ APR is priority 1, before anything else.

**Rung 3 (HSA).** Only available with a High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP). Best treated as a Legacy account: invest it, save it as a Roth-like medical vehicle.

**Rung 4 (Roth).** Especially strong for Bitcoin-heavy plans, because Bitcoin appreciation often puts you in a higher retirement bracket than you're in now.

**Rung 5 (traditional).** Continuing past the match is fine. Match rung caps at the match; traditional rung caps at the annual limit.

**Rung 6 (Bitcoin or taxable).** Split reflects your allocation tier from earlier.

### Special cases

- **No employer match.** Skip rung 1.
- **Not HSA-eligible.** Skip rung 3.
- **Income too high for Roth IRA direct contribution.** Look up the Backdoor Roth: contribute non-deductible to a Traditional IRA, then convert. Requires you to not have other pre-tax IRA balances (pro-rata rule).
- **No high-interest debt.** Skip rung 2.

|---|---|---|
| 401(k) | $1,000/mo | Captures full 50%-up-to-6% match ($12,000/yr) |
| High-interest debt | — | None |
| HSA | $300/mo | Building the medical Legacy fund |
| Roth IRA | $583/mo | Maxing the year's IRA limit ($7,000 when they ran it) |
| Traditional | — | Skip for now |
| Bitcoin + taxable | ~$2,117/mo | Split between Bridge and Legacy |

Every dollar has a route. Nothing is left "to figure out."

### The routing card

Post the waterfall where you make money decisions. When a bonus or windfall arrives, walk down the ladder:

- Am I capturing the full match?
- Is high-interest debt gone?
- HSA maxed?
- Roth maxed?
- Traditional 401(k) or IRA rung, depending on your bracket strategy.
- Taxable or Bitcoin.

One decision, made once, routes every future dollar correctly.

## 4.5 Asset location: the right account for each holding
*`TEACH` · 686 words · ~5 min*

**By the end of this lesson, you can:**

- Understand the three tax wrappers and their treatment
- Place each holding in the wrapper that minimizes lifetime tax drag
- See how asset location affects the retirement withdrawal order

---

You have three types of tax wrappers. The same $1 in Bitcoin sits very differently in each. Asset location is choosing which wrapper each holding lives in, to minimize your lifetime tax drag.

### The three wrappers

| Wrapper | Tax in | Growth | Tax out | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Taxable brokerage** | Already-taxed dollars | Taxed annually on dividends and gains | Taxed on gains only (long-term rates if held >1yr) | Most flexible. No age limits, no penalties. |
| **Traditional (401k, IRA)** | Pre-tax (deductible) | Tax-deferred | Ordinary income | RMDs start at 73. 10% penalty before 59½. |
| **Roth (IRA, 401k)** | After-tax (post-tax) | Tax-free forever | Tax-free forever | No RMDs on Roth IRA. |

### The principle

**Put your highest-growth assets in your best tax shelter.**

Growth compounds. A high-growth asset in a taxable account pays tax on every dividend, rebalance, and realized gain, and that drag compounds too. The same asset in a Roth grows tax-free for decades.

### Where each holding belongs

**Bitcoin.** Depends. Long-term capital gains on Bitcoin are 15 to 20% federal plus state. Inside a Roth (via a Bitcoin ETF), those gains are shielded and never taxed. Best of both worlds.

The catch: Bitcoin in a Roth means you can't easily rebalance or spend it before 59½ (10% penalty). Most self-custody Bitcoin sits outside any wrapper, which makes it taxable by default.

Common pattern: some Bitcoin in retirement wrappers (ETF form) for tax shielding, the majority in self-custody (taxable) for sovereignty.

**Stocks and index funds.**

- **Broad index funds.** Roth or Traditional. High long-term growth benefits most from shielding.
- **Individual stocks.** Same. If you're actively trading, taxable is fine because you're realizing gains anyway.

**Bonds.** Traditional 401(k)/IRA (bonds throw off ordinary-income yield, so sheltering it helps), OR taxable in muni-bond form (tax-free interest for higher earners). Not usually Roth. Bonds' low growth doesn't justify tax-free-forever shielding.

**Cash / money market.** Taxable. Roth's shelter is wasted on cash's tiny growth.

**Real estate (rental).** Taxable. Depreciation and 1031 exchanges are the tax tools here, not location.

**High-yield dividend stocks.** Traditional or Roth. Dividends are taxed annually in taxable accounts.

### The couple's location

| Holding | Account | Bucket |
|---|---|---|
| $30k cash | Taxable | Reserve |
| $15k HSA (index funds) | HSA | Bridge |
| $50k taxable brokerage (index funds) | Taxable | Bridge |
| $25k 529 (index funds) | 529 | Bridge |
| $175k Bitcoin (hardware) | Self-custody, taxable | Legacy |
| $70k 401(k) (index funds) | Traditional 401k | Legacy |
| $20k Roth IRA (index funds) | Roth | Legacy |

Decent placement. If they wanted to optimize: consider a small Bitcoin ETF position inside the Roth for tax-free growth, while keeping the majority in self-custody.

### Interaction with retirement withdrawals

Where each asset sits affects the withdrawal order in retirement (Module 6):

- **Roth is generally drawn last.** Tax-free growth is the most valuable.
- **Traditional gets drawn to fill low tax brackets first.**
- **Taxable gets drawn in the middle,** for cost-basis efficiency.

If all your Bitcoin is in taxable, that dictates when it gets sold and at what rate. If some sits in Roth, you have a tax-free spendable Bitcoin pool that opens different retirement paycheck options.

### Homework

For every account, ask: is the asset in it appropriate for the wrapper?

- Cash in a Roth? Move to taxable.
- Bonds in a Roth? Move to Traditional.
- Bitcoin only in taxable, with room in Roth? Consider a small ETF position for tax-free growth.
- High-growth stocks in taxable and low-growth bonds in Roth? Swap them.

Location isn't a one-time decision. Every year, and any time you rebalance, ask again.

### Step 1: Reality check on the Dashboard

### Step 2: Read the three timeframes and their sufficiency badges

Go to **Strategy → Allocation → Funding your timeframes**.

This section answers the question "do I have enough in each bucket?" You don't compute it. The app renders it.

Three rows:

| Row | Purpose | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| **Reserve** | Cash you can spend anytime. | Now |
| **Bridge** | Early-retirement years before 59½. | Short to medium |
| **Legacy** | Long-term growth, unlocked at 59½. | Long |

Each row shows a badge: **Funded** or **Behind**.

Above the rows, a strip reads either **Assigned accounts · projected** or **Projected values**, depending on whether accounts are already sorted. If it says "Projected values," you haven't done Step 3 yet, and the sufficiency numbers are a preview.

#### How to read the badges

| Pattern | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| All three **Funded** | Your buckets carry enough for their timeframe. | Nothing urgent. Recheck at the annual review. |
| **Reserve = Behind** | Your safety net is under-funded first. | Reserve is rung 1 of the waterfall. Fix it before anything else. Route surplus to cash. |
| **Bridge = Behind**, Reserve = Funded | Your early-retirement years (before 59½) don't add up. | Route surplus into taxable brokerage, HSA, or 529 accounts. |
| **Legacy = Behind**, Reserve and Bridge = Funded | Long-term compounding is short. | Route contributions to retirement wrappers (401(k), IRA), and any Bitcoin tier that fits your conviction. |
| **Reserve = Behind and Legacy = Funded** | Common misfit. A volatile long-term asset is doing bucket-1 work. Common with Bitcoin-heavy stacks. | Reassign accounts in Step 3, or draw cash out of Legacy to backfill Reserve. |

### Step 3: Sort your accounts into buckets

**Funding your timeframes → Assign accounts** opens a dialog titled **Sort accounts into buckets**.

The dialog instructions read *"Drag each account into the bucket that matches when you'll use it."*

Walk your list:

- Cash and savings → **Reserve**
- Taxable brokerage earmarked for a dated cost (kids' tuition, home purchase) → **Bridge**
- HSA → **Bridge** (or **Legacy** if you're using it as a Roth-like retirement account)
- 529 → **Bridge**
- Retirement accounts (401(k), IRA, Roth) → **Legacy**
- Bitcoin → **Legacy** (unless a specific chunk is reserved for a shorter timeframe)

The footer counter reads *"N accounts still unsorted"* until it flips to **"All accounts sorted."** Any unsorted account is money with no timeline job.

Close with **Done**. Watch the Funded / Behind badges update.

### Step 4: Save your target mix

**Allocation → Your target mix → Edit targets** (or **Set target mix** if none saved yet).

This is a real, saved setting. The editor heading reads **Set your target mix**, with the note *"Drag or type. Percentages must total 100% to save."*

If nothing is saved yet, four presets appear:

- **Bitcoin-forward**
- **Balanced**
- **Conservative**
- **Build your own**

Pick the range that matches the tier you defended in Lesson 1. The line at the bottom should read **✓ 100% allocated**. If it reads *"N% allocated · adjust to 100%"*, the editor won't let you save.

Set your **drift band**: *"Flag drift beyond ± __%"*. This is how far your live mix can wander from your target before the app flags a rebalance decision. A reasonable default is ±5 percentage points, but pick a number you'd actually act on.

Commit with **Save target**.

Two things to know:

- The app records your target. It doesn't prescribe one. The number is yours to defend.
- You don't assign holdings here. The editor's own note reads: *"You don't assign accounts here. Orange Plan rolls up what they hold automatically."*

### Step 5: How the target actually gets used

Stay on **Your target mix**.

When new savings don't name a specific holding, the engine routes them by this target. Change the target, and the plan re-solves. The number you just saved is what runs the waterfall from tomorrow forward.

### Step 6: Configure the 401(k) contribution row

**Cash Flow → Routing · waterfall order → Step 3 Contributions** → click the **401(k)** row.

The collapsed row shows a mode pill: **fixed**, **fill to match**, **max**, or **leftover**.

Open the row and walk it top to bottom.

**6.1: Contribution mode** — three options:

- **Custom $/mo.** A fixed dollar amount.
- **Fill to match.** Captures the full employer match, nothing more.
- **Max.** Up to the IRS annual limit. Verify the limit for the year.

Pick **Fill to match**. The row prints *"targets $X/yr"*. The app has computed what "fill to match" actually is based on your salary and match schedule.

Beside the mode control: **annual limit $X**, which flips to *"· at annual limit"* if a higher setting would exceed the cap.

**6.2: Employer match** — two fields: **Match rate __%** and **up to __% of pay**. Enter both. The row computes *"captures $X/mo"*, the actual dollar value of the employer match you're capturing.

If Fill to match shows greyed out with *"Fill to match is off"*, the row is telling you which prerequisite is missing:

- *"Needs a salary income source on this owner to size the match."* Fix on the income screen.
- *"Needs employer match details below."* Fill in the two match fields above.

**6.3: Tax treatment** — three options: **Traditional**, **Split**, or **Roth**. Two questions to answer, in this order: can this account hold what I want to own, and which wrapper treats it best (per the asset location lesson)?

**6.4: How it invests** — three modes:

- **Current mix.** Matches what the account already holds.
- **Set mix.** A fixed target inside this account.
- **Choose holdings.** Click **+ Add holding** to add a security. A newly-added holding tags itself *"· new holding"* so you can spot it.

To add an account type that isn't listed, click **+ Add account type** at the bottom.

### Step 7: Route with AI

**Cash Flow → Routing · waterfall order → Route with AI** (top right of the section header).

This AI review reads three things: your cash flow, your contribution plan, and your debts.

Click it. Its message reads **"Route my next dollar."** It asks one clarifying question:

*"Would you like to route the amount currently available in your plan, or test a different monthly or one-time amount?"*

Answer honestly, then wait until the review lands. Read at least one thing it surfaced out loud and say whether you agree.

**When to run it:** right here, after the contribution row is configured. It's a sanity check on where the next dollar goes across reserve, debt, and contributions.

> ⚠ It reviews and explains. It does not decide.

### Step 8: Compare the change in Scenarios

**Scenarios → Custom scenario** (header button) opens a **Create scenario** dialog.

Save the current setup as a scenario so you can compare against it. Saved scenarios live under **Your scenarios**: *"Saved what-ifs you can reopen and compare."*

Then run the built-in stress test: **What if... → Bear Market**. That preset drops Bitcoin 60% and stocks 40%, then recovers over 3 years.

Ask yourself the stress-test question: could you sit through that scenario and do nothing? If not, the allocation is too high. Adjust the target in Step 4 and re-run.

### Step 9: Review portfolio (AI)

**Strategy → Allocation → Review portfolio** (page header).

This AI review reads your portfolio, the allocation target you just saved, and the funding timeframes.

Click it. It asks the question the whole module has been building to:

*"What would force you to sell investments during a deep Bitcoin drawdown?"*

Answer it out loud. If your honest answer is "a lifestyle expense I forgot to fund," go fix Reserve or Bridge before you touch the mix.

> ⚠ This review explores and explains. It won't tell you what your Bitcoin percentage should be.

### Step 10: Confirm the record

Back to **Allocation → Your target mix**.

Two facts should be true and visible:

- The target and its drift band are saved. This is your number on the record.
- The waterfall order is Reserve → Extra debt → Contributions, and it runs monthly.

### What good looks like

- Your holdings match reality. The donut doesn't lie.
- All accounts sorted. Dialog footer reads "All accounts sorted."
- Bucket badges tell the true story. Reserve, Bridge, and Legacy all **Funded** is the finish line. Any **Behind** is your next action.
- Bucket-job match: nothing with Legacy-level volatility parked in Bridge money. The classic misfit is Reserve Behind while Legacy is Funded.
- Target mix at 100%, in a range you can defend, saved with a drift band you'd actually act on.
- **captures $X/mo** shows the full match captured. Anything less is free money on the table, and it's visible on the screen.
- Bear Market versus the new target: the plan survives, and your honest answer to the sit-still question is yes. If not, the allocation is too high.

### Handing it off

The waterfall runs monthly from here. Between now and the annual review, your job is to fund it. The app does the routing.
